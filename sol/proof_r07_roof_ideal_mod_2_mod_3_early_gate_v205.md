## 1. The whole roof direction ideal dies

Let

\[
 F=F(x,y)\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_0,
 \qquad
 H_0=\ker(F\to\Delta_0),
\tag{1.1}
\]

and put

\[
 J_0=\ker\bigl(
 \mathbf F_3[F]\longrightarrow\mathbf F_3[\Delta_0]
 \bigr).
\tag{1.2}
\]

V156 and v189 identify the exact exponent lattice as

\[
 \operatorname{exp}_{x,y}(H_0)=18\mathbf Z^2.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For \(\ell=2,3\), let

\[
 \alpha_\ell:F\twoheadrightarrow
 D_\ell\cong(C_\ell)^2
\tag{1.4}
\]

be v202's exact joint abelian occurrence map.

### Theorem 1.1 (ROOF IDEAL VANISHES IN BOTH SCREENS)

For \(\ell=2,3\),

\[
 \boxed{(\alpha_\ell)_*(J_0)=0,}
\tag{1.5}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{\bar{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}(J_0)=0.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

#### Proof

The group-algebra kernel \(J_0\) is the two-sided ideal generated by
\(h-1\), \(h\in H_0\).  Equation (1.3) and \(\ell\mid18\) give
\(\alpha_\ell(h)=1\).  Thus every generator and every two-sided translate
maps to zero, proving (1.5).  The projected endpoint map factors through
\((\alpha_\ell)_*\) by v203, which gives (1.6). \(\square\)

This is stronger than v204 Theorem 2.1 because
\(J_1\subseteq J_0\).  It covers all finite word-pair representatives of
all possible first-successor multiplier values above the same roof, not only
representative changes inside one named \(\mu_1\)-fibre.

## 2. The endpoint projection is the fixed residual

For each printed block \(B\in\{H1,H2,P\}\), retain v198's fixed endpoint

\[
 \epsilon_B=D_{1,B}e_B
\tag{2.1}
\]

and write

\[
 \bar\epsilon_\ell=
 \tau_{*,\ell}(\epsilon_{H1},\epsilon_{H2},\epsilon_P)
 \in E_\ell.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a finite word-pair multiplier \(M\in J_0\), v198 gives

\[
 \eta(M)=\epsilon-\mathcal E_d(M).
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Corollary 2.1 (ALL ROOF-RELATIVE MULTIPLIERS HAVE ONE SCREEN VALUE)

For \(\ell=2,3\) and every finite-support \(M\in J_0\),

\[
 \boxed{\bar\eta_\ell(M)=\bar\epsilon_\ell.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Project (2.3) and use Theorem 1.1. \(\square\)

Thus the screens do not require \(\mu_1\), a source representative of
\(\mu_1\), or a successor presentation.  They require only the literal
residual endpoint terms and the already frozen occurrence maps.

### Corollary 2.2 (EARLY UNIVERSAL-PAIR OBSTRUCTION)

If

\[
 \boxed{
 \bar\epsilon_2\ne0
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \bar\epsilon_3\ne0,}
\tag{2.5}
\]

then no finite-support \(M\in J_0\) and finite boundary chain \(q\) satisfy
v191's universal equality

\[
 \widetilde e-M\widetilde d=\widetilde D_2q.
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

Any equality (2.6) has zero endpoint by
\(\widetilde D_1\widetilde D_2=0\).  Its mod-\(\ell\) endpoint would
therefore be zero.  Corollary 2.1 says it equals the nonzero value in (2.5),
a contradiction. \(\square\)

The scope of Corollary 2.2 is exact but limited.  It excludes every finite
word-pair universal multiplier in the full roof ideal used by v191.  V191 is
a positive promotion theorem, not a proved classification of all possible
profinite GT lifts.  Therefore (2.5) must not be relabelled as a fake or
Ihara certificate without an additional completeness theorem connecting all
lifts to (2.6).

If both projections vanish, no positive conclusion follows.  The exact PB
endpoint may still be nonzero, and a finite-support universal pair may or may
not exist.

## 3. Earlier production order

The post-task193/task198 order is sharpened as follows.

1. Reconstruct the three literal residual endpoints \(\epsilon_B\) from the
   genuine second-rung rows, with every occurrence sign, prefix, inverse
   slot, and printed-order tag.
2. In the same term stream, reduce PB abelian exponent vectors modulo two
   and modulo three and collect \(\bar\epsilon_2,\bar\epsilon_3\).
3. On a nonzero result, emit the first tagged nonzero coefficient and stop
   every finite v191 multiplier lane by Corollary 2.2.
4. Only if both screens vanish, run v188's first-successor kernel closure,
   pointed membership, and ancestry compiler.
5. For the compiled \(M_0\), run v198's exact PB endpoint.  The two abelian
   projections need not be recomputed: Corollary 2.1 already fixes them.

This moves a complete necessary gate ahead of the large successor linear
algebra.  It adds no search radius, group enumeration, or GHA-heavy orbit.

vectors before reduction, the two collected tagged arrays, and destructive
