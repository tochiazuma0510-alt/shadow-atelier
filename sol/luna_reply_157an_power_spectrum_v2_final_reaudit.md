## F4: factor descent is strong, but compact-roof descent is not closed

The new factor check is a genuine finite relation test.  It enumerates the G9
and PSL factors from the independently rebuilt generators, requiring orders
2916 and 504 (`search/check_d972_power_spectrum_v2.py:162-200`).  For each of
all 972 rows it checks both target generator images are in the corresponding
factor and replays every directed Cayley edge; disagreement at any previously
reached vertex fails (`:203-230`).  This proves that each assignment descends
to an endomorphism of each factor, including relation consistency and image
membership.

However, the degree-36 roof used by the table is
`C=<direct_sum(x9,x4),direct_sum(y9,y4)>` (`search/check_d972_power_spectrum_v2.py:147-159`),
and the checker never enumerates `C`, checks `|C|=2916*504=1469664`, or checks
that each target pair `(ax,ay)` lies in `C`.  It only trusts the receipt's
scalar `roof_order`/`pure_quotient_order` fields (`:362-368`).  The producer's
core does perform the needed compact-order gate
(`search/d972_dovetail_core_v2.g:115-120`), but that is not an independent
checker gate.

Factor endomorphisms do not, by themselves, preserve an arbitrary proper
subdirect subgroup of a direct product: compatible quotient data can be lost,
and a pair whose two components lie in the factors can still lie outside `C`.
Thus the factor Cayley checks plus the 972^2 generator-image comparison
(`search/check_d972_power_spectrum_v2.py:320-348,473-476`) prove associativity
only after the unverified premise that `C` is the full direct product (or that
all target pairs preserve the actual compact subgroup).  Add an independent
compact-Cayley/order gate or an equivalent authenticated direct-product proof
before calling F4 closed.
