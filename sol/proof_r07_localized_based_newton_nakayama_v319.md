# R07 localized based Newton--Nakayama completion (v319)

Author: Sol / 2026-08-29

Status: paper theorem synthesizing v117, v124, v252, v263 and v266.  It gives
an alternative to the narrow cyclic-return/NLSAT route.  If the complete
doubly localized residual module has a finite free cover and the common-word
Jacobian is onto its leading quotient, based Nakayama constructs one exact
free-cover lift over the whole completion.  Localization stability then
makes every nonlinear remainder automatically correctable by a Newton--Hensel
recursion.  The required finite generation, leading all-generator solve and
actual common-word typing have not been established for R07.  No compatible
lift, fake certificate or Ihara witness is declared.

## 1. Complete based linear data

Let \(\Lambda\) be a complete separated ring for the powers of a two-sided
ideal \(J\), and suppose

\[
 \bigcap_{r\geq0}J^r=0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(A\) be a complete separated left \(\Lambda\)-module of legal
word-bearing corrections.  Let \(L\) be a complete separated left
\(\Lambda\)-module of localized residuals, and let

\[
 B:A\longrightarrow L
\tag{1.2}
\]

be the continuous common-word Jacobian.  Choose a finite free cover

\[
 F=\Lambda^t,
\qquad
 q:F\twoheadrightarrow L.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Assume the cover is strict:

\[
 \boxed{q(J^rF)=J^rL\quad(r\geq0).}
\tag{1.4}
\]

For the quotient filtration of a finitely generated complete module,
(1.4) is the natural topology condition; it is stated explicitly because an
ambient filtration need not induce the intrinsic one.

Suppose a word-bearing \(\Lambda\)-linear map \(s_0:F\to A\) solves the
leading quotient:

\[
 \boxed{(Bs_0-q)(F)\subseteq JL.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (COMPLETE LOCALIZED BASED LIFT)

There is a continuous \(\Lambda\)-linear endomorphism

\[
 R:F\longrightarrow JF
\tag{1.6}
\]

such that

\[
 Bs_0=q(1+R).
\tag{1.7}
\]

The series

\[
 U=(1+R)^{-1}=\sum_{m\geq0}(-R)^m
\tag{1.8}
\]

converges, and

\[
 \boxed{s=s_0U:F\longrightarrow A,\qquad Bs=q.}
\tag{1.9}
\]

Every value of \(s\) is a convergent combination of the original
word-bearing columns of \(s_0\).  No quotient-module splitter
\(L\to A\) and no annihilator condition are used.

#### Proof

For every basis vector \(e_i\), equation (1.5) and strictness give

\[
 Bs_0(e_i)-q(e_i)\in JL=q(JF).
\tag{1.10}
\]

Choose \(r_i\in JF\) mapping to that error and extend
\(R(e_i)=r_i\) \(\Lambda\)-linearly.  This gives (1.6)--(1.7).  Because
\(R(F)\subseteq JF\),

\[
 R(J^rF)\subseteq J^{r+1}F,
\qquad
 R^m(F)\subseteq J^mF.
\tag{1.11}
\]

Completeness and separatedness make (1.8) converge to the two-sided inverse
of \(1+R\).  Equation (1.9) follows from (1.7).  The word-bearing statement
follows term by term. \(\square\)

This is v124's based construction applied to the whole localized target
rather than to the narrow cyclic class of one defect.

### Corollary 1.2 (LEADING SURJECTIVITY IS ENOUGH)

If \(L\) is generated by \(t\) elements and

\[
 \bar B:A/JA\longrightarrow L/JL
\tag{1.12}
\]

is onto on their classes by word-bearing columns, then lifts of those
columns define \(s_0\) satisfying (1.5).  Hence (1.9) follows.

#### Proof

Let \(e_i\) map under \(q\) to the chosen generators.  Surjectivity of
(1.12) supplies \(a_i\in A\) with
\(Ba_i-q(e_i)\in JL\).  Set \(s_0(e_i)=a_i\) and apply Theorem 1.1.
\(\square\)

Thus the completed linear problem is reduced to one finite leading solve for
a generator tuple, not one unrelated solve at every Zassenhaus depth.

## 2. Nonlinear localization hypotheses

Let \(\mathcal W\) be the complete space of relative words in one fixed
cofinal pro-\(3\) context, and let

\[
 \Phi:\mathcal W\longrightarrow L
\tag{2.1}
\]

denote the literal pair of hexagon residuals and printed-order pentagon
residual.  The use of \(L\) as codomain is load-bearing: every reachable
literal residual must remain in this module.

Assume:

1. **localized stability.**  If \(w\) is reachable and \(\Phi(w)\in J^rL\),
   and \(a\in J^rA\) is a legal common-word correction, then the exact
   residual after its ordered materialization remains in \(L\);
2. **one-depth affine law.**  For \(c=\operatorname{Mat}(a)\),

   \[
   [\Phi(wc)]_r=[\Phi(w)]_r+B_r[a]_r
   \quad\text{in }J^rL/J^{r+1}L;
   \tag{2.2}
   \]

3. **depth gain.**  If the right side of (2.2) vanishes, then
   \(\Phi(wc)\in J^{r+1}L\); and
4. **materialization.**  The values returned by \(s\) have legal ordered
   common-word representatives preserving the registered relative,
   commutator and side-gate typing.

V252 supplies precisely the intended R07 form of hypotheses 1 and 3 after
actual formation/common-word binding:

\[
 L\subseteq
 R_S(G_{H1})\times R_S(G_{H2})\times
 \bigl(B_P\cap R_S(G_P)\bigr),
\tag{2.3}
\]

with its induced complete filtration.  V99/v251 supply (2.2).  Whether the
actual inverse-limit object in (2.3) is a finitely generated strict
\(\Lambda\)-module and whether (1.12) is onto remain separate questions.

## 3. The nonlinear completion theorem

### Theorem 3.1 (LOCALIZED BASED NEWTON COMPLETION)

Assume Theorem 1.1 and all four hypotheses of Section 2.  Start with a
reachable word \(w_0\) satisfying

\[
 z_0=\Phi(w_0)\in L.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At depth \(r\), strictness of \(q\) permits a coefficient

\[
 v_r\in J^rF,
\qquad
 q(v_r)=z_r:=\Phi(w_r).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define

\[
 a_r=-s(v_r)\in J^rA,
\qquad
 c_r=\operatorname{Mat}(a_r),
\qquad
 w_{r+1}=w_rc_r.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
 \Phi(w_r)\in J^rL
\quad\text{for every }r,
\tag{3.4}
\]

the product \(c_0c_1c_2\cdots\) converges, and its limit \(w_\infty\)
satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\Phi(w_\infty)=0.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Assume \(z_r\in J^rL\).  Equation (1.4) gives (3.2), and the
\(\Lambda\)-linearity and filtration preservation of \(s=s_0U\) put
\(a_r\) in \(J^rA\).  By (1.9),

\[
 B(a_r)=-Bs(v_r)=-q(v_r)=-z_r.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The affine law (2.2) therefore kills the degree-\(r\) residual.  Depth gain
places the new exact residual in \(J^{r+1}L\), while localized stability
keeps it inside \(L\).  This proves (3.4) by induction.

The correction \(c_r\) has depth \(r\), so the partial products are Cauchy.
Completeness gives \(w_\infty\), continuity gives
\(\Phi(w_\infty)\in J^rL\) for every \(r\), and separatedness gives (3.5).
\(\square\)

The quadratic term \(q_2\) of v263/v266 is not required to return to the
narrow cyclic class \(\overline{\Xi\beta}\).  It only has to lie in \(L\),
which is hypothesis 1.  Equation (1.9) then supplies its next correction.
The same statement holds for every transported-linear or higher nonlinear
remainder.

### Corollary 3.2 (CYCLIC RETURN IS AN OPTIONAL NARROW ROUTE)

There are two distinct sufficient routes:

1. the pointed cyclic route of v263, which proves every exact remainder is
   a retained coefficient multiple of \(\beta\); or
2. the localized based route of Theorem 3.1, which proves \(B\) covers the
   whole nonlinear-stable module \(L\).

Failure of \(q_2\in\Xi\beta\) rejects route 1 for the named data but does not
reject route 2.  Conversely, failure of full leading surjectivity in (1.12)
does not reject the pointed cyclic route.

## 4. Effective selector and finite certificates

At each finite graded layer, (3.2) is a finite linear coordinate problem in
the fixed free cover.  Because strictness proves membership in advance,
deterministic row reduction terminates with an ancestry.  Composing that
ancestry with the fixed Neumann operator \(s=s_0(1+R)^{-1}\) is a uniform
based selector.

If a literal residual compiler already returns the \(q\)-coordinates of its
output, no elimination is needed.  V266 supplies such word-bearing ancestry
for the first class-two remainder once the actual A5/A6 correction exists.
For later layers, a Hall-collection or presentation-boundary compiler may
play the same role; its correctness must be authenticated and is not assumed
here.

A positive finite certificate for Theorem 1.1 retains:

1. the finite generator tuple of \(L/JL\) and the strict free cover \(q\);
2. word-bearing leading columns \(s_0(e_i)\);
3. exact coordinates \(R(e_i)\in JF\) satisfying (1.7);
4. the finite Neumann truncation in every registered quotient;
5. direct common-word replay of \(Bs=q\); and
6. destructive controls for a generator, an \(R\)-coordinate, factor order,
   prefix, formation/Brunnian tag and common-source ancestry.

For an inverse-limit proof, the same finite presentation and \(R\) must be
natural under the chosen cofinal quotients.  A separate independently chosen
matrix at every level does not define (1.8).

## 5. Exact R07 boundary

Theorem 3.1 would close the relative pro-\(3\) nonlinear H1/H2/P recurrence
if all of the following are proved:

1. the actual doubly localized residuals form one complete finitely generated
   \(\Xi\)-module \(L\);
2. its filtration is intrinsic/strict as in (1.4);
3. one common word supplies leading preimages for a finite generator tuple
   of \(L/JL\), not only for one projected endpoint or one cyclic defect;
4. the resulting \(s_0\) and error lift \(R\) retain actual common-source
   word ancestry;
5. v252's localization statement is bound to the same inverse-limit module
   and every registered side gate; and
6. nonabelian, mixed-prime and perfect-core edges are treated separately.

The pending A0/A3/A4 owners are needed to instantiate the literal target and
common-word maps.  V317--v318 may help prove the leading marginal part of
(1.12) if the joint context image is a tree fibre product.  They do not by
themselves prove full H1/H2/P leading surjectivity.

This route is stronger than the pointed route but removes the all-depth
cyclic-return quantifier.  Its next mathematical decision is now finite and
