## 2. The 486-dimensional ideal is principal

### Lemma 2.1 (CENTRAL CYCLIC AUGMENTATION GENERATOR)

One has an equality of two-sided ideals

\[
 \boxed{I(R_0)=A(z-1)=(z-1)A.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \boxed{\dim_k I(R_0)=486.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For a normal subgroup \(R\triangleleft D\), the kernel of
\(k[D]\to k[D/R]\) is the ideal generated by \(r-1\), \(r\in R\).
Here \(R_0=\langle z\rangle\), and

\[
 z^2-1=(z+1)(z-1).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus \(z-1\) alone generates the kernel.  Since \(z\) is central, its left
and right principal ideals agree.  Finally the quotient map is onto and the
group-element bases have sizes \(729\) and \(243\), so the kernel dimension
is \(729-243=486\). \(\square\)

For an explicit independent basis, use the normal-form transversal

\[
 T=\{x^a y^b h^r:0\leq a,b<9,\ 0\leq r<3\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

It has 243 members and is a left and right transversal because \(R_0\) is
central.  Since

\[
 k[R_0]\cong k[T_z]/(T_z^3),\qquad T_z=z-1,
\tag{2.5}
\]

the 486 vectors

\[
 \boxed{\{t(z-1),\ t(z-1)^2:t\in T\}}
\tag{2.6}
\]

are the v214 basis in the canonical order which first sorts
\((a,b,r)\) lexicographically and then sorts the two augmentation degrees
as \(1,2\) inside each \(t\).

## 3. One seed gives the complete endpoint image

Define the occurrence-space seed

\[
 u_0=(z-1)\mathbin\odot w
     =z\mathbin\odot w-w\in\widehat E_1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(U_0\) be the smallest \(k\)-subspace of \(\widehat E_1\) which
contains \(u_0\) and is stable under the four marked actions
\(x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}\).

### Theorem 3.1 (SINGLE-SEED COMPLETE PRE-GATE)

\[
 \boxed{
 U_0=I(R_0)\mathbin\odot w,
 \qquad \dim_k U_0\leq486.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently the complete pre-gate is exactly

\[
 \boxed{\bar\epsilon_1\in C(U_0).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Because \(x,y\) generate \(D_1\), the smallest stable subspace containing
any vector \(u\) is \(A\mathbin\odot u\).  Associativity of the module action
and Lemma 2.1 give

\[
 A\mathbin\odot u_0
 =A\mathbin\odot\bigl((z-1)\mathbin\odot w\bigr)
 =A(z-1)\mathbin\odot w
 =I(R_0)\mathbin\odot w.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The action map \(I(R_0)\to\widehat E_1\),
\(a\mapsto a\mathbin\odot w\), is linear, so its image has dimension at most
486.  Equation (3.3) is then v214 equation (3.12). \(\square\)

### Exact queue algorithm

Insert only \(u_0\) into a sparse echelon in \(\widehat E_1\).  Whenever a
row raises rank, retain its source-algebra ancestry and enqueue its four
marked translates.  Queue exhaustion is exact because the terminal span is
stable and contains the seed.  There are at most 486 rank increases.

Only after this occurrence-space closure is complete should the retained
basis be sent through \(C\) and echeloned in \(E_1^{\rm blk}\).  Closing
\(C(u_0)\) directly under an invented common block action would be invalid:
the eleven occurrence actions differ and \(C\) is not asserted equivariant.

This gives a helper-nonshared cross-check architecture:

1. the producer uses the one-seed invariant queue of Theorem 3.1;
2. the independent checker inserts the canonical 486 vectors (2.6), acts on
   \(w\), and compares the resulting occurrence and block-image spans; and
3. both retain a coefficient ancestry or a separating dual for (3.3).

The producer and checker therefore exercise genuinely different completeness
arguments while deciding the same finite subspace.

## 4. Area-adapted basis for the simultaneous pointed gate

Let

\[
 1\longrightarrow K_0\longrightarrow\Delta_1
 \longrightarrow\Delta_0\longrightarrow1
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the actual first relative Frattini edge.  V212 supplies the canonical
surjective invariant area functional

\[
 \lambda_0:K_0\twoheadrightarrow R_0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

After v188 returns a word-bearing \(k\)-basis of the elementary-abelian group
\(K_0\), identify \(R_0\) additively by
\(z^a\mapsto a\in\mathbf F_3\).  Ordinary row reduction against these
literal scalar area values produces a word-bearing adapted basis

\[
 s_*,k_2,\ldots,k_t,
 \qquad \lambda_0(s_*)=z,
 \qquad \lambda_0(k_i)=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Here the value `1` denotes the identity of the multiplicatively written
group \(R_0\); equivalently the additive area coordinate of each \(k_i\)
is zero.

Let \(d_1\) be the actual pointed row.  In the product of the pointed module
and \(\widehat E_1\), define

\[
 v_*=\bigl((s_*-1)d_1,u_0\bigr),
 \qquad
 v_i=\bigl((k_i-1)d_1,0\bigr)\quad(2\leq i\leq t).
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (AT MOST ONE ENDPOINT-BEARING JOINT SEED)

Let \(\widehat W_0\) denote the occurrence-space invariant span and let
\(T(z,v)=(z,C(v))\) be the block map of v214 equation (4.8).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_0
 &=\mathbf F_3[\Delta_1]\langle v_*,v_2,\ldots,v_t\rangle\\
 &=\{(\kappa d_1,\kappa\mathbin\odot w):\kappa\in I_0\}
 \subseteq Z_1\oplus\widehat E_1,\\
 W_0=T(\widehat W_0)
 &=\{(\kappa d_1,C(\kappa\mathbin\odot w)):\kappa\in I_0\}
 \subseteq Z_1\oplus E_1^{\rm blk}.
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{4.5}
\]

In particular, every kernel-of-area generator has identically zero
exponent-nine endpoint coordinate.  The sole potentially nonzero endpoint
coordinate seed is \(u_0\); if it vanishes, the complete projected endpoint
image vanishes.  The remaining seeds affect only the pointed coordinate.

#### Proof

The adapted elements in (4.3) generate \(K_0\).  Normality gives

\[
 I_0=\ker\bigl(k[\Delta_1]\to k[\Delta_0]\bigr)
 =k[\Delta_1]\langle s_*-1,k_i-1\rangle.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The exponent-nine action factors through
\(q_1:\Delta_1\twoheadrightarrow D_1\).  Its restriction to \(K_0\) is
\(\lambda_0\).  Hence

\[
 (s_*-1)\mathbin\odot w=(z-1)\mathbin\odot w=u_0,
 \qquad
 (k_i-1)\mathbin\odot w=0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Substituting (4.6)--(4.7) into v214 equations (4.7)--(4.13) gives the first
two lines of (4.5).  Applying the \(k\)-linear map \(T\) only after this
occurrence-space closure gives the last two lines; no equivariance of \(C\)
is used. \(\square\)

The adapted basis does not discard \(\ker\lambda_0\).  Those directions may
be indispensable for solving the pointed equation even though the matching
exponent-nine quotient cannot see them.
