# R07 cyclic Fox-template base-change theorem v164

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper theorem.  This note reduces the infinite base-change hypothesis
of v133 to a finite equivariant Fox-template gate.  It does not assert that
the current R07 all-seven implementation has passed that gate, does not test
the completed Smith coordinates of the actual R07 class, and does not declare
a compatible cofinal lift, fake, or Ihara witness.

## 1. Compatible cyclic covers

Let (k) be a field of characteristic (p), let

\[
 \Gamma_a=C_{p^a}=\langle \tau_a\rangle,
 \qquad
 R_a=k[\Gamma_a],
\tag{1.1}
\]

and choose the quotient maps

\[
 \Gamma_{a+1}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma_a,
 \qquad \tau_{a+1}\longmapsto\tau_a.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Put (T=\tau-1).  The characteristic-(p) binomial identity gives

\[
 R_a\cong k[T]/(T^{p^a}),
 \qquad
 \Lambda:=\varprojlim_a R_a\cong k[[T]].
\tag{1.3}
\]

Let (X) be a finite connected cellular presentation complex, with a fixed
orientation and lift of every cell, and let

\[
 \chi:\pi_1(X)\longrightarrow\mathbf Z_p
\tag{1.4}
\]

be a homomorphism whose reductions define regular covers (X_a\to X) with
deck groups (Gamma_a).  A dense image in (mathbf Z_p) is sufficient;
surjectivity of every finite reduction is the load-bearing condition.

If (c_i) is the number of (i)-cells of (X), the chosen cell lifts give
canonical deck-equivariant identifications

\[
 C_i(X_a;k)\cong R_a^{c_i}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Equal group values belonging to different oriented cells remain different
coordinates in (1.5).

## 2. Cellular and Fox matrices commute with reduction

Choose a finite presentation of (pi_1(X)).  The cellular boundary matrices
of the covers are obtained by taking the corresponding Fox derivatives and
applying

\[
 k[\pi_1(X)]\longrightarrow R_a,
 \qquad g\longmapsto\tau_a^{\chi_a(g)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The same statement holds for any finite ordered list of word maps built from
products, inverses, substitutions, and left translates, provided the list of
source and target cell-orbits is fixed at the base.

### Lemma 2.1 (FOX REDUCTION NATURALITY)

Let (w) be a word in the fixed generators and let
(partial w/\partial x_j) be its Fox derivative.  For (b\geq a), reduction
(R_b\twoheadrightarrow R_a) sends the evaluated derivative at level (b)
to the evaluated derivative at level (a).  The assertion remains true for
every matrix obtained from a fixed finite ordered list of such derivatives,
substitutions, and left translations.

#### Proof

Fox differentiation takes values in the integral group ring and satisfies

\[
 \frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial x_j}
 =\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_j}
  +u\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_j},
 \qquad
 \frac{\partial u^{-1}}{\partial x_j}
 =-u^{-1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_j}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Every map in (2.1) is a ring homomorphism, and the level maps commute by
(1.2).  Applying them to (2.2) proves the claim for products and inverses;
induction proves it for every fixed word.  Substitution is composition of
group-ring homomorphisms and therefore commutes with the same reduction.
Left translation is multiplication by a group-ring element, which also
commutes with reduction.  Entrywise application proves the matrix statement.
(square)

### Proposition 2.2 (TRANSLATED FAMILY AS ONE MODULE IMAGE)

Let (d_1,\ldots,d_m\in R_a^n) be the base columns of a typed block.  The
span of all deck translates

\[
 \{\tau_a^s d_j:0\leq s<p^a, 1\leq j\leq m\}
\tag{2.3}
\]

is exactly the image of the (R_a)-linear matrix

\[
 B_a:R_a^m\longrightarrow R_a^n,
 \qquad e_j\longmapsto d_j.
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Every element of (R_a) has a unique expression
(sum_s\alpha_s\tau_a^s).  Hence an (R_a)-linear combination of the base
columns is precisely a (k)-linear combination of all columns in (2.3), and
conversely.  Typed direct sums preserve this equality block by block.
(square)

Thus a finite implementation may enumerate every translate, but the
mathematical object is a fixed-rank group-ring map.  Completeness of the
translated roster is required: a sampled translate family is not the image
in (2.4).

## 3. One completed matrix

Assume now that the all-seven source and target coordinates are finite typed
direct sums of cell-orbits, and that every column is generated by one fixed
finite ordered Fox template as in Section 2.  Let

\[
 A=\Lambda^m,
 \qquad Z=\Lambda^n,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and let (B:A\to Z) be the matrix obtained by evaluating the same template
in (Lambda).  Let (z\in Z) be the vector obtained from one fixed literal
word and the same ordered evaluation template.

### Theorem 3.1 (CYCLIC FOX-TEMPLATE BASE CHANGE)

For every (a), there are canonical identifications

\[
 A_a:=R_a\otimes_\Lambda A\cong R_a^m,
 \qquad
 Z_a:=R_a\otimes_\Lambda Z\cong R_a^n,
\tag{3.2}
\]

under which the complete finite all-translate system is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 B_a=R_a\otimes_\Lambda B,
 \qquad z_a=1\otimes z.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Moreover the squares

\[
\begin{CD}
 A_b @>{B_b}>> Z_b\\
 @VVV                 @VVV\\
 A_a @>{B_a}>> Z_a
\end{CD}
\qquad(b\geq a)
\tag{3.4}
\]

commute literally in the frozen cell, block, and occurrence order.

#### Proof

The chosen cell lifts give (3.2).  Lemma 2.1 says every base-template entry
at level (a) is the reduction of its completed entry.  Proposition 2.2
says its (R_a)-image is the complete span of all enumerated translates, so
no additional finite-stage column is missing.  The fixed literal target is
governed by the same Fox naturality.  These facts prove (3.3), and functorial
reduction proves (3.4).  Since typed blocks are direct summands, no equality
of underlying group bytes can identify distinct coordinates. (square)

### Corollary 3.2 (FINITE AUTHENTICATION OF AN INFINITE HYPOTHESIS)

To establish v133 hypothesis (1.4) for one cyclic lane, it is sufficient to
authenticate the following finite data:

1. one compatible deck character (1.4) and the marked generator maps (1.2);
2. one finite ordered roster of source and target cell-orbits;
3. one finite ordered Fox template for both hexagons, every literal printed
   pentagon occurrence, and every presentation-boundary block;
4. direct confirmation that the implementation's full translated columns
   are exactly the translates in Proposition 2.2; and
5. one literal target word whose finite targets are obtained by reduction.

No comparison of infinitely many separately generated matrices is then
needed.  Failure of any item is a typing failure and forbids use of v133.

## 4. Inverse-limit consequence

The modules in (3.1) are finite free, separated, and complete.  Hence

\[
 A\cong\varprojlim_a A_a,
 \qquad Z\cong\varprojlim_a Z_a.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (ONE SMITH TEST FOR THE CYCLIC TEMPLATE)

Under Theorem 3.1, the following are equivalent:

1. (Ba=z) has a solution in (A);
2. the finite systems have a compatible family of solutions;
3. the completed Smith divisibility conditions of v133 Theorem 2.1 hold.

When they hold, the v133 Smith homotopy produces one (a\in A), and its
reductions solve every cyclic level in the literal template order.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 supplies exactly the base-change hypotheses of v133, while
(4.1) supplies completeness and separatedness.  Apply v133 Theorem 2.1 and
Proposition 3.1. (square)

This theorem does not say that a solution at one finite level makes the Smith
conditions true.  A finite solution sees only truncated divisibility.  The
completed transformed target must still pass every invariant-factor
valuation, or equivalently pass the one completed test.

## 5. Exact charming coordinates

The two normalized exponent coordinates are invariant under deck
translation.  They therefore factor through augmentation

\[
 \Lambda\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda/(T)\cong k,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and form a torsion target ((\Lambda/(T))^2), not a free (Lambda)-summand
of (Z).  They must not be silently appended to the free Smith matrix of
Section 4.

For the R07 programme this causes no new infinite obstruction.  V157 gives
the normalized exponent selector and literal exactification at every rung,
and v158 proves that on the formation-purified branch it factors through the
formation selector.  Thus Theorem 4.1 is applied to the relation/formation
Fox system, while exact charmingness is handled by v157--v158.  Alternatively
one may retain a finite presentation of the torsion target and compute Smith
data for that presented complex, but freeness must not be asserted.

## 6. Boundary of the R07 application

Theorem 3.1 turns the infinite R07 base-change obligation into a finite
template audit, but that audit has not yet been performed.  In particular,
the following must be checked rather than inferred from equal dimensions:

1. the same cyclic deck generator acts on H1, H2, and all five ordered A.18
   coface occurrences;
2. the PB3/PB4 boundary columns are complete (R_a)-spans of fixed typed
   base columns;
3. the implementation's left-translation convention agrees with the deck
   action in (2.3);
4. changing cyclic level introduces no new cell orbit or section-dependent
   column outside the registered template; and
5. the actual R07 defect after the first exact common word is the reduction
   of one completed target (z).

If these gates pass, one completed Smith computation replaces all individual
cyclic-stage membership searches.  It still has to show that the named R07
target satisfies the Smith divisibility conditions.  A failed condition is
an exact cyclic inverse-limit obstruction for this branch, not a proof that
every possible cofinal branch fails.

Nonabelian simple chief refinements are not cyclic covers and are unaffected
by this theorem.  Their accepted-set and side-gate conditions remain the
separate perfect-core problem of v155/v159.
