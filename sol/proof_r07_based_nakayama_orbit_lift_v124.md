# R07 based Nakayama orbit lift v124

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper theorem.  It upgrades a leading all-seven orbit solution on a
finite free cover of the actual defect module to an exact solution through a
nilpotent, or complete separated, fixed-context radical.  Unlike the v111
orbit-wide splitter, it does not require annihilator compatibility.  The
price is that it produces based preimages of a registered generating tuple,
not a well-defined right inverse on the quotient module.  The current
target6 calculation does not yet satisfy the hypotheses.  No cofinal lift,
fake, or Ihara witness is declared.

## 1. The distinction missed by the splitter formulation

Let \(\Lambda\) be a ring with a two-sided ideal \(\mathfrak a\), let
\(W,Z\) be left \(\Lambda\)-modules, and let

\[
 B:W\longrightarrow Z
\tag{1.1}
\]

be the word-bearing orbit-column map.  In the R07 application,
\(\Lambda=\mathbf F_3[\Delta]\), \(B=D s\), and every coefficient in \(W\)
is materialized by a product of actual conjugated normal-relator words.

Let \(Z^{\rm act}\subseteq Z\) be generated by a registered finite tuple

\[
 z_1,\ldots,z_t,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and introduce its finite free cover

\[
 F=\bigoplus_{i=1}^t\Lambda e_i,
 \qquad
 q:F\twoheadrightarrow Z^{\rm act},
 \qquad q(e_i)=z_i.
\tag{1.3}
\]

An orbit-wide splitter would be a map \(Z^{\rm act}\to W\).  Such a map
must kill every relation in \(\ker q\); in the cyclic case this is exactly
the annihilator condition of v111.  To correct the named classes (1.2), it
is enough instead to lift the free-cover map \(q\) through \(B\):

\[
 \widetilde s:F\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad B\widetilde s=q.
\tag{1.4}
\]

There is no requirement that \(\widetilde s\) vanish on \(\ker q\).  Two
expressions of the same defect may therefore give different corrections,
but their difference lies in \(\ker B\).  This is harmless for a based
explicit witness.

## 2. Constructive free-cover Nakayama theorem

Assume first that

\[
 \mathfrak a^L=0
\tag{2.1}
\]

for some \(L\).  Let \(s_0:F\to W\) be a \(\Lambda\)-linear choice of
word-bearing orbit columns.  Suppose that for some \(j\geq1\)

\[
 (B s_0-q)(F)\subseteq\mathfrak a^j Z^{\rm act}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (BASED FREE-COVER NAKAYAMA LIFT)

Under (1.1)--(2.2), one can choose a \(\Lambda\)-linear endomorphism

\[
 R:F\longrightarrow F,
 \qquad R(F)\subseteq\mathfrak a^jF,
\tag{2.3}
\]

such that

\[
 B s_0=q(1+R).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The finite Neumann polynomial

\[
 U=(1+R)^{-1}
   =\sum_{m=0}^{\lceil L/j\rceil-1}(-R)^m
\tag{2.5}
\]

is defined, and

\[
 \boxed{\widetilde s=s_0U,\qquad B\widetilde s=q.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus every registered generator \(z_i\), and hence every displayed actual
defect \(q(v)\), has an explicit word-bearing preimage under \(B\).

#### Proof

Because \(q\) is onto,

\[
 q(\mathfrak a^jF)=\mathfrak a^jZ^{\rm act}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

For each basis vector choose \(r_i\in\mathfrak a^jF\) with

\[
 q(r_i)=B s_0(e_i)-q(e_i).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Define \(R(e_i)=r_i\) and extend \(\Lambda\)-linearly.  Equations
(2.8) give (2.4).  Since \(\mathfrak a\) is two-sided,

\[
 R(\mathfrak a^kF)\subseteq\mathfrak a^{k+j}F,
\qquad
 R^m(F)\subseteq\mathfrak a^{mj}F.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Hence \(R^m=0\) once \(mj\geq L\), so (2.5) is the two-sided inverse of
\(1+R\).  Finally,

\[
 B\widetilde s
 =B s_0(1+R)^{-1}
 =q(1+R)(1+R)^{-1}
 =q.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Every operation in (2.5) is a finite linear combination of the original
word-bearing orbit columns.  This proves the materialization assertion.
\(\square\)

### Remark 2.2 (no hidden splitting)

The theorem does **not** define

\[
 Z^{\rm act}\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad q(v)\longmapsto\widetilde s(v),
\tag{2.11}
\]

unless \(\widetilde s(\ker q)=0\).  Consequently it does not contradict the
v111 annihilator criterion.  That criterion is necessary for the quotient
map (2.11), whereas Theorem 2.1 only constructs the based lift (1.4).

## 3. Cyclic actual-class formula

Take \(t=1\), write \(z=z_1\), and let \(a_0=s_0(e_1)\).  Condition (2.2)
says that one may choose \(\rho\in\mathfrak a^j\) with

\[
 B a_0-z=\rho z,
 \qquad
 B a_0=(1+\rho)z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Corollary 3.1 (BASED CYCLIC UNIT CORRECTION)

\[
 \boxed{
 a=(1+\rho)^{-1}a_0
   =\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\lceil L/j\rceil-1}(-\rho)^m\right)a_0,
 \qquad Ba=z.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No condition

\[
 \operatorname{Ann}_\Lambda(z)a_0=0
\tag{3.3}
\]

is used.  Different choices of \(\rho\) can produce different based
preimages; all are legitimate if their materialized words pass the side
gates.

For a desired correction of a defect \(\beta\), put \(z=-\beta\).  This
keeps the frozen R07 convention \(\beta+D(c)=0\).

## 4. Complete separated version

Assume now that \(\Lambda,F,W,Z^{\rm act}\) are complete and separated for
the \(\mathfrak a\)-adic filtrations and that all displayed maps are
continuous.  The construction (2.8) again gives a filtration-raising
endomorphism \(R\).  Then

\[
 U=\sum_{m\geq0}(-R)^m
\tag{4.1}
\]

converges, and the same calculation proves (2.6).  Thus a compatible
free-cover error lift gives one closed-form correction through infinitely
many refinements of the **same complete filtered context module**.

This is the based analogue of the v111 homotopy.  It replaces a quotient
splitter by a lift of finitely many named generators and replaces the
annihilator equations by the error-coordinate equations (2.8).

## 5. Naturality across a matched inverse system

Let the data carry indices \(n\), and suppose reduction maps commute with
\(B_n,q_n,s_{0,n}\).  If the chosen free covers, generator tuples, and error
lifts satisfy

\[
 R_{n+1}\longmapsto R_n,
\tag{5.1}
\]

then every Neumann polynomial or series \(U_n=(1+R_n)^{-1}\) also commutes
with reduction.  Therefore

\[
 \widetilde s_n=s_{0,n}U_n
\tag{5.2}
\]

is a compatible based family.  Applied to compatible defect coordinates
\(v_n\in F_n\), it gives compatible correction values
\(\widetilde s_n(v_n)\).  V98 can then spell the values by an accumulated
ordinary-word product.

Condition (5.1) is weaker than a natural right inverse on the quotient
modules, but it is still genuine all-level information.  A solution and an
error lift at one finite context do not by themselves imply (5.1) after the
context group changes.  Nonabelian chief edges and nonlinear side gates are
also outside this theorem.

## 6. Fixed \(\Pi_4[3]\) consequence

For the current fixed context ring

\[
 \Lambda=\mathbf F_3[\Pi_4[3]],
 \qquad I=I(\Pi_4[3]),
\tag{6.1}
\]

v109 gives

\[
 I^{29}=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

If a full all-seven word-bearing orbit calculation at depth \(j=9\)
returns a finite generator lift \(s_0\) satisfying (2.2) in the **actual
full residual submodule**, then

\[
 R^4(F)\subseteq I^{36}F=0
\tag{6.3}
\]

and the exact fixed-context lift is

\[
 \boxed{\widetilde s=s_0(1-R+R^2-R^3).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus, after the error-coordinate gate is met, separate arbitrary searches at
Jennings depths 10, 11, and 12 are unnecessary: one depth-9 success and the
finite matrix \(R\) determine the correction through the whole depth-29
radical.

This consequence does not apply to the present task169 target6 result, even
if it is positive.  Task169 uses one projected hexagon component and a
registered 28-row overapproximation; it has not constructed the full
H1/H2/ordered-pentagon module, the generator cover \(q\), or an error lift
\(R\).  The first eligible input is the successor to task175: the exact
all-seven orbit solve with word-bearing columns.
