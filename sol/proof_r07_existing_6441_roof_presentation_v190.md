## 1. A finite-extension presentation lemma

Let (F=F(X)) be free on a finite marked set and let

\[
 1\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow G
 \xrightarrow{\pi}Q\longrightarrow1
\tag{1.1}
\]

be an exact finite marked extension.  Let

\[
 \rho:F\twoheadrightarrow G,
 \qquad \bar\rho=\pi\rho:F\twoheadrightarrow Q.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Choose source words (s_1,\ldots,s_k\in\ker\bar\rho) whose images

\[
 \gamma_i=\rho(s_i)
\tag{1.3}
\]

generate (Gamma).  Choose, for every (gamma\in\Gamma), a word
(	au_gamma) in the letters (s_i^{\pm1}) such that

\[
 \rho(\tau_gamma)=\gamma,
 \qquad \tau_1=1.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Assume a complete marked presentation

\[
 Q\cong\langle X\mid q_1,\ldots,q_m\rangle
\tag{1.5}
\]

has been authenticated.  Put

\[
 \delta_j=\rho(q_j)\in\Gamma.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Construct three source-word families.

1. For every (gamma\in\Gamma) and (1\leq i\leq k), take the Cayley-edge
   loop

   \[
    c_{\gamma,i}=\tau_gamma s_i
       \tau_{\gamma\gamma_i}^{-1}.
   \tag{1.7}
   \]

2. For every (u\in X^{\pm1}) and every (i), take the action loop

   \[
    a_{u,i}=u^{-1}s_i u\,
       \tau_{\rho(u)^{-1}\gamma_i\rho(u)}^{-1}.
   \tag{1.8}
   \]

   It is harmless to retain both orientations separately.
3. For every roof relator, take the adjusted lift

   \[
    \ell_j=q_j\tau_{\delta_j}^{-1}.
   \tag{1.9}
   \]

Every word (1.7)--(1.9) lies in (ker\rho).

### Theorem 1.1 (CAYLEY--ACTION--LIFT PRESENTATION)

Let (mathcal R) be the union of (1.7)--(1.9).  Then

\[
 \boxed{\ker\rho=\langle\!\langle\mathcal R\rangle\!\rangle_F,}
\tag{1.10}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{G\cong\langle X\mid\mathcal R\rangle.}
\tag{1.11}
\]

#### Proof

Let (P=F/\langle\!\langle\mathcal R\rangle\!\rangle).  Since every
relator evaluates to the identity in (G), there is a marked surjection

\[
 P\twoheadrightarrow G.
\tag{1.12}
\]

Let (C\leq P) be generated by the images of the words (s_i).  The full
Cayley-edge family (1.7) is the standard complete Cayley presentation of
(Gamma) on the possibly redundant generating list
(gamma_1,\ldots,gamma_k): scanning a word from the identity rewrites
each prefix to its chosen section (	au_gamma), and an identity word
rewrites to (	au_1=1).  Consequently

\[
 |C|\leq|\Gamma|.
\tag{1.13}
\]

The action relations (1.8) make (C) invariant under every marked generator
and its inverse.  Hence (C\triangleleft P).  Modulo (C), each adjusted
lift (1.9) becomes (q_j=1).  Completeness of (1.5) therefore gives

\[
 |P/C|\leq|Q|.
\tag{1.14}
\]

It follows that (P) is finite and

\[
 |P|\leq|\Gamma||Q|=|G|.
\tag{1.15}
\]

The surjection (1.12) gives the reverse inequality, so it is an isomorphism.
Its kernel is trivial, which is precisely (1.10). \(\square\)

The theorem requires the **complete** Cayley edge table and a complete
presentation of (Q).  A spanning tree, sampled edges, or relators which
merely evaluate to one would prove only the easy containment in (1.10).
