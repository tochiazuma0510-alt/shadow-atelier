## 1. Relative ideals and the induced coarse projection

Let `k=F_3` and let

\[
 G\mathrel{\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}^{\pi}}G_0,
 \qquad K=\ker\pi,
 \qquad q:G\twoheadrightarrow D,
 \qquad R=q(K).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Replace `D` by `q(G)` if necessary.  There is then an induced epimorphism

\[
 \bar q:G_0\twoheadrightarrow D/R,
 \qquad \bar q(\pi(g))=q(g)R.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
 I=\ker(k[G]\to k[G_0]),
 \qquad I_R=\ker(k[D]\to k[D/R]),
\tag{1.3}
\]

and let

\[
 Q=q_*|_I:I\longrightarrow I_R.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The map `Q` is onto.  Indeed, `I` is generated by the elements `g(k-1)`
with `g in G` and `k in K`, while `I_R` is generated by `d(r-1)` with
`d in D` and `r in R`; both required group maps are onto.  V247 therefore
supplies a word-bearing linear section

\[
 \alpha:I_R\longrightarrow I,
 \qquad Q\alpha=1.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently there is always an exact, but not yet explicit, split

\[
 \boxed{I=\alpha(I_R)\oplus\mathcal K_q,\qquad
        \mathcal K_q=\ker Q.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

The first summand is the projected relative-dihedral part.  The purpose of
Sections 2--3 is to give the complete finite word-bearing structure of the
second summand.

## 2. Local field-outer kernel

Assume, as at an elementary-abelian relative Frattini edge, that the exact
sequence `K -> R` has a group section.  Choose

\[
 K=C\oplus K_0,
 \qquad q|_C:C\mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\sim}}R,
 \qquad K_0=\ker(q|_K).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let `j:k[R]->k[K]` be the induced group-algebra section and write
`a_H` for the augmentation ideal of `k[H]`.  Then

\[
 \mathfrak a_K=j(\mathfrak a_R)\oplus L_0,
 \qquad
 L_0=\ker(\mathfrak a_K\to\mathfrak a_R).
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (LOCAL OUTER BASIS)

Under (2.1),

\[
 \boxed{L_0=k[C]\otimes\mathfrak a_{K_0}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \{c(u-1):c\in C,\ u\in K_0\setminus\{1\}\}}
\tag{2.4}
\]

is a basis of `L0`, and

\[
 \dim_k L_0=|K|-|R|.
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

The tensor decomposition `k[K]=k[C] \otimes k[K0]` identifies the map to
`k[R]=k[C]` with `1 \otimes augmentation`.  Its kernel is (2.3), and the
group-element difference basis of `a_K0` gives (2.4)--(2.5).  This is the
local decomposition proved in v339.  \(\square\)

Choose a group transversal `s(a) in G`, indexed by `a in G0`, for the left
`K`-cosets of `G`.  The relative ideal has the block decomposition

\[
 \boxed{I=\bigoplus_{a\in G_0}s(a)\mathfrak a_K.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus all translated local outer vectors

\[
 s(a)c(u-1)
 \quad(a\in G_0,\ c\in C,\ u\ne1\text{ in }K_0)
\tag{2.7}
\]

belong to `K_q`.  They contribute exactly

\[
 |G_0|(|K|-|R|)
\tag{2.8}
\]

independent directions.  They need not be the whole of `K_q`.

## 3. Prefix-collision kernel and a complete basis

For each `b in D/R`, choose one base point

\[
 a_b\in\bar q^{-1}(b).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For every `a in bar q^{-1}(b)`, there is a unique `r_a in R` such that

\[
 q(s(a))=q(s(a_b))r_a,
 \qquad r_{a_b}=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `B_R` be any basis of `a_R`; for example

\[
 B_R=\{r-1:r\in R\setminus\{1\}\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `a != a_b` in the same fibre and `v in B_R`, define

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_{a,v}
 =s(a)j(v)-s(a_b)j(r_av).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The expression is word-bearing because `j(v)` and `j(r_a v)` are finite
linear combinations of elements of the selected subgroup `C` of `K`.

### Theorem 3.1 (FULL PROJECTED-KERNEL BASIS)

The union of

1. every translated local basis vector (2.7), and
2. every prefix-collision vector (3.4)

is a basis of `K_q`.  In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \dim_k\mathcal K_q
 =|G_0|(|K|-|R|)
  +( |G_0|-|D/R|)(|R|-1).}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \dim_k\mathcal K_q
 =|G_0|(|K|-1)-|D/R|(|R|-1)
 =\dim I-\dim I_R.
\tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof

Use (2.2) inside every block of (2.6).  The direct sum of the translated
`L0` blocks is already contained in the kernel and gives the first family.
On the complementary anchor blocks, write an element as

\[
 \sum_{a\in G_0}s(a)j(v_a),
 \qquad v_a\in\mathfrak a_R.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The images belonging to different `D/R` cosets lie in distinct direct
summands of `I_R`.  Within the fibre over `b`, the kernel condition is

\[
 \sum_{a\in\bar q^{-1}(b)}r_av_a=0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Choose the nonbase values `v_a` freely.  Equation (3.8) forces

\[
 v_{a_b}=-\sum_{a\ne a_b}r_av_a,
\tag{3.9}
\]

and substitution into (3.7) expresses the fibre component as a sum of the
vectors (3.4).  Conversely (3.2) gives

\[
 Q(\gamma_{a,v})
 =q(s(a))v-q(s(a_b))r_av=0.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Independence follows first from the local direct sum (2.2), and then from
the coefficient in each nonbase anchor block of (3.4).  A fibre of size
`m` contributes `(m-1)(|R|-1)` collision directions.  Summing over all
fibres gives the second term of (3.5); (2.8) gives the first.  Formula
(3.6) follows by simplification and also audits rank-nullity.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (WHEN THE LOCAL OUTER PART IS COMPLETE)

The v339 translated `K0` augmentation is the whole of `K_q` if and only if
the induced map

\[
 \bar q:G_0\longrightarrow D/R
\tag{3.11}
\]

is injective, hence bijective.  If one fibre has more than one coarse
prefix, the collision roster (3.4) is load-bearing and cannot be replaced
by additional translates inside a single `K`-block.

#### Proof

The collision dimension in (3.5) vanishes exactly when
`|G0|=|D/R|`.  Since (1.2) is onto, this is equivalent to injectivity.
\(\square\)

At the first R07 edge, `R=<z0>` has order three.  With the v339 anchor
`k_z` and `q(s(a))=q(s(a_b))z0^{d_a}`, a literal collision basis is

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_{a,e}
 =s(a)(k_z^e-1)
  -s(a_b)k_z^{d_a}(k_z^e-1),
 \qquad e=1,2.}
\tag{3.12}
\]

Each vector expands into four retained source words, lies in the actual
roof-relative ideal, and has zero exponent-nine projected image.

## 4. Exact three-block A5 decomposition

Let the projected occurrence endpoint factor through `Q` as in v214:

\[
 \Phi:I\longrightarrow E,
 \qquad \Phi=\Psi Q,
 \qquad \Psi:I_R\longrightarrow E.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose the positive projected A3 gate returns `v0 in I_R` with

\[
 \Psi(v_0)=\bar\epsilon_1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (PROJECTED / LOCAL-OUTER / COLLISION SPLIT)

The complete affine set of first-edge coefficients passing the matching
projected endpoint is

\[
 \boxed{
 \{\mu\in I:\Phi(\mu)=\bar\epsilon_1\}
 =\alpha(v_0)+\alpha(\ker\Psi)+\mathcal K_q.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Using Theorem 3.1, the last summand is the direct sum of

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_q
 =\mathcal K_{\rm local\mbox{-}out}
  \oplus\mathcal K_{\rm prefix\mbox{-}collision}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the three honest word-bearing blocks are:

1. projected relative-dihedral directions `alpha(ker Psi)`;
2. translated local `K0` augmentation directions (2.7); and
3. projected-prefix collision directions (3.4).

#### Proof

By (1.6), write uniquely `mu=alpha(v)+z` with `z in K_q`.  Equation
(4.1) gives `Phi(mu)=Psi(v)`.  The solutions of `Psi(v)=bar epsilon_1`
are `v0+ker Psi`, proving (4.3).  Theorem 3.1 and the direct decompositions
used in its proof give (4.4).  \(\square\)

Let

\[
 \mathscr D:I\longrightarrow Z,
 \qquad \mathscr D(\mu)=\mu d_1
\tag{4.5}
\]

be the pointed row map.  The simultaneous A5 equations are now one finite
membership with an explicit complete domain:

### Corollary 4.2 (A5 MEMBER CRITERION AFTER A3/A4)

There is a coefficient satisfying

\[
 \mathscr D(\mu)=e_1,
 \qquad \Phi(\mu)=\bar\epsilon_1
\tag{4.6}

if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 e_1-\mathscr D\alpha(v_0)
 \in
 \mathscr D\bigl(\alpha(\ker\Psi)+\mathcal K_q\bigr).}
\tag{4.7}
\]

A retained ancestry in the three rosters constructs `mu` explicitly.  The
complete homogeneous direction required by v338 is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal N_1
 =\ker\left(
 \mathscr D|_{\alpha(\ker\Psi)+\mathcal K_q}
 \right).}
\tag{4.8}
\]

#### Proof

Substitute (4.3) into the first equation of (4.6).  Equation (4.7) is the
resulting affine membership.  Taking differences of two simultaneous
solutions gives (4.8), which equals `ker D intersect ker Phi` by (4.3) with
zero target.  \(\square\)

The existing occurrence-level invariant closure may compute the image in
(4.7) without materializing every basis vector.  If it claims a complete
domain kernel for v338, however, it must retain dependencies spanning all
three source blocks; an image rank alone loses (4.8).

## 5. Consequence for the generalized relative-dihedral homotopy

V339 identified the anchor and the local `K0` augmentation.  Theorem 3.1
shows the exact qualification:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{full field-even/projected kernel}
 =\text{local }K_0\text{ augmentation}
  \oplus\text{prefix-collision kernel}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The pure relative-dihedral section handles `alpha(I_R)`.  The second
homotopy tested by v333 must pair its score basis against both summands of
(5.1), not just against the primitive `ell_i-1` words inside one `K`-block.
This is still finite at every elementary-abelian edge.  Its size is given
exactly by (3.5), and every basis vector has literal ancestry.

At every matched rung, `K_m` and `R_m` are elementary abelian and the same
construction applies after row-reducing a word-bearing basis to choose a
section of `K_m -> R_m`.  By v259, these edge-local sections and fibre bases
need not be natural under reduction for one based compatible product.
What remains open is actual MEMBER/score success at every edge, not the
compatibility of already selected kernel-typed corrections.

This theorem does not promote one first-edge positive to all rungs.  It
does remove an unnamed component from the promised explicit lift: the full
linear correction roster at one rung is now projected directions plus the
two explicit kernel bases in (2.7) and (3.4).
