## 1. The closed reachable module

Let \(\Lambda\) be a compact Hausdorff topological ring, let
\(J\triangleleft\Lambda\) be a closed two-sided ideal, and form every module
product such as \(JM\) by taking the closed span of the finite products.
Let \(P\) and \(L_{\rm amb}\) be compact Hausdorff left
\(\Lambda\)-modules, and let

\[
 B:P\longrightarrow L_{\rm amb}
\tag{1.1}
\]

be continuous and \(\Lambda\)-linear.

Let \(Q\) be a compact group, let \(S=S^{-1}\subseteq Q\), and assume

\[
 Q=\overline{\langle S\rangle}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For a compact right \(Q\)-orbit

\[
 {\cal W}=w_0Q
\tag{1.3}
\]

let

\[
 \Phi:{\cal W}\longrightarrow L_{\rm amb}
\tag{1.4}
\]

be continuous.  No additivity or homomorphism property of \(\Phi\) is
assumed.  Define the class-specific target

\[
 \boxed{
 L_{\rm reach}
 =
 \overline{\Lambda B(P)+
 \sum_{w\in{\cal W}}\Lambda\Phi(w)}
 \ \leq L_{\rm amb}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The sum in (1.5) means finite sums before closure.  Thus
\(L_{\rm reach}\) is the smallest closed \(\Lambda\)-submodule containing
all actual leading correction values and all residuals which can occur on
the chosen correction orbit.  It is compact Hausdorff.

Write

\[
 \rho:L_{\rm reach}\longrightarrow
 \overline L_{\rm reach}:=
 L_{\rm reach}/JL_{\rm reach}
\tag{1.6}
\]

and let

\[
 \overline B:P/JP\longrightarrow\overline L_{\rm reach}
\tag{1.7}
\]

be induced by \(B\).

## 2. One orbit class forces leading onto

### Theorem 2.1 (ONE-DEFECT REACHABLE-CLASS LEADING ONTO)

Assume:

1. \(L_{\rm reach}\) is \(J\)-separated:

   \[
    \bigcap_{n\geq0}J^nL_{\rm reach}=0;
   \tag{2.1}
   \]

2. every topological generator changes the residual, modulo \(J\), by an
   element of the leading image:

   \[
    \rho\bigl(\Phi(ws)-\Phi(w)\bigr)
       \in\operatorname {im}\overline B
    \quad(w\in{\cal W},\ s\in S);
   \tag{2.2}
   \]

3. the one initial full residual class lies in that image:

   \[
    \rho\Phi(w_0)\in\operatorname {im}\overline B.
   \tag{2.3}
   \]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\overline B:P/JP\twoheadrightarrow
        L_{\rm reach}/JL_{\rm reach}}
\tag{2.4}
\]

is onto.  Consequently v377 Theorem 2.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{
 B(P)=L_{\rm reach},
 \qquad
 B(J^nP)=J^nL_{\rm reach}\quad(n\geq0).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Neither an isomorphism from a larger ambient leading target nor a global
continuous choice of preimages is required.

#### Proof

Put \(H=\operatorname {im}\overline B\).  The domain \(P/JP\) is compact,
so \(H\) is compact and therefore closed in the Hausdorff module
\(\overline L_{\rm reach}\).  It is a
\(\Lambda/J\)-submodule.

For a finite word \(a=s_1\cdots s_m\) in \(S\), repeated use of (2.2)
gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \rho\Phi(w_0a)
  &=
  \rho\Phi(w_0)
  +\sum_{i=1}^m
    \rho\bigl(
      \Phi(w_0s_1\cdots s_i)
      -\Phi(w_0s_1\cdots s_{i-1})
    \bigr)\\
  &\in H
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]

by (2.3).  Finite words in \(S\) are dense in \(Q\).  Continuity of the
action, \(\Phi\), and \(\rho\), together with closedness of \(H\), therefore
gives

\[
 \rho\Phi({\cal W})\subseteq H.
\tag{2.7}
\]

By definition, \(H\) also contains \(\rho B(P)\).  Since it is a closed
\(\Lambda/J\)-submodule, (1.5) and (2.7) imply

\[
 \rho(L_{\rm reach})\subseteq H.
\tag{2.8}
\]

The reverse inclusion is tautological, while
\(\rho(L_{\rm reach})=\overline L_{\rm reach}\).  Hence
\(H=\overline L_{\rm reach}\), proving (2.4).  Apply v377 Theorem 2.1,
using (2.1), to obtain (2.5). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (AFFINE GENERATOR FORM)

Condition (2.2) follows if, for every \(w\in{\cal W}\) and \(s\in S\),
there is some \(t(w,s)\in P\) for which

\[
 \Phi(ws)-\Phi(w)-B(t(w,s))\in JL_{\rm reach}.
\tag{2.9}
\]

There is no requirement that the choices \(t(w,s)\) vary continuously or
be compatible.  In particular, a depth-zero instance of the one-depth
affine law is enough for the leading theorem.

## 3. Relative prefixes give the depth-zero orbit law

The following elementary observation isolates what the physical R07 owner
must authenticate.  Put

\[
 \widehat\Xi=\mathbf F_3[[\widehat\Delta_\infty]],
 \qquad
 \widehat J=\ker\left(
  \widehat\Xi\longrightarrow
  \mathbf F_3[
   [\widehat\Delta_\infty/\widehat P]]
 \right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If \(a,a'\in\widehat\Delta_\infty\) have the same image modulo
\(\widehat P\), then

\[
 a'-a\in\widehat J.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, for every compact \(\widehat\Xi\)-module \(M\) and every
\(v\in M\),

\[
 a'v-av\in\widehat JM.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (RELATIVE-PREFIX LEADING INVARIANCE)

Suppose the exact enriched two-hexagon/printed-pentagon word-pair replay has
the following common physical typing.

1. The correction orbit is

   \[
    {\cal W}=\widehat w_0\widehat{\mathscr Q}_C,
    \qquad
    \widehat{\mathscr Q}_C\leq\widehat P.
   \tag{3.4}
   \]

2. For every registered seed-conjugate correction \(s\), the exact Fox
   difference between \(\Phi(ws)\) and \(\Phi(w)\) is obtained by the
   eleven signed occurrence terms.  Each term has the form

   \[
    \sigma_o A_o(w,s)\,
       \delta\bigl(\rho_o(s)\bigr),
   \tag{3.5}
   \]

   after the same complete PB3/PB4 boundary quotient and legal
   localization used by \(q_{\rm loc}\).

3. Replacing \(w\) by another point of (3.4), or inserting earlier
   occurrences of \(s\) into a prefix in (3.5), does not change the coarse
   image of that prefix.  The fixed leading column \(B(t_s)\) uses the
   corresponding coarse prefixes.

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi(ws)-\Phi(w)-B(t_s)
 \in\widehat J L_{\rm reach}}
 \qquad(w\in{\cal W})
\tag{3.6}
\]

for every registered seed-conjugate generator \(s\).  Hence the symmetric
generator family satisfies (2.2).

#### Proof

For each occurrence, the moving prefix \(A_o(w,s)\) and its fixed leading
prefix have the same image in
\(\widehat\Delta_\infty/\widehat P\) by item 3.  Their difference therefore
lies in \(\widehat J\) by (3.2).  Multiplying this difference by the
occurrence path in (3.5) puts the difference of the two occurrence columns
in \(\widehat J L_{\rm reach}\).  Sum the eleven terms, then pass through
the common complete boundary quotient and legal localization.  Both maps
are \(\widehat\Xi\)-linear under the stated physical typing, so the result
remains in \(\widehat J L_{\rm reach}\).  This is (3.6).

The inverse of a generator requires no extra formula.  Apply (3.6) for
\(s\) at \(ws^{-1}\), then negate the resulting congruence. \(\square\)

This lemma is a relative Fox-prefix calculation, not a numerical assertion
that a proposed task198/task382 owner has the required common ABI.  In
particular, endpoint-only serialization cannot authenticate item 2.
