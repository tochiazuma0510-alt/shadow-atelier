## 2. What normal generation does and does not prove

At the first relative Frattini edge write

\[
 F\mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\rho _1}}\Delta _1
 \twoheadrightarrow\Delta _0,
 \qquad K=\ker(\Delta _1\to\Delta _0),
 \qquad A=\mathbf F_3[\Delta _1],                          \tag{2.1}
\]

where \(K\) is elementary abelian. Put

\[
 b_i=\rho _1(s_i)\in K,
 \qquad I=\ker(A\to\mathbf F_3[\Delta _0]).                \tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (MODULE GENERATION, AND NO MORE)

\[
 \boxed{K=\mathbf F_3[\Delta _0]
              \langle b_1,\ldots,b_{44}\rangle .}         \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every element of `ker rho0` is a product of conjugates of the
\(s_i^{\pm1}\). Its image in the elementary abelian group \(K\) is therefore
an \(\mathbf F_3\)-linear sum of conjugates of the \(b_i\). Conjugation on
\(K\) factors through \(\Delta _0\). Conversely all those conjugates lie in
\(K\). This proves (2.3). \(\square\)

Equation (2.3) does **not** imply

\[
 I=\sum_{i=1}^{44}A(b_i-1).                               \tag{2.4}
\]

Normal/module generation permits conjugates \(g b_i g^{-1}\); a one-sided
left ideal generated by \(b_i-1\) does not automatically contain the needed
right translates. The identity

\[
 (b_i-1)g=g(g^{-1}b_i g-1)                               \tag{2.5}
\]

only moves the problem to the conjugate difference and cannot be used
circularly to prove that this difference is already in the same left ideal.
For example, if \(K=\mathbf F_3^2\) and \(C_2\) swaps its coordinates, one
nonzero vector module-generates \(K\), but its single difference need not
left-generate the full relative ideal.

This exposes the same gap in v350 Lemma 1.1: the 6,441 normal generators, by
themselves, prove soundness of every finite direct-relator combination but do
not prove completeness of their one-sided A5 span. Accordingly:

- Every previously returned direct-relator `MEMBER` remains mathematically
  sound, because each used \(g(b_i-1)\) lies in \(I\).
- A miss or a dual against only those columns is `UNKNOWN_INCOMPLETE`, not
  `NONMEMBER`.
- Completeness is restored below by constructing an ordinary group basis of
  \(K\), not merely a module-generating roster.

The erratum propagates to v351 only where v351 invokes v350's complete
6,441-roster span. V351's conditional lift-null absorption and every finite
augmented hit remain sound; its assertion that the old roster alone is a
positive-complete dovetail is withdrawn until the K closure below is added.

## 3. Word-bearing ordinary basis of K

Choose the four marked actors

\[
 \mathcal X=\{x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}\}\subset\Delta _1,         \tag{3.1}
\]

using their frozen source words. Start with the pairs \((b_i,s_i)\). Keep an
exact \(\mathbf F_3\)-echelon in \(K\), and whenever a pair \((k,u)\) raises
its rank, retain it and enqueue

\[
 \bigl(a k a^{-1},\; a u a^{-1}\bigr),
 \qquad a\in\mathcal X .                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Dependent rows are discarded only after exact reduction; retained rows keep
their literal source word or an equivalent compact ancestry DAG.

In the physical Fox model, “exact echelon in \(K\)” means the raw
relation-module rows modulo the complete typed PB-boundary subspace. It is
not ordinary independence in the ambient raw coordinate dictionary. A
producer may implement this quotient lazily with the existing boundary
correlation oracle, but a declared dependency or exhausted K closure must
carry the corresponding boundary ledger.

### Theorem 3.1 (FINITE WORD-BEARING K CLOSURE)

The queue terminates. If its retained rows are

\[
 (k_1,u_1),\ldots,(k_t,u_t),                              \tag{3.3}
\]

then \(k_1,\ldots,k_t\) are an ordinary \(\mathbf F_3\)-basis of \(K\), and
\(\rho _1(u_\ell)=k_\ell\), \(\rho _0(u_\ell)=1\) for every \(\ell\).

#### Proof

At most \(\dim_{\mathbf F_3}K\) rows raise rank, so each of their four action
edges is enqueued only once and the queue is finite. On exhaustion, the
retained span \(W_K\) contains every \(b_i\) and is invariant under the marked
generators and their inverses. Hence it is a \(\Delta _0\)-submodule of \(K\).
Lemma 2.1 gives \(W_K=K\). Independence is the echelon invariant, and the
word assertions follow inductively from (3.2). \(\square\)

The exact insertion-attempt bound is

\[
 44+4t,\qquad t=\dim_{\mathbf F_3}K,                       \tag{3.4}
\]

not a bound on sparse word length or wall time.

### Theorem 3.2 (ORDINARY BASIS GENERATES THE LEFT IDEAL)

\[
 \boxed{I=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}A(k_\ell-1).}                  \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Choose a transversal for \(\Delta _1\to\Delta _0\). Grouping coefficients by
quotient coset shows that \(I\) is the left span of \(g(k-1)\), with
\(g\in\Delta _1\) and \(k\in K\). Since the \(k_\ell\) are an ordinary basis,
each \(k\) is a product of powers of the \(k_\ell\). Repeated use of

\[
 uv-1=(u-1)+u(v-1),\qquad
 u^{-1}-1=-u^{-1}(u-1)                                   \tag{3.6}
\]

expresses \(k-1\) in \(\sum_\ell A(k_\ell-1)\). The reverse inclusion is
immediate because every \(k_\ell\) lies in \(K\). \(\square\)

At source level a primitive translate has the literal roof-fibre form

\[
 g(k_\ell-1)\longleftarrow (w u_\ell)-w,
 \qquad \rho _1(w)=g,\quad\rho _0(wu_\ell)=\rho _0(w).     \tag{3.7}
\]

## 5. Exact A7 and the raw/quotient distinction

Let \(U(M)\) denote v351's three exact endpoint coordinates. If \(M,M'\in
J_0\) have the same first-shadow coefficient, then

\[
 L=M-M'\in\mathcal L_1,\qquad U(M')+U(L)=U(M).             \tag{5.1}
\]

This conditional absorption statement is valid and is independent of the
chosen basis of \(K\). Once Theorem 3.1 has completed, Theorem 3.2 permits
every legal first-shadow coefficient to be represented by the word-bearing
pairs (3.7); a fair dovetail of v351's translated Schreier lift-null seeds
therefore remains positive-complete for all three exact endpoints.

In the quotient one may write the lift-null column as \((0,U(L))\). The raw
machine must instead retain

\[
 (\widetilde q_L,U(L))                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

and a PB-boundary ledger proving that the generally nonzero raw row
\(\widetilde q_L\) dies in the quotient. Replacing it by raw zero without that
ledger is unsound.

An augmented A7 zero reduction is sound at any finite time and returns
\(\mu _1=\theta\), the literal \(M\), and all three endpoint zeros. Failure of
a bounded lift-null dovetail remains `UNKNOWN_RESOURCE`; this note supplies
no finite A7 NONMEMBER criterion.

## 6. Machine contract and speed boundary

The resumable owner stores two coupled sparse states:

1. K quotient pivots, literal-word ancestry, selected K-boundary ledger and
   oracle cursor, and the exact conjugation-queue cursor.
2. Pre-\(C\) A5 pivots, coefficient ancestry, marked-action cursor, and the
   PB-boundary ledger/oracle cursor.

Every accepted K pivot is streamed once into the A5 owner. A checkpoint must
bind both ranks and both cursors; reloading a large historical boundary
closure is not a mathematical requirement. The independent positive checker
replays only the selected K/conjugation ancestry, coefficient/action ancestry,
raw PB support, and endpoint/lift-null support.

The exact initial-defect reduction is

```text

removed initial evaluations: 6397
additional required work:    finite K conjugation closure, at most 4t edges
```

This is a seed-call statement, not a 146-fold wall-time or word-letter bound.

negative completeness does require the compact K closure above.
