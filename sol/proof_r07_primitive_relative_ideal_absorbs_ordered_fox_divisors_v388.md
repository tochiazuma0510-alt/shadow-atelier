## 1. Primitive generation of the relative ideal

Let \(k=\mathbf F_3\), let

\[
 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow \Gamma
 \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\pi}\overline\Gamma\longrightarrow1
\tag{1.1}
\]

be a finite group extension, and put

\[
 R=k[\Gamma],\qquad
 \overline R=k[\overline\Gamma],\qquad
 I=\ker(R\longrightarrow\overline R).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Assume that \(K\) is elementary abelian and retain a literal word-bearing
basis

\[
 K=\langle k_1,\ldots,k_t\rangle_{\mathbf F_3}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (PRIMITIVE RELATIVE-IDEAL GENERATION)

As a left \(R\)-ideal,

\[
 \boxed{I=\sum_{j=1}^t R(k_j-1).}
\tag{1.4}
\]

More explicitly, if

\[
 u=k_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots k_{i_m}^{\epsilon_m},
 \qquad \epsilon_a\in\{1,-1\},
\tag{1.5}
\]

then

\[
 u-1
 =\sum_{a=1}^m
   k_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1}\cdots k_{i_{a-1}}^{\epsilon_{a-1}}
   (k_{i_a}^{\epsilon_a}-1),
\tag{1.6}
\]

where

\[
 k_j^{-1}-1=-k_j^{-1}(k_j-1),
 \qquad k_j^2-1=(1+k_j)(k_j-1).
\tag{1.7}
\]

#### Proof

Choose a left transversal \(T\) for \(K\) in \(\Gamma\).  The group rings
split as vector spaces into coset blocks, and the quotient map restricts on
each block to augmentation on \(k[K]\):

\[
 R=\bigoplus_{a\in T}a k[K],
 \qquad
 I=\bigoplus_{a\in T}a\mathfrak a_K,
\tag{1.8}
\]

where \(\mathfrak a_K=\ker(k[K]\to k)\).  Formula (1.6) is the exact
noncommutative telescoping identity, and (1.7) reduces inverse letters to the
chosen positive primitive differences.  Hence
\(\mathfrak a_K=\sum_j k[K](k_j-1)\).  Substitution in (1.8) proves
(1.4). \(\square\)

The argument uses only a generating roster for \(K\); elementary abelianity
is used to make the roster a deterministic basis of exactly \(t\) words.

## 2. Every fixed/moving prefix difference is primitive-generated

For two ordered lists \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\) and
\(b_1,\ldots,b_s\) in \(\Gamma\), the exact product-difference identity is

\[
 \prod_{r=1}^s a_r-\prod_{r=1}^s b_r
 =\sum_{r=1}^s
  a_1\cdots a_{r-1}(a_r-b_r)b_{r+1}\cdots b_s.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (ORDERED PREFIX DIVISOR FACTORIZATION)

Suppose \(\pi(a_r)=\pi(b_r)\) for every \(r\).  Then every summand in
(2.1), and hence the whole product difference, lies in \(I\).  Moreover it
has an explicit left-\(R\) factorization through the finite primitive roster
\((k_j-1)_{j=1}^t\).

#### Proof

Write \(a_r=b_r u_r\) with \(u_r\in K\).  Then

\[
 a_r-b_r=b_r(u_r-1),
\tag{2.2}
\]

and Theorem 1.1 expands \(u_r-1\) through the primitive differences.  The
right suffix in (2.1) causes no new generator.  Indeed, for \(u\in K\) and
\(g\in\Gamma\), normality of \(K\) gives

\[
 (u-1)g=g(g^{-1}ug-1),
\tag{2.3}
\]

and the conjugate \(g^{-1}ug\in K\) is again expanded by (1.6).  Thus every
term of (2.1) belongs to the left ideal in (1.4), with a finite literal
factorization. \(\square\)

This covers both a difference of two complete prefixes and a difference at
one occurrence slot.  Conjugation, inverse slots, and a right suffix change
only the left coefficients, not the primitive roster.

## 3. Ordered Fox cross terms use the same ideal

Let \(M\) be a left \(R\)-module and let

\[
 \delta:\Gamma\longrightarrow M,
 \qquad
 \delta(uv)=\delta(u)+u\delta(v)
\tag{3.1}
\]

be a crossed homomorphism.  Evaluated left Fox chains are the motivating
example.  For \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\in K\), put
\(p_r=d_1\cdots d_r\) and \(p_0=1\).

### Theorem 3.1 (ORDERED FOX-DIVISOR FACTORIZATION)

The exact difference between the Fox chain of the ordered product and its
raw additive sum is

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta(d_1\cdots d_s)-\sum_{r=1}^s\delta(d_r)
 =\sum_{r=1}^s(p_{r-1}-1)\delta(d_r).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Every coefficient \(p_{r-1}-1\) lies in \(I\) and has the explicit
primitive factorization (1.6)--(1.7).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta(d_1\cdots d_s)-\sum_r\delta(d_r)
 \in IM
 =\sum_{j=1}^t R(k_j-1)M.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The same conclusion holds after multiplying by any registered fixed
occurrence prefix and after replacing any \(d_r\) by its inverse.

#### Proof

Iteration of (3.1) gives

\[
 \delta(d_1\cdots d_s)
 =\sum_{r=1}^s p_{r-1}\delta(d_r),
\tag{3.4}
\]

so subtraction gives (3.2).  Since every \(p_{r-1}\) lies in \(K\),
Theorem 1.1 gives (3.3).  A fixed prefix multiplies the displayed
factorization on the left.  Inverse slots use
\(\delta(d^{-1})=-d^{-1}\delta(d)\) together with (1.7). \(\square\)

Thus the nonlinear crossed-prefix error isolated in v251--v252 is not a new
untyped family.  It is an exact relative-ideal multiple of the same finite
primitive kernel differences.

### Corollary 3.2 (FINITE PRIMITIVE-SEED ROSTER)

If the left \(R\)-module \(M\) is generated by
\(m_1,\ldots,m_r\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 IM=\sum_{j=1}^t\sum_{a=1}^r R(k_j-1)m_a.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular, for the free seed module \(P=R^r\) with ordered basis
\(e_1,\ldots,e_r\), the canonical primitive divisor roster is

\[
 \boxed{(k_j-1)e_a\qquad(1\leq j\leq t,\ 1\leq a\leq r).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof

The kernel \(I\) is a two-sided ideal.  After expanding the second factor
of an element of \(IM\) in the chosen module generators, every coefficient
still lies in \(I\).  Theorem 1.1 writes that coefficient as a sum of left
multiples of the \(k_j-1\), which gives (3.5).  The free case gives (3.6).
\(\square\)

Thus a roster of only \(t\) images is justified only when the physical
divisor module is cyclic, or when a separately authenticated map has already
folded all seed coordinates into one cyclic source.  V369 uses
\(P_C=\Xi^r\), so the seed index may not be silently discarded.

### Theorem 4.1 (ALL OCCURRENCE DIVISORS USE THE PRIMITIVE ROSTER)

#### Proof

## 5. Anchor/outer form and all-rung use

Apply v339's deterministic split at edge \(i\):

\[
 K_i=S_i\oplus K_i^0,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with literal anchor words \(s_{i1},\ldots,s_{ia_i}\) and field-outer words
\(\ell_{i1},\ldots,\ell_{ib_i}\).  Theorem 4.1 becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \pi_i(\mathcal D_{\rm Fox})\subseteq
 \widehat\Xi_i\cdot
 \{\Sigma_i((s_{ir}-1)e_{ia}),
    \Sigma_i((\ell_{iq}-1)e_{ia})\}_{r,q,a}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

No natural choice of the split basis under reduction is required for this
coordinatewise statement.  The global Fox divisors themselves are already a
compatible family.  Therefore, if the primitive images in (5.2) lie in
\(L_i=\pi_i(L_{\rm reach})\) at every matched coordinate, v386 Theorem 1.1
places every global divisor in \(L_{\rm reach}\), gives
\(E_{\rm Fox}=L_{\rm reach}\), and kills every intrinsic saturation class.

When \(r_i=1\), or when a physical cyclic divisor ABI has independently
been authenticated, (5.2) reduces to v386's smaller displayed roster.  In
general v386 Theorem 3.1 must be read with the seed-refined roster (4.2),
not with an unsupported rank-one assumption.

This separates two tasks which had previously been mixed:

1. **factorization:** formal and uniform by Theorems 1.1--4.1 once the
   physical occurrence/Fox ABI is authenticated;
2. **membership:** genuinely arithmetic and word-bearing; each primitive
   image in (5.2) must be shown to lie in the actual reachable image.

The second task may be discharged by an A0 common word, a target-specific
v333 score-column equality, or a stronger onto result.  Resource exhaustion
or an untyped endpoint rank cannot discharge it.
