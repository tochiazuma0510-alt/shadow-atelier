# PB3 two-relator presentation equality v121

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper proof from an explicit Tietze transformation, with an independent
split-presentation proof using the already established PB4 theorem v108.  This
closes `PB3-PRES-EQ` at paper-proof plus cross-checked-input grade.  It is not

## 1. Frozen marked presentation

Use the frozen PB3 pair order

\[
 (a,b,c)=(A_{12},A_{13},A_{23}).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The recursive constructor `pure_relations(3)` in
`search/d972_b345_seedspan_triple4_v1.py:549-563` returns exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_1&=a^{-1}babcb^{-1}c^{-1}b^{-1},\\
 r_2&=a^{-1}cab c^{-1}b^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
 G_3=\langle a,b,c\mid r_1,r_2\rangle .
\tag{1.3}
\]

The question is whether the canonical marked epimorphism

\[
 \eta_3:G_3\longrightarrow P_3,
 \qquad a\mapsto A_{12},\ b\mapsto A_{13},\ c\mapsto A_{23},
\tag{1.4}
\]

is an isomorphism.  Merely replaying the two relators in a finite quotient
would not prove this.

## 2. Direct Tietze normal form

Adjoin a generator

\[
 t=abc.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The first relation in (1.2) is equivalent to

\[
 babc=abcb,
\tag{2.2}
\]

and hence to \(bt=tb\).  Indeed, multiplying \(r_1=1\) on the left by
\(a\), and then moving the terminal inverse factors, gives (2.2); reversing
these steps recovers \(r_1\).

The second relation is equivalent to

\[
 cab=abc,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and hence to \(ct=tc\).  Again the transformations are reversible.
Eliminating \(a=t c^{-1}b^{-1}\) therefore gives the exact Tietze
transformation

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_3
 &\cong \langle b,c,t\mid [b,t]=1,[c,t]=1\rangle\\
 &\cong F(b,c)\times\langle t\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Under (1.4), \(t\) is the full twist
\(A_{12}A_{13}A_{23}\).  Forgetting the third strand gives the split
Fadell--Neuwirth sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow F(A_{13},A_{23})\longrightarrow P_3
 \longrightarrow P_2\cong\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The full twist is central and maps to the generator \(A_{12}\) of \(P_2\).
Thus it gives a central splitting, and

\[
P_3=F(A_{13},A_{23})\times
 \langle A_{12}A_{13}A_{23}\rangle .
\tag{2.6}
\]

Equations (2.4)--(2.6) already prove that \(\eta_3\) is an isomorphism.
The next section gives a second proof that uses only the marked presentation
machinery already authenticated in this repository.

## 3. Independent split-presentation proof from PB4-PRES-EQ

Let

\[
 G_4=F(A_{12},A_{13},A_{14},A_{23},A_{24},A_{34})/
 \langle\!\langle R_4\rangle\!\rangle
\tag{3.1}
\]

be the frozen eleven-relator group.  Theorem 4.1 of
`sol/proof_pb4_eleven_relator_presentation_equality_v108.md` proves, as a
marked group,

\[
 G_4\cong P_4.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The endpoint coface \(i_4\) and fourth-strand deletion \(d_4\) have marked
generator tables

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &a&b&c\\ \hline
i_4&A_{12}&A_{13}&A_{23}
\end{array},
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 &A_{12}&A_{13}&A_{14}&A_{23}&A_{24}&A_{34}\\ \hline
d_4&a&b&1&c&1&1.
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The recursive definition of `pure_relations(4)` first embeds the two old
rank-three relators, and then appends the nine action relators for the free
kernel generated by \(A_{14},A_{24},A_{34}\).  Consequently:

1. \(i_4(r_1),i_4(r_2)\) are literally the first two rows of \(R_4\);
2. \(d_4\) sends those first two rows to \(r_1,r_2\);
3. \(d_4\) sends each of the remaining nine action rows to the empty word,
   because every rank-four kernel generator maps to \(1\).

Hence both maps descend to marked homomorphisms

\[
 \widehat i_4:G_3\longrightarrow G_4,
 \qquad
 \widehat d_4:G_4\longrightarrow G_3,
 \qquad
 \widehat d_4\widehat i_4=\operatorname{id}_{G_3}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now take \(g\in\ker\eta_3\).  Naturality of the marked maps and (3.2) give

\[
 \eta_4(\widehat i_4(g))=i_4(\eta_3(g))=1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \widehat i_4(g)=1.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Applying \(\widehat d_4\) and using (3.4) gives \(g=1\).  Thus \(\eta_3\)
is injective; it is visibly onto, so it is an isomorphism.

### Theorem 3.1 (PB3-PRES-EQ)

\[
\boxed{
 \langle A_{12},A_{13},A_{23}\mid r_1,r_2\rangle
 \cong P_3
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

as a marked group in the exact word convention used by the R07 programs.

## 4. Fox-boundary consequence

Let \(E_3\) be any frozen finite marked quotient of \(P_3\), let
\(H=\ker(P_3\to E_3)\), and put \(R=\mathbf F_3[E_3]\).  The Fox complex of
the exact presentation (3.6) has

\[
 R^2\xrightarrow{D_2}R^3\xrightarrow{D_1}R.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore the span of every left translate of the two frozen Fox columns is
the true presentation-boundary image, and

\[
 H_1(H;\mathbf F_3)\cong\ker D_1/\operatorname{im}D_2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular the H1 and H2 blocks in the all-seven R07 stack may mark their
PB3 `D2` input as exact.  This statement does not identify
\(\ker D_1\) with \(\operatorname{im}D_2\), and it says nothing yet about
solubility of the stacked target.
