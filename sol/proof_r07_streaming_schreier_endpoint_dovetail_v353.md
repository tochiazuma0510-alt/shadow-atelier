### Proposition 1.1 (EXACT SUCCESSOR EQUALITY)

For literal source words `v,w`,

\[
 \boxed{\rho _1(v)=\rho _1(w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \kappa(v)=\kappa(w).}
\tag{1.4}

#### Proof

Equation (1.1) defines `Delta1` as the image inside the ten-factor direct
product.  The affine multiplication law in each `E_j1` is completely
determined by the ordered pair (1.2), and task198's state contains both
entries rather than a quotient of them.  Equality in the direct product is
therefore coordinatewise equality of the ten complete pairs, which is

arithmetic path.  No A4 basis and no enumeration of `K` is needed to ask one

## 2. Schreier generators may be streamed by Cayley edges

Let

\[
 \mathcal A=(x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1})
\tag{2.1}
\]

in the frozen shortlex order.  Starting at the identity, run breadth-first
search in the marked right Cayley graph of `Delta1`.  The first word reaching
`q` is denoted `s(q)`.  When the edge `(q,t)` is explored, let

\[
 q'=q\rho _1(t),\qquad
 n(q,t)=s(q)t,s(q')^{-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The target representative `s(q')` is either created by that edge or was
created earlier.  In either case Proposition 1.1 directly replays

\[
 \rho _1(n(q,t))=1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (STREAMED SCHREIER ROSTER)

If every queued vertex and all four of its marked edges are eventually
processed, the nonidentity words (2.2) generate

\[
 N_1=\ker\bigl(\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Delta _1\bigr)
\tag{2.4}
\]

as a group, after mapping the literal `F(x,y)` words into the common exact
source image `Gamma`.  Identities and duplicate literal words may be deleted
after (2.3) is retained.

#### Proof

Because `Delta1` is finite and generated by (2.1), fair BFS eventually visits
every vertex and edge.  The first-arrival edges form a prefix-closed spanning
tree.  Formula (2.2) is the standard Schreier generator associated with a
directed Cayley edge.  Schreier rewriting expresses every word in
`ker(F(x,y)->Delta1)` as a product of these edge words and their inverses.
Mapping that equality to `Gamma` gives (2.4); words already trivial in
`Gamma` simply disappear.  This is the literal form of v310 (1.4)--(1.5).
\(\square\)

At any finite time the partial edge roster is only a positive search roster.
Queue exhaustion is not required before using a discovered generator in a
positive certificate, but a partial roster has no negative force.

## 3. A fair two-dimensional dovetail

Enumerate freely reduced translating words

\[
 V_0=1,V_1,V_2,\ldots
\tag{3.1}
\]

in shortlex order.  Number nontrivial Schreier seeds by their discovery order
`n_0,n_1,...`.  For every pair `(i,j)` define

\[
 L_{i,j}=V_j(n_i-1)=V_jn_i-V_j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Use any schedule satisfying both conditions:

1. every pending Cayley edge eventually receives a BFS step; and
2. for every discovered `n_i`, every pair `(i,j)` eventually receives a
   column step.

For example, alternate one BFS edge with one step of the diagonal enumeration
`i+j=0,1,2,...`, skipping indices not yet discovered and revisiting their
diagonals after later discoveries.  Equivalently keep one monotone cursor per
discovered seed and service the first `r` cursors during round `r`.

### Proposition 3.1 (FAIRNESS)

Every finite-support element of

\[
 \mathcal L_1=ker\bigl(k[\Gamma]\to k[\Delta _1]\bigr)
\tag{3.3}
\]

is a finite linear combination of columns (3.2), and every column in one such
finite expression is emitted after finitely many unbounded schedule steps.

#### Proof

Theorem 2.1 and v310 Theorem 2.2 give

\[
 \mathcal L_1=\sum_i k[\Gamma](n_i-1).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Every group-algebra element has finite support, and the words (3.1) map onto
`Gamma`, so (3.4) expands into a finite sum of (3.2).  Each of its finitely
many seeds is found after finitely many BFS steps by Theorem 2.1.  The second
fairness condition then emits each required translation after finitely many
additional steps.  \(\square\)

This theorem gives no practical time bound.  In particular, a resource stop
before a hit is `UNKNOWN_RESOURCE`, never A7 NONMEMBER.

## 5. Replayable checkpoint theorem

At a finite stopping time retain:

- all discovered section words `s(q)` and their complete state-key digests;
- the vertex/edge cursor and all retained literal Schreier words;
- the fair translation cursors;
- the finite endpoint echelon and its word-pair ancestry; and
- the task193, task198, canonical `M` and canonical endpoint physical
  identities.

### Proposition 5.1 (CHECKPOINT RECONSTRUCTION DOES NOT CHANGE THE UNIVERSE)

If every stored section word is reevaluated through the same authenticated
rho1 owner, every edge and seed is replayed, and the echelon is rebuilt from
its retained literal columns, then resuming from the next cursors enumerates
exactly the suffix of the original schedule.  No serialized Python group
object is a premise.

#### Proof

The BFS transition and equality decisions are deterministic functions of
the frozen letter order, the literal section words and the exact key (1.3).
The translation schedule is deterministic from its integer cursors.  Sparse
echelon insertion is deterministic from the ordered literal columns.
Reevaluation therefore restores the same finite state and the next step;
induction gives the same suffix.  \(\square\)

An internal RSS or wall guard can therefore publish a genuine continuation
point.  An external OS kill before the atomic checkpoint is uploaded remains
unrecoverable and must not be described as a saved state.
