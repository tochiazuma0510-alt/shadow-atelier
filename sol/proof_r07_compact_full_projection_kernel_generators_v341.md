## 3. Four-word collision seeds

Let `j:k[R] -> k[K]` be the section induced by `sigma`, and choose the
standard basis

\[

\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 \boxed{c_{j,v}=(n_j-1)j(v).}
\tag{3.2}

### Lemma 3.1 (LEGAL FULL-KERNEL SEEDS)

Every `c_{j,v}` belongs to `ker Q`.  If `v=r-1`, its literal expansion is

\[
 \boxed{

\tag{3.3}
\]

so it has four retained source words.

#### Proof

Under `k[G] -> k[G0]`, the right factor `j(v)` maps to its coefficient sum,
which is zero.  Thus (3.2) belongs to `I`.  Under `q_*`, the left factor
`n_j-1` maps to zero by (2.7), so `Q(c_{j,v})=0`.  Expanding the two factors
gives (3.3).  \(\square\)

Let `ell_1,...,ell_t` be a word-bearing basis of `K0` obtained from the A4
basis by v339.  The local outer augmentation is generated by the primitive

\[
 \mathcal K_{\rm local\mbox{-}out}

### Theorem 3.2 (COMPACT FULL-KERNEL GENERATOR THEOREM)

The complete projected kernel is

\[

\[

\tag{3.6}

#### Proof

The local part of v340's basis is generated by (3.4), using

\[

Then `q(n)=1`, `pi(n)=a_b^{-1}a in L`, and the section identity

 \gamma_{a,v}=s(a_b)(n-1)j(r_av).

Thus, after a left translation, it is enough to treat `(n-1)j(v')` for an

\tag{3.13}

Hence all of v340's collision basis lies in the right side of (3.5).

Conversely Lemma 3.1 and (3.4) put every displayed generator in `ker Q`,
