## 1. The non-repeating frontier

V99 already proves, on the actual matched diagram-chief ladder, the affine
linearization, literal closedness, and residual/Jacobian naturality formerly
called the structural parts of HT1, HT2, and HT5.  V98 removes compatible
ordinary-word spelling as a separate obstruction.  V109--v110 identify the
actual normal-orbit columns which can materialize one common correction word
in the target relation modules.

The remaining abelian question is not whether an unrelated coefficient
vector can be chosen at every finite level.  It is whether those actual
columns contain a **natural filtered right inverse on the particular defect
subsystem**.  This note gives the exact Hensel criterion for that statement.

## 2. Actual orbit-column datum at one edge

Fix one abelian chief edge of the matched ladder.  Let

\[
 D:C\longrightarrow Z
\tag{2.1}
\]

be the v99 actual common-word correction map into the complete closed
two-hexagon/ordered-pentagon residual module.  All linear side coordinates
which are imposed at this edge may be appended to \(Z\).  Let \(\beta\in Z\)
be the actual defect.

Let \(\Delta\) be the finite group generated by the registered simultaneous
context-conjugation operators on the stacked seven evaluation blocks, and put
\(\Lambda=\mathbf F_3[\Delta]\).  This is a hypothesis about one diagonal
action on the complete stack; seven unrelated lists of conjugators do not
define \(\Delta\).  After exact presentation and context typing, v110 gives a
finite normal-generator roster \(\mathcal R\).  Its full conjugacy-orbit
columns define a
\(\Lambda\)-linear map

\[
 B:A\longrightarrow Z,
 \qquad A=\Lambda^{(\mathcal R)},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and a word-bearing map

\[
 s:A\longrightarrow C,
 \qquad Ds=B.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Here a basis coefficient \(g e_r\) is materialized by an actual conjugate
\(u_g r u_g^{-1}\).  If exponent-mod-three coordinates are required, append
them to the target of \(B\) and restrict to the zero fibre.  Equation (2.3)
is about correction values in the abelian chief kernel; v98 supplies an
ordinary word representing each selected value.

Let \(\mathfrak a\triangleleft\Lambda\) be a nilpotent ideal contained in
the Jacobson radical.  In the current third-primary window it is supplied by
the appropriate transition/radical filtration.  Write

\[
 Z\supset \mathfrak a Z\supset\mathfrak a^2Z\supset\cdots,
 \qquad \mathfrak a^LZ=0
\tag{2.4}
\]

at a finite edge.  On the inverse limit the analogous filtration is assumed
complete and separated, and all displayed maps are assumed continuous.

The use of the full orbit in (2.2) is load-bearing: conjugation equivariance

An arbitrary list of 28 projected coefficient rows does not supply (2.2).

## 3. Filtered correction theorem

Let \(Z^{\rm act}\subseteq Z\) be a \(\Lambda\)-submodule containing the
actual defect and stable under all operators below.  It may be much smaller
than the complete closed residual module.

Assume there is a continuous filtered \(\Lambda\)-linear map

\[
 \sigma:Z^{\rm act}\longrightarrow A
\tag{3.1}
\]

such that, with

\[
 h_0=s\sigma,
 \qquad R=1-Dh_0=1-B\sigma,
\tag{3.2}
\]

one has

\[
 R(\mathfrak a^kZ^{\rm act})
 \subseteq \mathfrak a^{k+1}Z^{\rm act}
 \quad(k\geq0).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Equivalently, when all maps are \(\Lambda\)-linear, it is enough that

\[
 B\sigma=1
 \quad\bmod\ \mathfrak a Z^{\rm act}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (FILTERED ACTUAL-ORBIT HOMOTOPY)

Under (2.1)--(3.4), the finite or convergent Neumann formula

\[
 \boxed{
 h=h_0\sum_{j\geq0}R^j
 =s\sigma\sum_{j\geq0}(1-B\sigma)^j
 }
\tag{3.5}
\]

is a right inverse to \(D\) on \(Z^{\rm act}\):

\[
 \boxed{Dh=1_{Z^{\rm act}}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

At a finite edge the sum stops after at most \(L\) terms.  Hence

\[
 \boxed{c=-h(\beta)}
\tag{3.7}
\]

is an actual common-word correction value and is given explicitly by a
finite product of the orbit words in (2.3).  It is an admissible edge
correction if the word-bearing section \(s\) lands in the required marking,
exact-commutator, relative, and nonlinear side-gate domain.

#### Proof

Condition (3.3) gives \(R^L=0\) on a finite edge and topological nilpotence
on the complete inverse limit.  Therefore (3.5) is defined.  From (3.2),

\[
 Dh=(1-R)\sum_{j\geq0}R^j=1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The coefficient vector \(\sigma\sum R^j\beta\) lies in \(A\); applying the
word-bearing section \(s\) proves the materialization statement.  Finally
v99's exact affine identity gives

\[
 \beta+D(-h\beta)=0.
\tag{3.9}
\]

\(\square\)

This is a class-specific homotopy.  It does not require \(D\) to split on
all of \(Z\), and therefore does not revive the false demand that every
abstract relative module be contracted.

## 4. A finite cyclic criterion for the actual class

The phrase ``right inverse on the actual defect subsystem'' can be tested
without asking for a right inverse on the complete abstract residual module.
Pass to the leading quotient

\[
 \overline\Lambda=\Lambda/\mathfrak a,qquad
 \overline A=A/\mathfrak a A,qquad
 \overline Z=Z^{\rm act}/\mathfrak a Z^{\rm act},
\tag{4.1}
\]

and let \(\overline B:\overline A\to\overline Z\) be induced by \(B\).
For one fixed stacked actual defect \(z=\overline\beta\), take the smallest
context-stable leading subsystem

\[
 \overline Z_z=\overline\Lambda z.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its left annihilator is

\[
 K_z=\operatorname{Ann}_{\overline\Lambda}(z)
 =\{\lambda\in\overline\Lambda:\lambda z=0\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proposition 4.1 (ACTUAL-CLASS ANNIHILATOR TEST)

There is an \(\overline\Lambda\)-linear map

\[
 \overline\sigma:\overline Z_z\longrightarrow\overline A,
 \qquad
 \overline B\,\overline\sigma=1_{\overline Z_z},
\tag{4.4}
\]

if and only if there is a coefficient vector \(a\in\overline A\) such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \overline B a=z,
 \qquad K_z a=0.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

When (4.5) holds, the splitter is the explicit formula

\[
 \boxed{\overline\sigma(\lambda z)=\lambda a.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof

If (4.4) exists, take \(a=\overline\sigma(z)\).  Then
\(\overline B a=z\), and for \(\lambda z=0\), linearity gives
\(\lambda a=\overline\sigma(\lambda z)=0\).  Conversely, the second
condition in (4.5) makes (4.6) independent of the expression \(\lambda z\):
if \(\lambda z=\mu z\), then \(\lambda-\mu\in K_z\), hence
\((\lambda-\mu)a=0\).  The first condition in (4.5) gives

\[
 \overline B\,\overline\sigma(\lambda z)
 =\lambda\overline B a=\lambda z.
\]

\(\square\)

Thus membership \(z\in\operatorname{im}\overline B\) is necessary but is
not the desired uniform certificate.  The missing datum is exactly a
preimage whose context relations contain all relations of \(z\).  This is
why one successful target6 vector cannot by itself be propagated through
the context orbit or through finer levels.

For a subsystem generated by several registered actual classes
\(z_1,\ldots,z_t\), the same statement uses a presentation

\[
 \overline\Lambda^q\xrightarrow{Q}\overline\Lambda^t
 \longrightarrow\overline Z^{\rm act}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

One chooses \(a_i\in\overline A\) with
\(\overline B a_i=z_i\) and imposes the finite relation condition

\[
 Q(a_1,\ldots,a_t)=0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is equivalent to a well-defined leading splitter.  In the current
single-word R07 lane, the stacked two-hexagon/pentagon defect is one vector,
so (4.5) is the sharp first test.  If return or arithmetic operators are not
contained in the registered context algebra, they must first be adjoined to
that algebra; silently omitting them would make (4.5) too weak.

Lifting \(\overline\sigma\) to a filtered \(\sigma\) satisfying (3.3), and
doing so naturally at every edge, is still a separate obligation.  Once such
a lift is supplied, Theorem 3.1 performs all higher radical corrections.

## 5. Naturality gives all abelian refinements at once

Now put a subscript \(n\) on the data at every active abelian edge.  Suppose
the context maps, actual orbit recipes, modules, ideals, \(s_n\), and
\(\sigma_n\) commute with every matched refinement.  Assume that the same
leading identity (3.4) holds on the compatible actual defect subsystems
\(Z_n^{\rm act}\).

### Theorem 5.1 (COFINAL ACTUAL-ORBIT SELECTOR)

The homotopies

\[
 h_n=s_n\sigma_n\sum_{j\geq0}(1-B_n\sigma_n)^j
\tag{5.1}
\]

commute with refinement.  The corrections

\[
 c_n=-h_n(\beta_n)
\tag{5.2}
\]

therefore give the compatible value-level selector required in v99
equations (5.1)--(5.3).  If every \(s_n\) has the admissibility property stated after
Theorem 3.1, V98 materializes the successive values in the accumulated
transition kernels, and the resulting infinite product solves every abelian
chief refinement.  Without that property, (5.1)--(5.2) solve the relation
residuals only.

#### Proof

Refinement commutes with \(B_n,s_n,\sigma_n\), hence with
\(R_n=1-B_n\sigma_n\), every partial sum in (5.1), and its finite or complete
limit.  Thus it commutes with \(h_n\) and (5.2).  Theorem 3.1 kills the
defect at each edge.  Apply the nested word construction of v98. \(\square\)

The theorem is the precise infinite-refinement generalization of the
relative-dihedral correction.  It propagates a **natural leading splitting**,
not a bare success at one finite stage.

5. Check

   \[
   \overline B\,\overline\sigma

   \tag{7.1}
   \]

first nontrivial Hensel canary.  The paper promotion then proves that the
