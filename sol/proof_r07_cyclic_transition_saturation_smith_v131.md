# R07 cyclic transition saturation and Smith selector v131

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper proof.  This note identifies the v129 saturation kernel with
an exact Tor boundary and gives a closed coordinate test for a cyclic
group-ring refinement.  It also separates that refinement kernel from the
relative subgroup-cohomology kernel of v130.  The actual R07 Smith data and
actual error coordinates have not yet been computed.  No cofinal lift, fake,

## 1. The saturation kernel is a Tor boundary

Let \(\Lambda\) be a ring, let \(J\) be a two-sided ideal, and let

\[
 B:A\longrightarrow Z
\tag{1.1}
\]

be a homomorphism of left \(\Lambda\)-modules.  Fix \(z\in Z\), and put

\[
 M=M_z=B(A)+\Lambda z,\qquad Q=Z/M.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Regard \(\Lambda/J\) as a right \(\Lambda\)-module.  Applying
\((\Lambda/J)\otimes_\Lambda-\) to

\[
 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0
\tag{1.3}
\]

gives the exact segment

\[
 \operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\Lambda/J,Z)
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\Lambda/J,Q)
 \xrightarrow{\partial}
 M/JM\longrightarrow Z/JZ.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (TOR-SATURATION IDENTITY)

The v129 cyclic saturation kernel is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{M\cap JZ}{JM}
 =\operatorname{im}\partial
 \cong
 \frac{\operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\Lambda/J,Q)}
 {\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\Lambda/J,Z)}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

In particular, each of the following is sufficient for every actual error
in \(M\cap JZ\) to admit a v129 correction:

1. \(Q\) is Tor-acyclic for this reduction, meaning the middle Tor group in
   (1.4) is zero (ordinary \(\Lambda\)-flatness is sufficient);
2. the inclusion \(M\hookrightarrow Z\) remains injective after reduction
   modulo \(J\); or
3. (1.3) splits as a sequence of \(\Lambda\)-modules.

If \(Z\) is projective, then the first term of (1.4) is zero and the
saturation kernel is canonically the complete Tor group
\(\operatorname{Tor}_1^\Lambda(\Lambda/J,Q)\).

#### Proof

There are canonical identifications

\[
 (\Lambda/J)\otimes_\Lambda M=M/JM,
 \qquad
 (\Lambda/J)\otimes_\Lambda Z=Z/JZ.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The kernel of the last map in (1.4) is therefore

\[
 \ker(M/JM\to Z/JZ)=(M\cap JZ)/JM.
\tag{1.7}
\]

Exactness gives both equalities in (1.5).  The three sufficient conditions
make the last map injective directly, and projectivity of \(Z\) kills the
left Tor term. \(\square\)

This is a direct cochain-free interpretation of v129 (1.4).  For one R07
class, full vanishing of the Tor group is unnecessary: only the boundary
class represented by the named error has to vanish.

## 2. A cyclic group-ring transition is a chain-ring problem

Let \(k\) have characteristic \(p\), put \(q=p^a\), and write

\[
 P_a=\langle g\rangle\cong C_q,
 \qquad
 R_a=k[P_a].
\tag{2.1}
\]

With \(t=g-1\), the characteristic-\(p\) binomial identity gives

\[
 R_a\cong k[t]/(t^q).
\tag{2.2}
\]

For the quotient \(P_a\twoheadrightarrow P_{a-1}\) which sends \(g\) to a
chosen generator, put \(m=q/p\).  Its group-ring kernel is

\[
 \boxed{
 J_a=\ker(R_a\to R_{a-1})=(g^m-1)R_a=t^mR_a.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed, \(g^m-1=(g-1)^m\) because \(m\) is a power of \(p\), and the
quotient of (2.2) by \((t^m)\) is \(k[t]/(t^m)=R_{a-1}\).

Equation (2.3) is load-bearing.  The transition ideal in v129 is generally
not the augmentation ideal \((t)\).

## 3. Smith form of a submodule of a free chain-ring module

Let

\[
 R=k[t]/(t^q),\qquad J=(t^m),\qquad 1\leq m<q,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and suppose the actual residual ambient module is free,

\[
 Z=R^n.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (CHAIN-RING SUBMODULE FORM)

For every submodule \(M\leq R^n\), there is an \(R\)-basis
\(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) of \(Z\) and uniquely determined integers, after sorting,

\[
 0\leq\alpha_1\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_n\leq q
\tag{3.3}
\]

such that

\[
 \boxed{M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n t^{\alpha_i}R e_i,}
\tag{3.4}
\]

where \(t^qR=0\).

#### Proof

The ideals of \(R\) form the chain

\[
 R\supset tR\supset\cdots\supset t^qR=0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If \(M\ne0\), let \(t^\alpha R\) be the ideal generated by all coordinates
of all vectors of \(M\).  Choose a vector having a coordinate of valuation
\(\alpha\).  After factoring out \(t^\alpha\), its coordinate vector is
unimodular, so an invertible change of the ambient basis sends it to
\(t^\alpha e_1\).  Every first coordinate of every element of \(M\) is a
multiple of \(t^\alpha\); subtracting a suitable multiple of this selected
vector clears that coordinate.  Hence

\[
 M=t^\alpha R e_1\oplus M',
 \qquad M'\leq Re_2\oplus\cdots\oplus Re_n.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Induction on \(n\) gives (3.4).  The ideals occurring in the successive
quotients, equivalently the invariant factors of the inclusion, give the
sorted uniqueness. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (CYCLIC SATURATION SMITH FORM)

In the coordinates (3.4),

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{M\cap t^mZ}{t^mM}
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^n
 \frac{t^{\max(\alpha_i,m)}R}
 {t^{\min(q,\alpha_i+m)}R}.}
\tag{3.7}

Its \(k\)-dimension is

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=1}^n
 \left(
 \min(q,\alpha_i+m)-\max(\alpha_i,m)
 \right).}
\tag{3.8}

Every summand in (3.8) is nonnegative.  It is zero precisely when
\(\alpha_i=0\) or \(\alpha_i=q\).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 M\cap t^mZ=t^mM
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \alpha_i\in\{0,q\}\ \text{for every }i.}
\tag{3.9}

Equivalently, \(M\) is a direct summand spanned by a subset of an ambient
basis, or \(Z/M\) is free over \(R\).

#### Proof

On the \(i\)-th coordinate,

\[
 t^{\alpha_i}R\cap t^mR
 =t^{\max(\alpha_i,m)}R,
 \qquad
 t^m(t^{\alpha_i}R)
 =t^{\min(q,\alpha_i+m)}R.
\tag{3.10}
\]

This proves (3.7).  Since \(\dim_k t^rR=q-r\), taking dimensions gives
(3.8).  If \(0<\alpha_i<q\), then the summand has dimension

\[
 \begin{cases}
  \min(\alpha_i,q-m),&\alpha_i<m,\\
  \min(m,q-\alpha_i),&\alpha_i\geq m,
 \end{cases}
\tag{3.11}
\]

which is positive.  For \(\alpha_i=0\) or \(q\), it is zero.  This proves
(3.9).  The final equivalences follow immediately from (3.4). \(\square\)

Thus a full automatic transition theorem is very rigid: every proper
nonzero invariant factor creates some saturation classes.  This does not
mean the named R07 class is nonzero; it says that its coordinate must be
tested.

## 4. Closed selector for the one actual class

For \(v\in R\), use the truncated valuation

\[
 \nu(v)=\max\{r:v\in t^rR\},\qquad \nu(0)=q.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
 e=Ba_0-z\in M\cap t^mZ
\tag{4.2}
\]

be the actual lifted error, and write \(e=\sum_i e_i e_i^{\rm bas}\) in the
Smith ambient basis of Lemma 3.1.

### Theorem 4.1 (ACTUAL-CLASS VALUATION SELECTOR)

The v129 saturation class of \(e\) vanishes if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu(e_i)\geq\min(q,\alpha_i+m)
 \quad\text{for every }i.}
\tag{4.3}

When (4.3) holds, division by \(t^m\) in the diagonal coordinates returns
an explicit \(y\in M\) with

\[
 e=t^m y.
\tag{4.4}

Let

\[
 C:A\oplus R\longrightarrow M,
 \qquad C(a,\lambda)=Ba+\lambda z
\tag{4.5}

be the defining surjection, retained with word-bearing preimages.  Choose
\((a_1,\lambda_1)\) with \(C(a_1,\lambda_1)=y\), and set

\[
 \boxed{d=t^m a_1,\qquad \rho=t^m\lambda_1.}
\tag{4.6}

Then

\[
 \boxed{e=Bd+\rho z,\qquad d\in JA,\quad\rho\in J,}
\tag{4.7}

so v129 gives the exact next correction

\[
 \boxed{a=(1+\rho)^{-1}(a_0-d).}
\tag{4.8}

If (4.3) fails, the first failed diagonal coordinate, followed by projection
to the quotient in (3.7), is an exact separating certificate for this typed
module and this actual error.

#### Proof

By (3.7), the class of \(e\) is zero exactly when its \(i\)-th coordinate
lies in \(t^{\min(q,\alpha_i+m)}R\), which is (4.3).  In that case the
coordinatewise quotient by \(t^m\) lies in \(t^{\alpha_i}R\), hence the
resulting vector \(y\) lies in \(M\) and satisfies (4.4).  Surjectivity of
(4.5) gives \((a_1,\lambda_1)\).  Multiplying (4.5) by \(t^m\) proves
(4.7), and v129 Theorem 2.1 proves (4.8).  A failed coordinate is nonzero in
its displayed quotient summand, so it supplies the stated separator.
\(\square\)

This is a uniform finite selector: the same Smith reduction, valuation test,
and division rule works for every cyclic scale.  It proves solvability at a
given scale exactly when the actual coordinates pass (4.3); it does not hide
a failed stage behind an unbounded word search.

## 5. The procyclic specialization

For the edge

\[
 k[C_{p^a}]\twoheadrightarrow k[C_{p^{a-1}}],
\tag{5.1}
\]

substitute

\[
 q=p^a,qquad m=p^{a-1}.
\tag{5.2}

The complete saturation dimension and actual-class threshold are therefore

\[
 \dim_k K_a
 =\sum_i
 \bigl(
 \min(p^a,\alpha_i+p^{a-1})
 -\max(\alpha_i,p^{a-1})
 \bigr),
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
 \nu(e_i)\geq
 \min(p^a,\alpha_i+p^{a-1}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

If every actual quotient \(Z_a/M_a\) is free over \(k[C_{p^a}]\), all
cyclic transition edges are intrinsically cartesian and v129 Theorem 5.1
constructs the compatible cofinal lift with no further membership searches.
Without that freeness, (5.4) is still a closed class-specific selector at
every edge.  Sequential compatibility is automatic because (4.8) is built
as a lift of the already selected coarse solution.

For a nonfree ambient \(Z_a\), Theorem 1.1 remains valid and a finite
presentation computes the Tor boundary or, equivalently, the augmented
v129 system directly.  The Smith formulas above must not be applied by first
embedding \(Z_a\) in a larger free module unless the induced map on the
saturation kernel is also proved injective.

## 6. Separation from the v130 relative H2 kernel

V130 studies the subgroup-restriction kernel, with
\(C_{p^{a-1}}=\langle g^p\rangle\) included as a subgroup,

\[
 \ker\bigl(
 H^2(C_{p^a},V)\to H^2(C_{p^{a-1}},V)
 \bigr),
\tag{6.1}
\]

using the augmentation operator \(t=g-1\).  Its denominator is
\(t^{p^a-1}V\), and only a short near-regular Jordan interval can occur.

The direct context-changing problem of v129 instead uses the quotient map
\(C_{p^a}\twoheadrightarrow C_{p^{a-1}}\), sending \(g\) to a generator,
and reduces the group ring along (5.1).  Its kernel is

\[
 J_a=(t^{p^{a-1}}),
\tag{6.2}
\]

and asks for the saturation quotient

\[
 (M_a\cap t^{p^{a-1}}Z_a)/t^{p^{a-1}}M_a.
\tag{6.3}
\]

These are different functors.  In particular, the fact that a regular
permutation block is safe for (6.1) does not by itself make an arbitrary
submodule inclusion saturated in (6.3).  V130 applies to R07 only after its
displayed cochain-level comparison (5.1) has been constructed.  Theorems
1.1 and 4.1 of this note apply directly to the v129 transition and require
no identification of the roof \(C_9\) power with the printed five-coface
A.18 rho operation.

This gives two sound and complementary all-stage lanes:

1. a genuine relative-cohomology occurrence may use the v130 Jordan
   selector; and
2. the actual group-ring refinement may always use the v131 Tor/Smith
   selector on its named saturation error.

Neither lane alone proves that every R07 actual class vanishes.
