# R07 relative based Nakayama transition v126

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper theorem.  It upgrades v124 from one fixed Jennings ring to a
single context-changing nilpotent transition and identifies the exact
transverse obstruction.  It does not assert that every R07 transition is
cartesian, does not solve a nonabelian chief edge, and does not declare a
fake or Ihara witness.

## 1. The question left by the fixed-context formula

V124 proves that, in one fixed filtered context, a word-bearing based lift
whose error has coordinates in the actual target free cover is corrected by

\[
 s=s_0(1+R)^{-1}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For the fixed ring \(\mathbf F_3[\Pi_4[3]]\), a depth-nine error has
\(R^4=0\), so (1.1) is the four-term formula

\[
 s=s_0(1-R+R^2-R^3).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The remaining issue is not compatibility of this polynomial: polynomials
automatically commute with reduction.  The issue is whether a coarse based
lift can be lifted to the finer coefficient module with an error which has
coordinates in the finer actual target.  This note isolates that issue.

## 2. One nilpotent transition

Let

\[
 \pi:\Lambda'\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda,
 \qquad J=\ker\pi,
 \qquad J^L=0
\tag{2.1}
\]

be a surjection of rings.  Let \(A',Z'\) be left \(\Lambda'\)-modules with
reductions \(A,Z\), and let

\[
 B':A'\longrightarrow Z',
 \qquad B:A\longrightarrow Z
\tag{2.2}
\]

commute with reduction.  Put

\[
 F'=(\Lambda')^t,
 \qquad F=\Lambda^t,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and let \(q':F'\twoheadrightarrow Z'_{\rm act}\subseteq Z'\) be a named
free cover reducing to \(q:F\twoheadrightarrow Z_{\rm act}\).  In the R07
application, basis elements of \(A'\) carry actual common correction words,
and the columns of \(q'\) are named generators of the all-seven actual
defect subsystem.

Assume a coarse based lift has already been constructed:

\[
 s:F\longrightarrow A,
 \qquad Bs=q.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Write

\[
 K_A=\ker(A'\to A),
 \qquad K_Z=\ker(Z'\to Z).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Choose any \(\Lambda'\)-linear lift \(\widehat s:F'\to A'\) of \(s\).  Its
transition error is

\[
 e_{\widehat s}=B'\widehat s-q':F'\longrightarrow K_Z.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Changing \(\widehat s\) by a map into \(K_A\) changes (2.6) by a map into
\(B'(K_A)\).  The based-coordinate correction in (1.1) can absorb a map
into \(q'(JF')\).  Hence the exact new obstruction target is

\[
 \boxed{
 T_\pi=K_Z/\bigl(B'(K_A)+q'(JF')\bigr).}
\tag{2.7}
\]

The class of (2.6) in \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda'}(F',T_\pi)\) is
independent of the chosen lift \(\widehat s\).  Call it
\(\omega_\pi(s)\).

## 3. Relative based transition theorem

### Theorem 3.1 (RELATIVE-BASED-NAKAYAMA)

Suppose \(A'\to A\) is onto and

\[
 \boxed{\omega_\pi(s)=0.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then there are maps

\[
 \delta:F'\longrightarrow K_A,
 \qquad R:F'\longrightarrow JF'
\tag{3.2}
\]

such that, for \(s_0=\widehat s-\delta\),

\[
 B's_0=q'(1+R).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 s'=s_0(1+R)^{-1}
    =s_0\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}(-R)^m}
\tag{3.4}
\]

satisfies

\[
 B's'=q',
 \qquad s'\bmod J=s.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Every coefficient in (3.4) is a finite \(\Lambda'\)-linear combination of
the same word-bearing columns used by \(s_0\).  Thus (3.4) is an explicit
finer based lift whenever \(\delta\) and \(R\) are returned with column
provenance.

#### Proof

Because \(F'\) is free and \(A'\to A\) is onto, a lift \(\widehat s\)
exists.  The equality \(Bs=q\) implies (2.6).  Condition (3.1), applied to
the finitely many basis vectors of \(F'\), says precisely that one may write

\[
 e_{\widehat s}=B'\delta+q'R
\tag{3.6}
\]

with \(\delta(F')\subseteq K_A\) and \(R(F')\subseteq JF'\).  Replacing
\(\widehat s\) by \(s_0=\widehat s-\delta\) gives (3.3).

Inductively,

\[
 R^m(F')\subseteq J^mF',
\tag{3.7}
\]

so \(R^L=0\).  The finite geometric series in (3.4) is therefore the
two-sided inverse of \(1+R\).  Equation (3.3) gives

\[
 B's'=q'(1+R)(1+R)^{-1}=q'.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Both \(\delta\) and \(R\) reduce to zero, so (3.5) follows.  The final
word-bearing assertion is immediate from the finite expression (3.4).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (cartesian transition)

If

\[
 K_Z=q'(JF'),
\tag{3.9}
\]

then \(T_\pi=0\), so every coarse based lift has an explicit finer lift.
More generally it is enough that

\[
 K_Z=B'(K_A)+q'(JF').
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus a context change introduces no new search once its kernel residuals
are generated by old coefficient-kernel columns together with the named
target coordinates.  The only genuinely new abelian information is the
quotient (2.7).

## 4. Cyclic actual-class form

Let \(t=1\), let \(q'(1)=z'\), and let \(a\in A\) satisfy \(Ba=z\).  Lift
\(a\) arbitrarily to \(\widehat a\in A'\).  The transition obstruction is
the class of

\[
 B'\widehat a-z'\in K_Z
\tag{4.1}
\]

modulo \(B'(K_A)+Jz'\).  If it vanishes, choose \(d\in K_A\) and
\(\rho\in J\) with

\[
 B'(\widehat a-d)=(1+\rho)z'.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{a'=(1+\rho)^{-1}(\widehat a-d),\qquad B'a'=z'.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This makes the distinction from the v111 annihilator test explicit.
Equation (4.3) lifts one named class; it does not define a
\(\Lambda'\)-linear splitter on the quotient cyclic module
\(\Lambda'z'\) unless the annihilator relations are also respected.

## 5. Normal p-context kernels

Let \(k=\mathbf F_p\), let \(\Delta'\twoheadrightarrow\Delta\) have finite
normal p-group kernel \(N\), and let

\[
 \Lambda'=k[\Delta'],\qquad\Lambda=k[\Delta].
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
 J=\ker(k[\Delta']\to k[\Delta])
   =k[\Delta']I(N)
\tag{5.2}
\]

is nilpotent.  Indeed, normality of \(N\) makes the right side a two-sided
ideal and

\[
 J^m=k[\Delta']I(N)^m.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The augmentation ideal \(I(N)\) of a finite p-group over \(\mathbf F_p\)
is nilpotent, hence so is \(J\).  Therefore every cartesian normal
p-context refinement is covered by Theorem 3.1.  A Jennings bound for
\(I(N)\) gives an explicit truncation bound in (3.4).

This observation does not say that the actual R07 defect subsystem is
cartesian.  It says that, after cartesianness or the weaker equality (3.10)
has been proved, no additional compatibility choice or depth-by-depth
search remains at that transition.

## 6. Compatible inverse systems

Consider a nested inverse system in which every abelian context transition
has the form (2.1)--(2.3).  Fix deterministic word-bearing lifts of source
columns and deterministic preimage routines for (3.6).  Starting from one
based lift \(s_0\), apply (3.4) successively.

### Theorem 6.1 (NILPOTENT-TRANSITION TOWER)

If \(\omega_{\pi_n}(s_n)=0\) at every transition, the maps produced by
Theorem 3.1 form a compatible family

\[
 s_{n+1}\longmapsto s_n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

If the system is complete and separated, they define a continuous based
inverse-limit lift.  If every transition is cartesian, the single coarse
based lift is the only membership computation: all later abelian lifts are
obtained by the finite formulas (3.4).

#### Proof

Equation (3.5) gives (6.1) at each step.  Compatibility is therefore
inductive, and completeness gives the inverse-limit map.  In the cartesian
case Corollary 3.2 makes every obstruction vanish. \(\square\)

The theorem does not cover a nonabelian chief edge: there is then no
nilpotent additive coordinate matrix \(R\), and the finite nonlinear
accepted set remains a separate gate.

## 7. Exact R07 consequence and next certificate

For the current fixed \(\Pi_4[3]\) context, v124 already proves the stronger
depth-nine four-term formula (1.2).  The new conclusion is that the same
based mechanism crosses a later abelian context refinement as soon as the
finite transition quotient (2.7) vanishes on the named all-seven generator
tuple.

Accordingly, a positive successor to task175 should retain:

1. a finite free cover \(q:F\to Z_{\rm act}\) of the named complete stacked
   defect subsystem;
2. the word-bearing coarse lift \(s\);
3. at a context-changing canary edge, exact bases for \(K_A,K_Z,q'(JF')\)
   and \(B'(K_A)\);
4. the transverse obstruction coordinates (2.6)--(2.7); and
5. when they vanish, the actual \(\delta,R\) and the materialized polynomial
   (3.4).

This is strictly weaker than constructing an orbit-wide annihilator
splitter and strictly stronger than finding unrelated solutions at two
levels.  It is the shortest exact test of whether the explicit lift really
propagates across the next abelian context change.
