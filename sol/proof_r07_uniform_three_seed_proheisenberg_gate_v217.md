# R07 uniform three-seed pro-Heisenberg endpoint gate v217

Author: Sol / 2026-08-28

Status: paper theorem extending the single-seed first-edge refinement v216 to
every relative pro-3 Frattini edge.  V213 bounds the one-power-ahead

## 1. One arbitrary relative Frattini edge

Put \(k=\mathbf F_3\).  Retain v213--v214's correctly typed edge

\[
 F(x,y)\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_{m+1}
 \stackrel{p_{m+1}}\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_m,
 \qquad K_m=\ker p_{m+1},
\tag{1.1}
\]

and its matching one-power-ahead quotient

\[
 q_{m+1}:\Delta_{m+1}\twoheadrightarrow
 D_{m+1}\cong\mathcal H_2(3^{m+2}).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Set

\[
 R_m=q_{m+1}(K_m)
 \leq L_m=\ker(D_{m+1}\to D_m).
\tag{1.3}
\]

V213 Theorem 5.1 gives

\[
 R_m\cong(C_3)^{a_m},\qquad 0\leq a_m\leq3.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The subgroup \(R_m\) is normal in \(D_{m+1}\), because \(K_m\) is normal in
\(\Delta_{m+1}\) and \(q_{m+1}\) is onto.  Write

\[
 A_{m+1}=k[D_{m+1}],\qquad
 I(R_m)=\ker\bigl(A_{m+1}\to k[D_{m+1}/R_m]\bigr).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Choose an ordered \(\mathbf F_3\)-basis
\(r_{m,1},\ldots,r_{m,a_m}\) of \(R_m\).  When \(a_m=0\), every sum indexed
by this basis is zero.

## 2. A normal elementary-abelian defect needs at most three generators

### Lemma 2.1 (UNIFORM IDEAL GENERATORS)

\[
 \boxed{
 I(R_m)=
 \sum_{i=1}^{a_m} A_{m+1}(r_{m,i}-1).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus the relative ideal has at most three left-ideal generators, independently
of \(m\) and independently of its vector-space dimension.

#### Proof

For any normal subgroup \(R\triangleleft D\), the kernel of
\(k[D]\to k[D/R]\) is the two-sided ideal generated by \(r-1\),
\(r\in R\).  The identities

\[
 ab-1=(a-1)+a(b-1),\qquad
 a^{-1}-1=-a^{-1}(a-1)
\tag{2.2}
\]

show that \(r-1\) for every word in the basis elements belongs to the left
ideal

\[
 L=\sum_i A_{m+1}(r_{m,i}-1).
\tag{2.2a}
\]

For \(g\in D_{m+1}\) and \(r\in R_m\),

\[
 (r-1)g=g(g^{-1}rg-1)\in L,
\tag{2.2b}
\]

because normality gives \(g^{-1}rg\in R_m\), and (2.2) puts its difference
from one in \(L\).  Thus \(L\) is stable under right multiplication by every
group-basis element, and hence is a two-sided ideal.  It contains all standard
generators of \(I(R_m)\), while \(L\subseteq I(R_m)\).  Therefore
\(L=I(R_m)\). \(\square\)

For a retained left transversal \(T_m\) of \(R_m\) in \(D_{m+1}\), an
explicit vector-space basis is

\[
 \left\{
 t\prod_{i=1}^{a_m}(r_{m,i}-1)^{e_i}:
 t\in T_m,\quad
 (e_1,\ldots,e_{a_m})\in\{0,1,2\}^{a_m}\setminus\{0\}
 \right\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 k[R_m]\cong
 k[T_1,\ldots,T_{a_m}]/(T_1^3,\ldots,T_{a_m}^3),
 \qquad T_i=r_{m,i}-1,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and \(k[D_{m+1}]=\bigoplus_{t\in T_m}t\,k[R_m]\) as a vector space.  Hence
(2.3) has

\[
 |D_{m+1}/R_m|(3^{a_m}-1)
 =|D_{m+1}|-|D_{m+1}/R_m|
\tag{2.5}
\]

members, agreeing with v214 equation (3.5).

## 3. The complete projected endpoint from at most three seeds

Let \(\widehat E_{m+1}\), \(w_m\), the occurrence-wise twisted action, the
block map \(C_m\), and the affine residual
\(\bar\epsilon_{m+1}^{\,\widetilde\mu_m}\) be those of v214.  Define

\[
 u_{m,i}=(r_{m,i}-1)\mathbin\odot w_m
 \in\widehat E_{m+1}
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq a_m).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(U_m\) be the smallest \(k\)-subspace containing these seeds and stable
under the four marked actions \(x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}\).

### Theorem 3.1 (UNIFORM THREE-SEED PRE-GATE)

\[
 \boxed{
 U_m=I(R_m)\mathbin\odot w_m
 =\sum_{i=1}^{a_m}A_{m+1}\mathbin\odot u_{m,i}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently v214's complete projected pre-gate is

\[
 \boxed{
 \bar\epsilon_{m+1}^{\,\widetilde\mu_m}
 \in C_m(U_m),}
\tag{3.3}
\]

and its invariant closure starts from at most three rows.  If \(a_m=0\), the
gate is simply the equality
\(\bar\epsilon_{m+1}^{\,\widetilde\mu_m}=0\).

#### Proof

The marked elements \(x,y\) generate \(D_{m+1}\), so the smallest stable
subspace containing the seeds is their \(A_{m+1}\)-span.  Associativity of the
occurrence action and Lemma 2.1 give

\[
 \sum_i A_{m+1}\mathbin\odot
       \bigl((r_{m,i}-1)\mathbin\odot w_m\bigr)
 =\left(\sum_iA_{m+1}(r_{m,i}-1)\right)\mathbin\odot w_m
 =I(R_m)\mathbin\odot w_m.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equation (3.3) is exactly v214 equation (3.12). \(\square\)

The closure must occur in \(\widehat E_{m+1}\) before applying \(C_m\).
The block map is only \(k\)-linear; no common block action or equivariance is
introduced.

The action map from \(I(R_m)\) to \(\widehat E_{m+1}\) is linear, so

\[
 \dim_k U_m\leq\dim_k I(R_m)
 =|D_{m+1}|-|D_{m+1}/R_m|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The queue therefore has at most this many rank increases.  Memory and
arithmetic work still depend on the sparse row representation and elimination
method; no rung-independent complexity bound is claimed.

## 4. At most three endpoint-bearing seeds in the joint gate

V213 gives a surjective module map

\[
 \lambda_m:K_m\twoheadrightarrow R_m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

After the word-bearing kernel closure at this edge, row-reduce its literal
three-coordinate one-power-ahead values.  Choose a word-bearing adapted basis

\[
 s_{m,1},\ldots,s_{m,a_m},\,
 k_{m,a_m+1},\ldots,k_{m,t_m}
\tag{4.2}
\]

such that

\[
 \lambda_m(s_{m,i})=r_{m,i},\qquad
 \lambda_m(k_{m,j})=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the actual pointed row \(d_{m+1}\), put

\[
 v_{m,i}=
 \bigl((s_{m,i}-1)d_{m+1},u_{m,i}\bigr)
 \quad(1\leq i\leq a_m),
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
 v_{m,j}=
 \bigl((k_{m,j}-1)d_{m+1},0\bigr)
 \quad(a_m<j\leq t_m).
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (UNIFORM ADAPTED JOINT SPAN)

Let \(\widehat W_m\subseteq
Z_{m+1}\oplus\widehat E_{m+1}\) be the occurrence-space span and let
\(\mathcal T_m(z,v)=(z,C_m(v))\).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_m
 &=k[\Delta_{m+1}]
   \langle v_{m,1},\ldots,v_{m,t_m}\rangle\\
 &=\{(\kappa d_{m+1},\kappa\mathbin\odot w_m):
       \kappa\in I_m\},\\
 W_m=\mathcal T_m(\widehat W_m)
 &=\{(\kappa d_{m+1},C_m(\kappa\mathbin\odot w_m)):
       \kappa\in I_m\}.
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{4.6}
\]

At most \(a_m\leq3\) of the displayed seeds have a potentially nonzero
endpoint coordinate.  Every basis vector of \(\ker\lambda_m\) affects only
the pointed coordinate.

#### Proof

The adapted basis generates \(K_m\), so normality and (2.2) give

\[
 I_m=k[\Delta_{m+1}]
 \langle s_{m,i}-1,k_{m,j}-1\rangle.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The endpoint action factors through \(q_{m+1}\), whose restriction to \(K_m\)
is \(\lambda_m\).  Equations (4.3) therefore give the endpoint coordinates
in (4.4)--(4.5).  Substitution into v214 equations (4.7)--(4.13) proves the
two occurrence-space equalities in (4.6); applying \(\mathcal T_m\) after
closure proves the block-space equality. \(\square\)

## 5. Canonical finite-stage choice and all-rung use

The defect layer \(L_m\) has three normalized Malcev coordinates: the two
degree-one power coordinates and the commutator-power coordinate, divided by
their common \(3^{m+1}\) factor and reduced modulo three.  A deterministic
finite-stage implementation may therefore:

1. compute \(R_m\) as the exhausted
   \(\mathbf F_3[\Delta_m]\)-orbit span of the literal relator-defect vectors
   under the exact conjugation action on the three normalized Malcev
   coordinates, as in v213 equation (6.3), retaining word ancestry;
2. take the terminal subspace's reduced-row-echelon basis in the fixed order
   \(x,y,[x,y]\);
3. retain the lexicographically first word ancestry for every pivot vector;
4. use the resulting at-most-three seeds in Theorem 3.1; and
5. adapt the full word-bearing \(K_m\)-basis to the same pivots for
   Theorem 4.1.

This is one algorithm valid at every edge.  It removes an exponentially large
input roster and does not make unrelated arbitrary section choices.  It does
not assert that the pivot bases at distinct edges themselves form a compatible
inverse system.

For compatibility, fix the lower/root coefficient and the named roof/row
branch.  Let \(\mathcal S_n\) be the finite set of projected solution histories
\((\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_n)\) which satisfy every gate and literal reduction through
rung \(n\).  Join each history to its truncation and, if needed, adjoin one
formal root below the finite level-zero set.  Every \(\Delta_i\) and
\(k[\Delta_i]\) is finite, so every level is finite and the union is a rooted
finitely branching tree.  If \(\mathcal S_n\neq\varnothing\) for every \(n\),
Koenig's lemma gives an infinite compatible projected branch.

A single positive edge does not imply this hypothesis, and a negative edge
for one lower coefficient does not exclude other lower histories unless the
complete depth-\(n\) tree has been exhausted.

Thus the theorem supplies the promised all-rung finite compiler for the
matching pro-Heisenberg projected gate.  This is a necessary screen, not the
full return-even/full-\(P_0\) homotopy.  A closed-form compatible selector
would add a proof that the relevant child map is always nonempty (or a natural
contracting homotopy); that statement is not smuggled into the generator
bound.
