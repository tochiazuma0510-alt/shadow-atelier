## 2. A finite state group with an exact source action

Let \(E_{\exp}=\mathbf F_3^2\), and form the finite group

\[
 \mathfrak G=U_0^{+}\times Q_1\times E_{\exp}^{+},
\tag{2.1}
\]

where the first and third factors are additive and the middle factor retains
its actual nonabelian multiplication.  On \(\Omega\), define

\[
 \widehat\Psi(c)=\bigl(J_0(c),\Psi_1(c),\exp(c)\bigr).
\tag{2.2}
\]

By (1.4), \(\Psi_1\)'s homomorphism property and exponent additivity,

\[
 \widehat\Psi:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathfrak G
\tag{2.3}
\]

is a group homomorphism.  It is not asserted to extend with the same direct-
product law to all of \(F\).

For \(s\in F\), let \(\bar\sigma_{o,0}(s)\) be its roof value in occurrence
\(o\).  Define

\[
 \alpha_s(v,q,e)=
 \left(
   \bigl(\bar\sigma_{o,0}(s)v_o\bigr)_o,
   \Psi_1(s)q\Psi_1(s)^{-1},
   e
 \right).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The first coordinate uses the genuine blockwise left actions; it is not one
action on a prematurely aggregated column.  Each \(\alpha_s\) is an
automorphism of \(\mathfrak G\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat\Psi(scs^{-1})=\alpha_s(\widehat\Psi(c))
 \quad(s\in F,\ c\in\Omega).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

For one occurrence, put \(u=\sigma_o(s)\) and \(w=\sigma_o(c)\).  Since
\(w\) has roof value one,

\[
 \delta_0(uwu^{-1})
 =\delta_0(u)+\bar u\delta_0(w)
   +\bar u\delta_0(u^{-1})
 =\bar u\delta_0(w).
\tag{2.6}
\]

The next-rung affine representation is a homomorphism, hence sends the same
word to \(\psi_{o,1}(s)\psi_{o,1}(c)\psi_{o,1}(s)^{-1}\).  Conjugation does
not change exponent sums.  Taking the typed product proves (2.5).
\(\square\)

## 3. Infinite normal words collapse to one finite closure

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the smallest subgroup of \(\mathfrak G\) which contains

\[
 \{\widehat\Psi(r):r\in\mathcal R_\Omega\}
\tag{3.1}
\]

and is invariant under \(\alpha_x,\alpha_{x^{-1}},\alpha_y,
\alpha_{y^{-1}}\).

### Theorem 3.1 (FINITE NORMAL-CLOSURE IMAGE)

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C=\widehat\Psi(\Omega).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Moreover \(\mathcal C\) is computable by a terminating finite closure.  Every
retained state can carry a finite literal ancestry DAG built only from:

```text
LEAF(r), PRODUCT(a,b), INVERSE(a), CONJUGATE(x,a), CONJUGATE(y,a).
```

Expanding that DAG gives a literal word in \(\Omega\) realizing the state.

#### Proof

The right side of (3.2) is a subgroup by (2.3), contains (3.1), and is
invariant under the four source actions by (2.5).  Hence
\(\mathcal C\subseteq\widehat\Psi(\Omega)\).

Conversely, every element of the normal closure (1.1) is a finite product of
inverses of conjugates \(uru^{-1}\), with
\(r\in\mathcal R_\Omega\) and \(u\) a word in \(x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}\).
Repeated use of (2.5), subgroup multiplication and inversion places its state
in \(\mathcal C\).  This proves the reverse inclusion.

The ambient group (2.1) is finite.  Start with the subgroup generated by
(3.1), add the images under \(\alpha_x^{\pm1},\alpha_y^{\pm1}\), regenerate
the subgroup, and repeat.  A strict step enlarges a finite subgroup, so the
process terminates at the required least invariant subgroup.  Recording the
operation which first produced each generator/state gives the ancestry DAG.
\(\square\)

This is stronger than a fair enumeration of infinitely many source words.
It gives a complete finite decision at the fixed pair of rungs.  It need not
be small: a resource-bounded unfinished closure is still
`UNKNOWN_RESOURCE`, not a negative result.

## 5. Positive and negative certificates

A witness-first producer need not finish \(\mathcal C\).  On reaching a
state satisfying (4.11), it emits:

1. the literal/DAG A0 ancestry and all normal-generator/conjugation nodes;
2. every occurrence-level roof Fox state and its task175 aggregation;
3. the A0 boundary coefficients and exact exponent replay;
4. every next-rung affine occurrence state and direct printed-order
   reconstruction of \(\mathscr D_1(gc)\);
5. the accepted A3/A4/\(\kappa_0\) identities;
6. the slice coefficients \(\theta\) and direct equality (4.12); and
7. \(\mu_1\), with both its endpoint and action equations.

An independent positive checker replays only this finite ancestry and the two
memberships; it does not need to trust or reproduce the producer's search
order.

A complete negative is stronger and may be expensive.  It must authenticate
the final invariant subgroup \(\mathcal C\), prove closure under subgroup
operations and all four source actions, prove that it contains every base
row state, and exhaust the coset (4.5) or its distinct projection (4.14)
against the exact A5 predicate.  A cap, missing state shard or unclosed action
returns UNKNOWN.
