# R07 graded transverse homotopy ladder v127

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: paper theorem.  It turns the one-shot transverse obstruction of v126
into a finite, graded, constructive test and states the exact compatibility
condition needed at an inverse limit.  It does not assert that the R07
field-outer classes pass this test.  No cofinal lift, fake, or Ihara witness

## 1. Why the one-shot quotient is not yet a selector

Use the notation of v126.  Thus

\[
 \pi:\Lambda'\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda,
 \qquad J=\ker\pi,
 \qquad J^L=0,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 B':A'\longrightarrow Z',
 \qquad q':F'\twoheadrightarrow Z'_{\rm act},
 \qquad F'=(\Lambda')^t.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Let (s:F\to A) be a coarse based lift, choose a lift
\(\widehat s:F'\to A'\), and put

\[
 e_1=B'\widehat s-q'.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For the strict transition considered here assume

\[
 \ker(A'\to A)=JA',
 \qquad
 \ker(Z'\to Z)=JZ'.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Then (e_1(F')\subseteq JZ').  V126 says that the lift exists exactly when
this error lies in

\[
 B'\operatorname{Hom}(F',JA')
 +q'\operatorname{Hom}(F',JF').
\tag{1.5}
\]

Formula (1.5) is exact but does not by itself say how to find the two
preimages.  The point of this note is that nilpotence splits this single
membership question into finitely many linear graded questions.

## 2. The relative correction complex

For (r\geq1), define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M^r
  &=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda'}(F',J^rA')
    \oplus
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda'}(F',J^rF'),\\
 \mathcal N^r
  &=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda'}(F',J^rZ'),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal C_r:\mathcal M^r\longrightarrow\mathcal N^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_r(\delta,R)=B'\delta+q'R.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The two-sidedness of (J) gives

\[
 \mathcal M^{r+1}\subseteq\mathcal M^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal N^{r+1}\subseteq\mathcal N^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_r(\mathcal M^{r+1})
 \subseteq\mathcal N^{r+1}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence (2.2) induces the graded map

\[
 \operatorname{gr}^r\mathcal C:
 \mathcal M^r/\mathcal M^{r+1}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal N^r/\mathcal N^{r+1}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For a residual (e_r\in\mathcal N^r), its (r)-th transverse class is

\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_r(e_r)=
 [e_r]\in
 \mathcal O_r:=
 \frac{\mathcal N^r}
      {\mathcal C_r(\mathcal M^r)+\mathcal N^{r+1}}.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Only the subspace generated by the actual columns of (e_r) has to be
tested.  Surjectivity of (2.4) on the whole ambient module is not required.

## 3. Finite graded lifting theorem

### Theorem 3.1 (GRADED TRANSVERSE LIFT)

Starting with (e_1) in (1.3), perform the following recursion.  If
\(\omega_r(e_r)=0\), choose

\[
 (\delta_r,R_r)\in\mathcal M^r
\tag{3.1}
\]

such that

\[
 e_{r+1}:=e_r-\mathcal C_r(\delta_r,R_r)
 \in\mathcal N^{r+1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then the following are equivalent.

1. The v126 transverse obstruction of (s) vanishes.
2. There is a choice in (3.1) for every (r=1,\ldots,L-1).
3. There are
   \(\delta:F'\to JA'\) and (R:F'\to JF'\) satisfying

\[
 e_1=B'\delta+q'R.
\tag{3.3}
\]

When these conditions hold, one may take

\[
 \delta=\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}\delta_r,
 \qquad
 R=\sum_{r=1}^{L-1}R_r,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and the explicit finer lift is

\[
 \boxed{
 s'=(\widehat s-\delta)(1+R)^{-1}
    =(\widehat s-\delta)\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}(-R)^m.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

It satisfies (B's'=q') and reduces to (s).

#### Proof

If (3.1)--(3.2) can be performed through (r=L-1), telescoping gives

\[
 e_L=e_1-B'\sum_r\delta_r-q'\sum_rR_r.
\tag{3.6}
\]

But (e_L\in\mathcal N^L=0), which proves (3.3).  Conversely, suppose
(3.3) holds.  Taking its leading class modulo (J^2Z') proves
\(\omega_1(e_1)=0\).  Choose the leading graded parts of (delta,R),
subtract them, and repeat modulo (J^{r+1}Z').  This supplies (3.1) at
every grade.  Thus the three conditions are equivalent.  Formula (3.5) is
then v126 Theorem 3.1; (R^L=0) makes the displayed inverse finite.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (first-failure certificate)

For a fixed chain of choices through grade (r_0-1), a nonzero class
\(\omega_{r_0}(e_{r_0})\) proves that this chain cannot be continued by a
grade-(r_0) correction.  At a secondary grade the residual can depend on
the earlier preimages, so a negative result for the whole registered module
requires exhausting the affine family of admissible earlier choices and
showing that every resulting grade-(r_0) class is nonzero.  Even that is
only a local negative result for the fixed correction roster and target
cover; it is not a nonexistence theorem after either is enlarged.

## 4. A closed actual-class selector

Suppose that, on the actual residual space reached by the recursion, one has
chosen graded right inverses

\[
 h_r:\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{gr}^r\mathcal C)_{\rm act}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathcal M^r/\mathcal M^{r+1},
 \qquad
 (\operatorname{gr}^r\mathcal C)h_r=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Fix deterministic lifts of the values of (h_r) to (mathcal M^r).
Equations (3.1)--(3.2) with

\[
 (\delta_r,R_r)=h_r([e_r])
\tag{4.2}
\]

are then a closed finite selector.  No search over unrelated finite-stage
solutions remains.  Crucially, (4.1) is needed only on the successive
actual-class orbit generated by (e_1), not on every vector of
\(\mathcal N^r/\mathcal N^{r+1}).

This is the precise role of the relative-dihedral idea.  If the actual
graded residual decomposes as

\[
 V_r^{\rm act}=V_r^-\oplus V_r^+,
\tag{4.3}
\]

where the return involution is odd on (V_r^-) and even on (V_r^+), one
may use

\[
 h_r=h_r^{\rm dih}\oplus h_r^{\rm out}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The dihedral antisymmetrizer supplies (h_r^{\rm dih}) when its usual
odd-part hypotheses hold.  The field-outer/full-(P_0) calculation must
supply (h_r^{\rm out}) on the actual even survivor.  A pure
\(1-\theta\) construction cannot supply the second summand.  Conversely,
one does not need a classification or splitting of the entire ambient
field-outer module if the orbit of the actual R07 residual spans a smaller
subspace.

## 5. Compatible inverse limits

Let the transition above occur at every edge (n+1\to n) of a cofinal
inverse system.  At edge (n), denote the graded selectors by (h_{n,r}).
Assume:

1. the initial word-bearing lifts commute with reduction;
2. the actual residual spaces commute with reduction; and
3. the chosen graded selectors and their representative lifts commute with
   reduction on those actual residual spaces.

### Theorem 5.1 (RELATIVE HOMOTOPY TOWER)

Under these three assumptions, applying (4.2) and then (3.5) at every edge
produces a compatible family of based lifts

\[
 s_{n+1}\longmapsto s_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

In the complete separated inverse limit this family defines one continuous
explicit lift.  Thus the compatibility problem for the corrections
\((c_n)\) is exactly the naturality of the actual-class selectors
\(h_{n,r}\), not an additional choice made after solving every finite
stage.

#### Proof

By assumption, reduction sends (e_{n+1,r}) to (e_{n,r}).  Naturality of
(h_{n,r}) therefore sends ((\delta_{n+1,r},R_{n+1,r})) to
\((\delta_{n,r},R_{n,r})\), and induction in (r) gives compatible sums
\(\delta_n,R_n\).  The inverse polynomial in (3.5) is functorial under ring
homomorphisms, so the resulting (s_n) satisfy (5.1).  Completeness gives
the inverse-limit map. \(\square\)

Pointwise vanishing of every finite obstruction without compatible choices
does not by itself prove Theorem 5.1.  A deterministic formula which happens
to commute with reduction, such as a dihedral antisymmetrizer, is valuable
precisely because it supplies condition 3 automatically on its summand.
