## 1. The relative ideal in the full group algebra

Let (k=\mathbf F_p), and let

\[
 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow \Delta _1
 \stackrel{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\Delta _0\longrightarrow1
\tag{1.1}
\]

be an extension of finite groups with
(K\cong(C_p)^t).  Put

\[
 \Lambda=k[\Delta _1],\qquad
 \Lambda _0=k[\Delta _0],\qquad
 J=\ker(\Lambda\to\Lambda _0),
\tag{1.2}
\]

and let \(\mathfrak a_K\) be the augmentation ideal of \(k[K]\).

### Lemma 1.1 (CROSSED RELATIVE-IDEAL POWERS)

For every \(r\geq1\),

\[
 \boxed{J=\mathfrak a_K\Lambda,
 \qquad J^r=\mathfrak a_K^r\Lambda.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{J^{(p-1)t+1}=0.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

Choose one set-theoretic section of \(\pi\).  It gives the vector-space
decomposition

\[
 \Lambda=\bigoplus_{q\in\Delta _0} k[K]\widetilde q.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The induced map to \(\Lambda _0\) applies augmentation to the coefficient
in every summand, so its kernel is exactly
\(\mathfrak a_K\Lambda\).  Normality of \(K\) makes
\(\mathfrak a_K\) invariant under conjugation by every
\(\widetilde q\).  Consequently multiplication of two homogeneous summands
with coefficients in \(\mathfrak a_K^a\) and
\(\mathfrak a_K^b\) has coefficient in
\(\mathfrak a_K^{a+b}\).  Induction gives
\(J^r\subseteq\mathfrak a_K^r\Lambda\); the reverse inclusion follows
from \(\mathfrak a_K\subseteq J\) and the fact that \(J^r\) is a right
ideal.  This proves (1.3).

Choose a basis \(z_1,\ldots,z_t\) of \(K\).  In characteristic \(p\),

\[
 k[K]\cong
 k[T_1,\ldots,T_t]/(T_1^p,\ldots,T_t^p),
 \qquad T_i=z_i-1.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The augmentation ideal is generated by the \(T_i\).  A nonzero monomial
has total degree at most \((p-1)t\), so
\(\mathfrak a_K^{(p-1)t+1}=0\).  Equation (1.3) gives (1.4). \(\square\)

No semisimplicity of \(k[\Delta _0]\), splitting of (1.1), or
prime-to-\(p\) hypothesis on \(|\Delta _0|\) is used.  The section in
(1.5) is only a vector-space coordinate device; the group extension may be
non-split.

## 2. The finite Neumann section over the actual action ring

Let

\[
 f:M\longrightarrow N
\tag{2.1}
\]

be a homomorphism of left \(\Lambda\)-modules, with \(N\) finite free, and
assume that

\[
 \bar f:M/JM\longrightarrow N/JN
\tag{2.2}
\]

is onto.  Choose leading preimages and form \(s_0:N\to M\) and
\(E=fs_0-1_N\) as in v362.  Then \(E(N)\subseteq JN\), so Lemma 1.1 gives

\[
 E^{(p-1)t+1}=0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (FULL-ACTION FINITE SPLITTER)

The polynomial

\[
 \boxed{
 U=\sum_{i=0}^{(p-1)t}(-E)^i,
 \qquad s=s_0U}
\tag{2.4}
\]

is a \(\Lambda\)-linear section of \(f\):

\[
 \boxed{fs=1_N.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently, for every \(r\geq1\), the two v361 defects vanish:

\[
 \boxed{S_r(f)=0=T_r(f).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (2.3) makes (2.4) the finite inverse of \(1_N+E=fs_0\).
Thus \(fs=(1_N+E)U=1_N\).  Apply v361 Corollary 2.2 to the resulting split
epimorphism. \(\square\)

If \(N\) is finite projective rather than free, Lemma 1.1 still puts
\(J\) inside the Jacobson radical because every nilpotent ideal lies there.
V362 Corollary 1.2 therefore gives a split epimorphism from leading
surjectivity.  Formula (2.4) is the directly serialized version when a
physical free basis is available.

## 3. Exact R07 consequence

\[
 K=\ker(\Delta _1\to\Delta _0)\cong(C_3)^t.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The Newton/action ideal is the full relative ideal

\[
 J=\ker\bigl(\mathbf F_3[\Delta _1]
       \longrightarrow\mathbf F_3[\Delta _0]\bigr),
\tag{3.2}
\]

not merely the augmentation ideal in the isolated kernel algebra.  Lemma
1.1 proves directly on the correctly typed ring that

\[
 \boxed{J^{2t+1}=0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence v362 needs no extra locality, semisimplicity or crossed-product
splitting assumption at this finite edge.  For either packaged map

\[
 G:\widetilde A\to Y,

\tag{3.4}
