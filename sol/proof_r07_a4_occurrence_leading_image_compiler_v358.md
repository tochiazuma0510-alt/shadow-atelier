## 1. The occurrence-level map must come before localization

Let (k=\mathbf F_3), and retain one elementary-abelian relative
Frattini edge

\[
 \Delta _1\mathrel{\mathop{\twoheadrightarrow}^{\pi}}\Delta _0,
 \qquad K=\ker\pi,
 \qquad A=k[\Delta _1],
 \qquad I=\ker(A\to k[\Delta _0]).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Both marked groups are generated by the displayed images of (x,y).  A
positive A4 receipt supplies an ordered word-bearing basis

\[
 K=\langle k_1,\ldots,k_t\rangle_k
\tag{1.2}
\]

and literal source words (u_i\in F(x,y)) evaluating to the (k_i).

Let \(\widehat Z\) be the finite leading module in which every signed H1,
H2 and printed-pentagon occurrence is still separately tagged.  It may also
carry any linear formation, commutator and side-gate coordinates on which
the common diagonal action has been authenticated.  The roof-fixed Fox map
is

\[
 \widehat B:I\longrightarrow\widehat Z.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Before occurrences are combined, (1.3) is (A)-linear: left translation
of a common-word coefficient translates all of its tagged occurrences
simultaneously.  This is exactly the occurrence-level equivariance used in
v242.  In contrast, the printed block sum and the later localization
quotients need only be (k)-linear.

Accordingly, let

\[
 C_{\rm loc}:\widehat Z\twoheadrightarrow Q_{\rm loc}
\tag{1.4}
\]

be an authenticated finite leading target map.  It includes the printed
block sum and, when available, the complete formation/Brunnian localization
quotient.  Put

\[
 B_{\rm loc}=C_{\rm loc}\widehat B:I\longrightarrow Q_{\rm loc}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

No (A)-linearity is assumed for (C_{\rm loc}) or (B_{\rm loc}).

## 2. The A4 basis generates the entire relative ideal

### Lemma 2.1 (A4 PRIMITIVE IDEAL ROSTER)

Under (1.1)--(1.2),

\[
 \boxed{I=\sum_{i=1}^t A(k_i-1).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every summand has the literal roof-fibre form

\[
 g(k_i-1)=gk_i-g.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For any epimorphism of groups, the kernel of the induced group-algebra map
is the left ideal generated by (k-1), (k\in K).  If
(k=s_1\cdots s_m), with every (s_j=k_{i_j}^{\epsilon_j}), then

\[
 k-1=\sum_{j=1}^m
 s_1\cdots s_{j-1}(s_j-1),
 \qquad
 k_i^{-1}-1=-k_i^{-1}(k_i-1).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the basis elements in (1.2) generate all (k-1), proving (2.1).
Since (k_i) reduces to the identity in \(\Delta _0\), the two words in
(2.2) have the same roof value.  \(\square\)

V340's local-outer and prefix-collision vectors are explicit bases after a
chosen projected split.  Lemma 2.1 does not contradict that decomposition:
it says that every one of those vectors is already a finite linear
combination of marked translates of the much smaller primitive roster
(k_i-1).  A score or image computation therefore does not have to
materialize all coarse-prefix fibres first.

## 3. Exhausted occurrence closure is the complete Jacobian image

For each A4 basis word form the literal occurrence column

\[
 v_i=\widehat B(k_i-1)\in\widehat Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Start an echelon with the nonzero (v_i).  Whenever a row raises rank,
retain its coefficient ancestry in the form

\[
 \theta_j=\sum_\ell a_{j\ell}
 g_{j\ell}(k_{i(j,\ell)}-1),
 \qquad
 w_j=\widehat B(\theta_j),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and enqueue the row.  Apply (x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}) through the simultaneous
tagged-occurrence action, enqueueing only rank increases.  Stop when the
queue is empty.

### Theorem 3.1 (FINITE COMPLETE OCCURRENCE IMAGE)

The terminal span is

\[
 \boxed{
 W=\operatorname{span}_k\{w_j\}
   =\operatorname{im}\widehat B.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{im}B_{\rm loc}=C_{\rm loc}(W).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The construction terminates after at most \(\dim_k\widehat Z\) rank
increases and retains a literal common-word preimage for every basis row.

#### Proof

By Lemma 2.1, every element of (I) is a finite sum of elements
(g(k_i-1)).  Since \(\Delta _1\) is generated by (x,y) and
\(\widehat B\) is (A)-linear, every image

\[
 \widehat B(g(k_i-1))=g\,v_i
\tag{3.5}
\]

belongs to the invariant closure.  Hence
\(\operatorname{im}\widehat B\subseteq W\).  Conversely every queued row
is obtained from the seed columns by the same action and linear
combination, so it lies in \(\operatorname{im}\widehat B\).  This proves
(3.3), while (3.4) follows from (1.5).  Finite dimension gives termination,
and replaying the same row operations on (2.2) gives the ancestry (3.2).
\(\square\)

The order of operations is load-bearing.  One must close in
\(\widehat Z\) and apply (C_{\rm loc}) only afterwards.  Closing the
already block-summed rows would silently impose an action on a quotient on
which no such action has been proved.

### Corollary 3.2 (FINITE LEADING ONTO DICHOTOMY)

Assume the map (1.4) and a basis of (Q_{\rm loc}) are complete.  Exact
row reduction on the columns (C_{\rm loc}(w_j)) returns exactly one of:

1. **ONTO:** a literal preimage in (I) for every chosen generator of
   (Q_{\rm loc}); or
2. **NOT ONTO:** a nonzero functional
   \(\lambda\in Q_{\rm loc}^*\) satisfying

   \[
    \lambda C_{\rm loc}(W)=0.
   \tag{3.6}
   \]

Thus, after the leading identification

\[
 Q_{\rm loc}\cong L/JL
\tag{3.7}
\]

is authenticated, the ONTO branch supplies precisely the word-bearing
all-generator leading solve required by v319 and v334.

#### Proof

Equation (3.4) identifies the tested finite span with the whole image of
(B_{\rm loc}).  Finite-dimensional row-space duality gives the two
alternatives.  In the ONTO branch, substitute each retained combination of
the (w_j) into (3.2) to recover its literal preimage.  Under (3.7), these
are the leading columns (s_0(e_i)) of v319.  \(\square\)

There is also a useful stronger first test.  If (W=\widehat Z), then
(B_{\rm loc}) is onto for **every** surjective localization map (1.4).
If (W\ne\widehat Z), that is not a negative result for a smaller localized
quotient; one must apply its actual (C_{\rm loc}) and use Corollary 3.2.

## 4. The structural solve does not wait for the A0 word

The map \(\widehat B\), its seed columns (3.1), and their marked actions are
fixed by the roof, tower and occurrence ledger.  V263 Lemma 2.1 proves that
replacing a partial word (F) by (Fu), (u\in P_{(1)}), does not change
the associated-graded Jacobian at any later Zassenhaus depth.  Therefore the
specific A0 correction word is needed to instantiate the first literal
residual, but not to compute the structural image (3.3)--(3.4).

This separates two GHA lanes after an A4 positive terminal:

1. **structural lane:** compute the complete occurrence image and the raw or
   localized all-generator onto gate from A4 plus the fixed tower owners;
2. **witness lane:** use the accepted A0/task193 word for its actual A5--A9
   residuals and endpoint certificates.

The lanes meet when the witness residual is expressed in the structural
based cover.  V357 then absorbs every occurrence-prefix transport as a
filtration-raising additive perturbation; no new transported-Jacobian search
is introduced.

## 5. What A4 does and does not close

Theorem 3.1 removes three apparent prerequisites from the leading solve:

1. enumeration of \(\Delta _1\);
2. enumeration of all coarse-prefix fibres and explicit construction of
   v340's collision basis; and
3. availability of the actual A0 correction word.

It does **not** identify the full raw occurrence target with the doubly
localized module.  The remaining target-side obligation is an authenticated
finite map (1.4) and the two-way identification (3.7).  In R07 this includes:

1. all signed H1/H2/pentagon occurrence and printed-order maps;
2. the complete linear Brunnian and formation support, not only a sampled
   syzygy roster;
3. every registered commutator and side-gate coordinate; and
4. either a strict filtered realization of (L), or the pointed saturation
   coefficients required by v321.

Nor does a finite first-edge ONTO result by itself prove naturality through
all matched refinements.  V99 gives naturality of the literal Jacobians, but
the target quotients, leading preimages and strict/weighted cover must also
reduce compatibly, or the vertical v314/v332 obstruction must be solved at
each new edge.
