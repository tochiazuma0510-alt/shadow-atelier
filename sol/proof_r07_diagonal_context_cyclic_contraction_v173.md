## 1. The common action is the diagonal context image

At relative-Frattini level \(n\), retain the seven separately tagged
evaluation contexts: the two hexagon contexts and the five ordered pentagon
cofaces. Write their coefficient groups as \(Q_{i,n}\), \(1\leq i\leq7\),
and let

\[
 \rho_{i,n}:F_2\longrightarrow Q_{i,n}
\tag{1.1}
\]

be the homomorphism obtained by evaluating the same source word in context
\(i\). Define the simultaneous context group

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_n=\operatorname{im}\!\left(
 F_2\xrightarrow{(\rho_{1,n},\ldots,\rho_{7,n})}
 \prod_{i=1}^7Q_{i,n}\right).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus \(\Delta_n\) is not any one PB3 or PB4 deck group and is not their
unrestricted direct product. It is exactly the subdirect group produced by
one common source conjugator. Put

\[
 \Lambda_n=\mathbf F_3[\Delta_n].
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proposition 1.1 (DIAGONAL CONTEXT ACTION)

The complete block-tagged residual module \(Z_n\), the actual common-word
correction module \(A_n\), and the literal correction map

\[
 B_n:A_n\longrightarrow Z_n
\tag{1.4}
\]

are naturally \(\Lambda_n\)-modules and a \(\Lambda_n\)-linear map,
respectively. Matched refinement induces surjections

\[
 \Delta_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\Delta_n,
 \qquad \Lambda_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_n,
\tag{1.5}
\]

and all these actions and maps commute with reduction.

#### Proof

For \(u,r\in F_2\), the occurrence of the conjugate \(uru^{-1}\) in context
\(i\) is the conjugate of the occurrence of \(r\) by \(\rho_{i,n}(u)\).
Consequently one source conjugator acts simultaneously on all seven tagged
blocks through the tuple in (1.2). Fox linearization in an abelian chief
kernel commutes with this conjugation, so the literal boundary/correction
map is equivariant. The exact-commutator domain is characteristic and the
relative-formation domain is normal, hence their intersection is stable
under the same action. This gives (1.3)--(1.4).

Every matched refinement commutes with all seven word-evaluation maps.
It therefore maps the diagonal image at level \(n+1\) onto the diagonal
image at level \(n\), and equivariance commutes with this reduction. This
proves (1.5). \(\square\)

In particular, the common scalar object required but not authenticated in
v172 is canonical once the seven context maps are retained. Using one
component algebra on the other six blocks would still be ill-typed; (1.2)
is the correction.

## 2. A cofinal multiplicative ideal on the relative pro-3 lane

Let

\[
 \Delta_\infty=\varprojlim_n\Delta_n,
 \qquad
 \Xi=\mathbf F_3[[\Delta_\infty]]
       =\varprojlim_n\mathbf F_3[\Delta_n].
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let \(P=\ker(\Delta_\infty\to\Delta_0)\). On the tower of v145, \(P\) is
a finitely generated pro-3 group: \(\Delta_\infty\) is topologically
finitely generated by the common source images, and \(P\) is an open
subgroup of the relative pro-3 kernel. Let

\[
 \mathfrak j=\overline{\langle p-1:p\in P\rangle}\triangleleft\Xi.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (COFINAL AUGMENTATION FILTRATION)

The powers \((\mathfrak j^m)_{m\geq0}\) form a complete separated
multiplicative filtration. At every fixed finite relative pro-3 quotient,
some \(\mathfrak j^m\) acts by zero. Hence a \(\mathfrak j\)-adically
convergent correction determines compatible corrections in every finite
window of the relative pro-3 lane.

#### Proof

Let \(U\triangleleft P\) be open and normal in \(\Delta_\infty\). The image
of \(\mathfrak j\) in the finite algebra belonging to \(P/U\) is the
augmentation ideal of a finite 3-group, extended by finitely many cosets.
That ideal is nilpotent, so \(\mathfrak j^{N(U)}\) vanishes after reduction
modulo \(U\). Conversely, because \(P\) is topologically finitely
generated, the quotient of \(\mathbf F_3[[P]]\) by the \(m\)-th augmentation
power is finite dimensional; adjoining the finitely many coarse cosets
keeps \(\Xi/\mathfrak j^m\) finite dimensional. Thus the powers are open
and cofinal. If an element lies in every power, its image lies in every
power of every finite quotient augmentation ideal and is therefore zero;
the inverse-limit element is zero. Completeness follows from (2.1), and
\(\mathfrak j^r\mathfrak j^s\subseteq\mathfrak j^{r+s}\) is immediate.
\(\square\)

This filtration is cofinal with the finite relative pro-3 topology. It is
not an identification of the Frattini rung number with a Jennings depth;
v145's warning separating those two indices remains in force.

## 3. One-word cyclic Neumann theorem

Assume the completed actual correction datum of v111/v172 exists on this
matched tower. Thus let \(A=\varprojlim A_n\), \(Z=\varprojlim Z_n\), and
\(B=\varprojlim B_n\). By Proposition 1.1 these are complete continuous
\(\Xi\)-modules and \(B\) is \(\Xi\)-linear. Let
\(\beta=(\beta_n)\in Z\) be the compatible actual defect and define its
closed cyclic context subsystem

\[
 L=\overline{\Xi\beta}\leq Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Suppose a literal word-bearing correction gives \(a\in A\). Consider the
three conditions

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\text{(C1)}&&\operatorname{Ann}_\Xi(\beta)\,a=0,\\
 &\text{(C2)}&&\varepsilon:=\beta-Ba\in\mathfrak jL,\\
 &\text{(C3)}&&\overline{\Xi a}\text{ lies in the registered linear
 correction domain, and every finite Neumann partial correction preserves
 the nonlinear side gates.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Condition (C1) says that every context relation of the actual defect is also
a relation of the chosen correction. Condition (C2) is the genuine next-
filtration canary: the error must lie one step deeper in the *same actual
cyclic subsystem*, not merely in \(\mathfrak jZ\). In (C3), the linear
domain includes the exact-commutator, joint-formation, marking, and repeated-
context restrictions. The separate finite-partial-product clause is
load-bearing for automorphism/onto and every other nonlinear gate.
Because \(\mathfrak j\) is compact and the orbit map is continuous, its
image \(\mathfrak j\beta=\mathfrak jL\) is closed. Thus (C2) really does
provide at least one \(\mu\in\mathfrak j\) with
\(\varepsilon=\mu\beta\).

### Theorem 3.1 (DIAGONAL CYCLIC CONTRACTION)

Under (C1)--(C3), choose \(\mu\in\mathfrak j\) with
\(\varepsilon=\mu\beta\). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 h(\lambda\beta)=
 \lambda\left(\sum_{r\geq0}\mu^r\right)a}
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Xi)
\tag{3.3}
\]

is a well-defined continuous \(\Xi\)-linear map \(L\to A\), independent of
the chosen expression of an element of \(L\), and

\[
 \boxed{Bh=1_L.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 c_\infty=-h(\beta)
 =-\left(1+\mu+\mu^2+\cdots\right)a}
\tag{3.5}
\]

is one compatible correction value on every finite relative pro-3
refinement. At each fixed finite quotient the series is finite.

#### Proof

The orbit map \(\Xi\to L\), \(\lambda\mapsto\lambda\beta\), is a continuous
surjection from a compact ring onto a closed module. Its kernel is
\(\operatorname{Ann}_\Xi(\beta)\). Condition (C1) therefore makes

\[
 s(\lambda\beta)=\lambda a
\tag{3.6}
\]

a well-defined continuous \(\Xi\)-linear map \(L\to A\). Put
\(T=1_L-Bs\). By (C2),

\[
 T(\lambda\beta)=\lambda(\beta-Ba)=\lambda\mu\beta.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Hence

\[
 T(\mathfrak j^mL)\subseteq\mathfrak j^{m+1}L
\tag{3.8}
\]

for every \(m\). Lemma 2.1 and v172 Theorem 2.1 now give
\(h=s\sum_{r\geq0}T^r\) and \(Bh=1_L\). Applying the powers of \(T\) to
\(\beta\) yields \(T^r\beta=\mu^r\beta\), which is (3.3)--(3.5).
If another \(\mu'\in\mathfrak j\) represents the same error, then every
\((\mu^r-\mu'^r)\beta\) is zero because both expressions equal
\(T^r\beta\); (C1) makes the difference kill \(a\). Thus the displayed
value is unchanged. Nilpotence after every finite reduction follows from
Lemma 2.1. \(\square\)

Formula (3.5) is the promised all-stage explicit selector. Its terms are
not unrelated finite choices: \(\mu^ra\) is the registered linear
combination of simultaneous context conjugates of the same literal word.
V98 converts each finite reduction of this value into an ordinary nested
correction word.

## 4. What must be checked, and what need not be recomputed

At a finite quotient, (C1) is the exact relation/annihilator gate

\[
 \ker(\Lambda_n\to\Lambda_n\beta_n)
 \subseteq
 \ker(\Lambda_n\to A_n,\ \lambda\mapsto\lambda a_n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

It is ordinary finite linear algebra once the genuine diagonal orbit roster
and the literal correction columns are present. The completed condition is
equivalent to (4.1) at every cofinal finite reduction. A single leading
quotient does not imply all of them unless the relation presentation itself
has been proved stable under refinement.

Likewise, task193 can test the first nonzero instance of (C2): after the
task186 word is applied, its genuine second-rung defect must be identified
with \(\mu_1\beta_1\) for a typed element of the first relative augmentation
ideal. Membership only in the ambient residual module is insufficient.
Once the literal formula for \(\mu\) and its naturality under matched
refinement are proved, Theorem 3.1 replaces all later rung-by-rung solves.

Thus the uniform proof does **not** require a new arbitrary preimage at every
rung. It requires one word \(a\), its full context-relation compatibility,
and one natural deeper-error formula. These are exactly the extra pieces of
information which an explicit lift can expose and a bare membership vector
cannot.
