# R07 abelian repair-orbit collapse v203

Author: Sol / 2026-08-28

Status: paper optimization and exact compression of v200--v202.  In a
mod-\(\ell\) abelian endpoint screen, the two translating words in the
normal-relator form collapse to their product, and all projected successor
relators collapse to a basis of a subgroup of \((C_\ell)^2\).  The complete
projected repair space therefore needs at most 8 columns for \(\ell=2\) and
18 columns for \(\ell=3\), after one complete two-dimensional relator-rank
scan.  A projected NO remains an exact same-successor obstruction; a
projected YES remains only a seed.  No screen has yet been run and no lift,
fake certificate, or Ihara witness is declared.

## 1. One common abelian source quotient

Fix a prime \(\ell\).  Let

\[
 \alpha_\ell:F(x,y)\twoheadrightarrow
 D_\ell\cong(C_\ell)^2
\tag{1.1}
\]

be the exact joint mod-\(\ell\) occurrence map from v202.  The H1/1
coordinate makes (1.1) injective on source abelianization modulo \(\ell\),
so two source words have the same \(D_\ell\)-value if and only if their
\((x,y)\) exponent vectors agree modulo \(\ell\).

Let

\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}:
 \mathbf F_3[D_\ell]\longrightarrow E_\ell
\tag{1.2}
\]

be the linear map obtained by applying all eleven induced occurrence maps,
then their fixed prefixes, signs, and H1/H2/P combinations.  The projected
endpoint-change map factors exactly as

\[
 \bar{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}
 =\widetilde{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}\circ(\alpha_\ell)_*.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This factorization is valid only after projection to the abelian endpoint
groups.  It is not an equivariance or finite-state assertion for the exact
PB endpoint map.

## 2. Projected successor-kernel subgroup

Let

\[
 H_1=\ker(F(x,y)\to\Delta_1),
 \qquad
 J_1=\ker\bigl(\mathbf F_3[F]\to\mathbf F_3[\Delta_1]\bigr),
\tag{2.1}
\]

and define

\[
 R_\ell=\alpha_\ell(H_1)\le D_\ell.
\tag{2.2}
\]

If \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_t\}\) is any complete finite normal-relator roster
for \(H_1\), then

\[
 R_\ell=
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_\ell}
 \{\operatorname{exp}_{x,y}(s_i)\bmod\ell:1\le i\le t\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus \(r_\ell:=\dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}R_\ell\) belongs to
\(\{0,1,2\}\).  Formula (2.3) needs one complete scan of the relator roster,
but stores only a two-row echelon and a retained source relator for each
pivot.

### Lemma 2.1 (IMAGE OF THE SUCCESSOR DIRECTION IDEAL)

Choose relators \(r_1,\ldots,r_{r_\ell}\in H_1\) whose exponent classes form
a basis of \(R_\ell\).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 (\alpha_\ell)_*(J_1)
 =\sum_{q=1}^{r_\ell}
   \mathbf F_3[D_\ell]\bigl(\alpha_\ell(r_q)-1\bigr).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

V195 identifies \(J_1\) with the two-sided ideal generated by \(h-1\),
\(h\in H_1\).  Its image under \((\alpha_\ell)_*\) is therefore the ideal
of \(\mathbf F_3[D_\ell]\) generated by \(r-1\), \(r\in R_\ell\).

The finite group \(D_\ell\) is abelian.  Every \(r\in R_\ell\) is a word in
the chosen basis elements, and the identities

\[
 ab-1=(a-1)+a(b-1),
 \qquad
 a^{-1}-1=-a^{-1}(a-1)
\tag{2.5}
\]

express \(r-1\) as a left group-algebra combination of
\(\alpha_\ell(r_q)-1\).  This proves one inclusion in (2.4); the reverse
one is immediate. \(\square\)

Normal generation of the original successor kernel remains load-bearing in
(2.3).  A partial relator stream can give a lower bound on \(R_\ell\), but
cannot certify that the displayed projected ideal is complete.

## 3. Complete orbit collapse

### Theorem 3.1 (ABELIAN REPAIR-ORBIT COLLAPSE)

The complete projected same-successor repair space is

\[
 \boxed{
 \bar{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}(J_1)=
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_3}
 \left\{
  \widetilde{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}
  \bigl(d(\alpha_\ell(r_q)-1)\bigr):
  d\in D_\ell,\ 1\le q\le r_\ell
 \right\}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

It has a complete generating roster of size at most

\[
 \boxed{|D_\ell|r_\ell\le2\ell^2.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Apply the linear factorization (1.3) to Lemma 2.1.  This immediately gives
(3.1), and v202 gives \(|D_\ell|=\ell^2\), proving (3.2). \(\square\)

Equivalently, start with v195's two-sided column.  In every abelian
occurrence quotient,

\[
 \bar\rho_o(A)
 (\bar\rho_o(s)-1)
 \bar\rho_o(B)
 =
 \bar\rho_o(AB)(\bar\rho_o(s)-1).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus \((A,B)\) depends only on one joint value \(d=\alpha_\ell(AB)\).
Relations with the same exponent vector modulo \(\ell\) have the same joint
image, and the group-basis reduction (2.5) leaves at most \(r_\ell\) relator
directions.  Formula (3.1) is therefore a proved orbit quotient, not an
unrecorded duplicate-column heuristic.

If \(r_\ell=0\), then

\[
 \bar{\mathcal E}_{d,\ell}(J_1)=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

In that case any nonzero projected endpoint is immediately an exact
same-\(\mu_1\) obstruction.  If \(r_\ell=1\) or two, Gaussian elimination on

V202's counts \(16t\) and \(81t\) remain correct for a literal exhaustive
enumeration of all \((\bar A,s_i,\bar B)\) triples.  Theorem 3.1 proves that
production need not materialize those triples: the two-dimensional rank
scan plus at most 8 or 18 essential columns has exactly the same projected
span.

## 5. Certificate and safe production order

After the actual \(M_0\) and complete successor relators exist:

1. compute the exact PB endpoint; skip repair if it is zero;
2. scan every complete successor relator, retain its two integer exponent
   sums, reduce modulo \(\ell\), and build \(R_\ell\);
3. retain the lexicographically first literal relator for every pivot of
   \(R_\ell\);
4. enumerate the exact four or nine elements of \(D_\ell\) and all columns
   (3.1);
5. decide projected membership and emit coefficients or a full dual; and
6. use v200's pullback theorem for a NO, while replaying every YES candidate
   in exact PB normal form.

The independent checker reads the complete relator stream separately,
recomputes normal-roster completeness pins, exponent vectors, pivot ancestry,
the exact \(R_\ell\) rank, all \(|D_\ell|r_\ell\) columns, and the target
pairings.  It rejects deletion of a nonpivot relator too: such a deletion
may change the proof that the rank scan covered the complete normal roster
even when the final two-dimensional span happens to remain unchanged.

This theorem closes only the finite projected selector.  A screen pass does
not prove an exact repair, and a screen obstruction excludes only the named
first multiplier's affine fibre, not every possible multiplier or every
cofinal prime lane.
