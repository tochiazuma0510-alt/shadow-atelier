## 1. Filtered Cayley--Fox complex

Let

[
 1\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow Q_2\longrightarrow Q_1\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad V=C_3^3,quad Q_1=P\times C_2^2,quad |P|=504,
                                                               \tag{1.1}
]

be the split first rung with the marked lifts of v443.  Put (k=\mathbf F_3)
and filter (k[Q_2]) by powers of the augmentation ideal (I\subset k[V]).
The right Fox complex is

[
 C_1=k[Q_2]^2\xrightarrow{\partial} C_0=k[Q_2]
       \longrightarrow k\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad \partial(a,b)=a(X-1)+b(Y-1).                 \tag{1.2}
]

Its first homology is the cycle module (Z_2=\ker\partial).  The filtration
has associated coefficient algebra

[
 M=\operatorname {gr}k[V]
   =k[u_1,u_2,u_3]/(u_1^3,u_2^3,u_3^3),              \tag{1.3}
]

with degree dimensions (h_d=(1,3,6,7,6,3,1)) for (0\le d\le6).

For a left source character (\lambda\in\widehat {C_2^2}), the (E_1)
page of the filtered two-term complex is exactly the v454 associated
Cayley boundary.  Therefore

[
 \dim E^1_{1,d,\lambda}=504h_d+m_{d,\lambda},
 \qquad
 \dim E^1_{0,d,\lambda}=m_{d,\lambda}.               \tag{1.4}
]

The second equality is the cokernel calculation already audited in Task575.

## 2. The first transgression is onto every positive (H_0) grade

The marked elements (X,Y\in Q_2) generate (Q_2).  Consequently the
endpoint-holonomy map

[
 \ker(F(x,y)\to Q_1)\longrightarrow V                \tag{2.1}
]

is onto: its image is the kernel of the subgroup generated by (X,Y) over
their image (Q_1), which is all of (V).  Taking initial augmentation
terms says that the degree-one holonomies of (Q_1)-closed marked words span

[
 I/I^2=M_1=\langle u_1,u_2,u_3\rangle.                \tag{2.2}
]

The Fox cycle of such a closed word is an (E_1) class in homological degree
one and filtration degree zero.  Its first filtered boundary is its endpoint
holonomy, so the spectral-sequence differential contains (2.2):

[
 d_1:E^1_{1,0}\twoheadrightarrow E^1_{0,1}=M_1.       \tag{2.3}
]

The construction is compatible with multiplication by an associated
coefficient monomial.  Thus the image of (d_1) in (H_0(E^1)=M) is the
ideal generated by (M_1).  Every positive monomial in (1.3) has a factor
(u_i), including at the truncated top degree, and hence

[
 \boxed{d_1:E^1_{1,d,\lambda}	woheadrightarrow
                E^1_{0,d+1,\lambda}}
 \qquad(0\le d<6).                                    \tag{2.4}
]

Here the character statement follows either by tracking the sign of the
factored (u_i), or invariantly because (2.3) is (C_2^2)-equivariant and
(k[C_2^2]) is semisimple.  Thus the rank in character (\lambda) is
(m_{d+1,\lambda}).

There is no incoming differential to homological degree one, because (1.2)
has no (C_2) term.  After (2.4), positive-degree (E^2_{0,*}) is zero; the
only remaining (H_0) class is the degree-zero trivial augmentation.  Hence
no higher differential can leave homological degree one, and the spectral
sequence collapses there.
