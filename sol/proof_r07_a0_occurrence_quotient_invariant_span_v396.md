## 1. The occurrence-level source must be retained

Let

\[
 F=\langle x,y\rangle,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\langle\!\langle r_1,\ldots,r_{6441}\rangle\!\rangle_F
\tag{1.1}
\]

be the registered task198 normal correction domain.  Let \(\mathcal O\) be
the frozen eleven-occurrence roster used by task175, with repeated
occurrences, signs, prefixes and H1/H2/P block tags kept separate.  For every
occurrence put

\[
 \sigma_o:F\longrightarrow P_{B(o)},
 \qquad
 U=\bigoplus_{o\in\mathcal O} C_o,
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(C_o\) is the corresponding raw Fox relation module.  Define

\[
 J(c)=\bigl(\delta_o(\sigma_o(c))\bigr)_{o\in\mathcal O}
 \quad(c\in\Omega).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every element of \(\Omega\) has identity roof value in every occurrence.
The Fox product rule therefore gives

\[
 J(cd)=J(c)+J(d),\qquad J(c^{-1})=-J(c).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Let \(L_g:U\to Z\) be the fixed signed-prefix aggregation for
\(g=g_{760}\).  The actual A0 correction column is

\[
 \mathscr V(c)=L_gJ(c).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The aggregation is deliberately applied only after the occurrence-level
calculation.  Different occurrences have different actors, so in general
there is no single action on \(Z\) through which \(L_g\) is equivariant.

## 2. Source conjugation is generated by four sparse actions

For \(s\in F\), define the block-diagonal action

\[
 \rho(s)(u_o)_o=
 \bigl(\overline{\sigma_o(s)}\,u_o\bigr)_o.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(c\in\Omega\), the cocycle cancellation in each occurrence gives

\[
 \boxed{J(scs^{-1})=\rho(s)J(c).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Indeed, with \(u=\sigma_o(s)\) and \(w=\sigma_o(c)\), the roof value of
\(w\) is one and hence

\[
 \delta(uwu^{-1})
 =\delta(u)+\bar u\delta(w)+\bar u\delta(u^{-1})
 =\bar u\delta(w).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let \(W\le U\) be the least vector subspace containing all \(J(r_i)\) and
invariant under

\[
 \rho(x),\rho(x^{-1}),\rho(y),\rho(y^{-1}).
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (A0 NORMAL IMAGE AS AN INVARIANT SPAN)

\[
 \boxed{J(\Omega)=W.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

The left side is a vector subspace by (1.4), contains every seed, and is
invariant under (2.4) by (2.2), so \(W\subseteq J(\Omega)\).  Conversely,
every element of the normal closure (1.1) is a finite product of inverses of
conjugates \(ur_i u^{-1}\).  Equations (1.4) and (2.2) put its image in the
invariant span of the seeds.  Thus \(J(\Omega)\subseteq W\).  \(\square\)

Consequently a queue which inserts the 6,441 seed rows and applies the four
actions only to rank-raising rows terminates at exactly \(W\).  If
\(r=\dim W\), it makes at most

\[
 \boxed{6441+4r}
\tag{2.6}
\]

insertion attempts.  It never enumerates the joint state group, a Q0 fibre,
or a conjugating-word roster.

Every retained row is word-bearing.  A seed stores `LEAF(r_i)`; an action
stores `CONJUGATE(letter,parent)`; row addition and scalar two store ordered
`PRODUCT` and `INVERSE` nodes.  Expanding the resulting finite DAG gives a
literal word of \(\Omega\).

## 3. The complete boundary is another invariant span

Write

\[
 Z=Z_{H_1}\oplus Z_{H_2}\oplus Z_P\oplus\mathbf F_3^2
\tag{3.1}
\]

and let \(D=D_{H_1}\oplus D_{H_2}\oplus D_P\) be the complete typed
PB3/PB4 boundary image.  The authenticated base roster has two PB3 relators
in each of H1 and H2 and eleven PB4 relators in P: fifteen typed seeds in
total.  Close the H1 and H2 seeds under the three marked PB3 generators and
their inverses, and close the P seeds under the six marked PB4 generators
and their inverses.  Keep the three tags separate.

### Theorem 3.1 (A0 FULL-MARKED BOUNDARY CLOSURE)

The three exhausted queue spaces are exactly
\(D_{H_1},D_{H_2},D_P\).

#### Proof

Every queued row is a translate of a presentation-boundary row, so each
queue is contained in its typed boundary image.  At exhaustion the PB3
queues are invariant under all three marked generators and inverses, and the
PB4 queue under all six.  These generators generate the respective marked
quotients, so the queues contain every translate of every base boundary
row.  This is the reverse containment.  Strict pivot increase proves finite
termination.  \(\square\)

If the three terminal ranks are \(b_1,b_2,b_3\), the complete boundary costs
at most

\[
 \boxed{15+6(b_1+b_2)+12b_3}
\tag{3.2}
\]

insertion attempts.  This replaces the repeated support-by-104-descriptor
correlation at every changing dual.  The latter remains a valid independent
checker, but is not the preferred producer hot path.

## 4. Safe occurrence quotient before closure

For each occurrence let \(B_o\le C_o\) be an authenticated subspace which
is invariant under the four source actions, and put

\[
 B=\bigoplus_oB_o.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Assume the physical aggregation satisfies

\[
 L_g(B)\subseteq D.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then \(L_g\) induces

\[
 \overline L_g:U/B\longrightarrow Z/D,
 \qquad
 \overline L_g(u+B)=L_g(u)+D.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (BOUNDARY-QUOTIENT PRUNING)

Let \(\overline W\) be the invariant span in \(U/B\) of the 6,441 quotient
seeds.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
  D+\mathscr V(\Omega)=
  \{d+L_g(w):d\in D,\ w\in W\},
  \qquad
  (D+\mathscr V(\Omega))/D=\overline L_g(\overline W).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

The first equality is Theorem 2.1 and (1.5).  Invariance of \(B\) makes the
four actions well-defined on \(U/B\), so the quotient of the least invariant
span is the least invariant span of the quotient seeds.  Condition (4.2)
makes (4.3) well-defined, and applying the quotient map to the first equality
gives the second.  \(\square\)

This is the safe place to remove boundary directions.  Quotienting or
aggregating occurrences before (4.2) is authenticated is forbidden.  With
no accepted \(B\), take \(B=0\); Theorems 2.1 and 5.1 below remain exact.

## 5. Finite A0 decision and literal positive certificate

Let \(T\in Z\) be the independently reconstructed base defect, including
the two exponent coordinates.  The A0 equation is

\[
 -T\in D+\mathscr V(\Omega).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (FINITE A0 INVARIANT-SPAN SELECTOR)

After the boundary closure of Theorem 3.1 and the correction closure of
Theorem 2.1 (or its safe quotient form in Theorem 4.1), exact sparse
elimination decides (5.1).  On MEMBER it returns a finite literal correction
word \(c_\star\in\Omega\), exponent vector zero in \(\mathbf F_3^2\), and a
typed boundary preimage \(d\in D\) satisfying

\[
 T+\mathscr V(c_\star)=d.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Applying the independently selected registered exactifying relators as in
v156/v265 then gives a literal \(c_{\rm exact}\in\Omega\) whose two integer
exponent sums are exactly zero and whose all-seven boundary class is the
same accepted class.

#### Proof

Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 identify both summands in (5.1) exactly.  Membership is
therefore finite-dimensional linear algebra.  Coefficient ancestry in the
correction basis expands to a product of the literal normal-word DAGs, with
coefficient two interpreted as inverse.  Boundary coefficients remain in
the boundary preimage and are not inserted into the source word.  Direct
replay gives (5.2), and the two exponent rows impose the mod-three gate.
The v156 exact exponent lattice and v265 direct positive replay prove the
last exactification assertion.  \(\square\)

This is a complete finite decision if both queues exhaust.  A resource stop
before exhaustion is `UNKNOWN_RESOURCE`; a current-prefix dual is not a
separator for the missing action closure.

## 6. Speed and memory can be optimized together

The mathematical live state of the preferred producer is only:

1. three typed sparse boundary echelons and their seed/parent/action DAGs;
2. one occurrence-level correction echelon and its compact DAG;
3. the fixed target reduction; and
4. the finite marked action tables.

It does not require the old 1.66 GB adaptive-search transcript, all previous
duals, contributor lists, 22,912,880 support/descriptor pairs, Q0 section
states, or the global conjugator roster.  The durable old checkpoint remains
a fallback discovery artifact but is not a premise of this selector.

Parallel work is restricted to immutable candidate production:

- the H1, H2 and P boundary closures may run as separate tagged shards;
- for one correction frontier batch, the four signed actions may be computed
  in parallel; and
- evaluation of the 6,441 literal seeds may use disjoint prefix-DAG shards.

One central reducer alone decides rank, target reduction and ancestry IDs.
Workers receive only compact sparse frontier rows and read-only action
tables; they never inherit or copy the full reducer/checkpoint state.  Batch
merge is deterministic, but unlike the old batch-64 dual search it does not
repeat a group-state scan.

On a positive terminal, v278 selected-support replay reconstructs only the
nonzero correction and boundary ancestors and then directly checks (5.2).
No negative conclusion is drawn from a speculative or unfinished shard.

## 7. Relation to v308 and the all-rung route

V308 retains the additional nonabelian next-rung state \(Q_1\) in order to
select jointly for A0 and A5.  The present theorem drops \(Q_1\) and proves
the smaller standalone A0 decision.  Thus it can run before A4 and can feed
the accepted word to task193.  If that first word later fails A5, v346's
projected Schreier-coset fallback remains the exact complete alternative;
the present theorem does not linearize the nonlinear A5 predicate.

Likewise this finite A0 closure does not prove that every cofinal rung has a
nonempty accepted set.  It is a faster finite base-word constructor, not the
missing inverse-limit homotopy.
