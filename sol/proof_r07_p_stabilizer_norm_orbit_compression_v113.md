# R07 three-primary stabilizer-norm orbit compression v113

Author: Sol / 2026-08-27

Status: exact paper theorem for compressing the v111 actual-class splitter
test over a finite three-group.  It gives a word-bearing basis for all
preimages which respect a registered stabilizer.  It does not identify the
stabilizer or full annihilator of the stacked g760 defect, and it does not
declare a lift or witness.

## 1. Setup

Let \(k=\mathbf F_3\), let \(P\) be a finite three-group, and let

\[
 \Lambda=k[P],
 \qquad
 A=\Lambda^{(\mathcal R)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The basis vector \(g e_r\) represents the actual conjugate of the registered
normal word \(r\) by a section word for \(g\).  Let

\[
 B:A\longrightarrow Z
\tag{1.2}
\]

be the equivariant stacked correction map, after quotienting by every exact
presentation boundary which belongs to the target.  Additional linear side
coordinates may be appended to \(Z\).

Fix an actual defect \(z\in Z\), and let

\[
 H=\operatorname{Stab}_P(z)=\{h\in P:hz=z\}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For a subgroup \(H\leq P\), write

\[
 N_H=\sum_{h\in H}h\in k[P].
\tag{1.4}
\]

No division by \(|H|\) is made.  In characteristic three such division would
be invalid whenever \(H\neq1\).

## 2. Fixed vectors in the free orbit module

Choose one representative \(g\) from every left coset \(Hg\) of \(H\) in
\(P\).

### Lemma 2.1 (FREE-MODULE FIXED BASIS)

The vectors

\[
 \boxed{N_Hg e_r
 : Hg\in H\backslash P,\ r\in\mathcal R}
\tag{2.1}
\]

form a \(k\)-basis of

\[
 A^H=\{a\in A:ha=a\text{ for every }h\in H\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

In one regular summand \(k[P]e_r\), left multiplication by \(H\) permutes
the group basis in the disjoint orbits \(Hg\).  A vector is fixed precisely
when its coefficients are constant on each such orbit.  The orbit sums
\(N_Hg e_r\) have disjoint supports and give one basis vector per orbit.
Taking the direct sum over \(r\) proves the claim.  \(\square\)

The image column attached to (2.1) is the literal sum

\[
 B(N_Hg e_r)=\sum_{h\in H}B(hg e_r).
\tag{2.3}
\]

At the correction-value level this is materialized by the ordered product of
the corresponding actual conjugate words, with coefficients reduced modulo
three.  Direct word replay remains required because the product is formed in
the nonabelian source before passage to the active abelian layer.

## 3. Stabilizer-compatible membership

### Theorem 3.1 (STABILIZER-NORM PREIMAGE TEST)

There is a preimage of \(z\) which satisfies every stabilizer relation,

\[
 Ba=z,
 \qquad ha=a\quad(h\in H),
\tag{3.1}
\]

if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 z\in\operatorname{span}_k
 \{B(N_Hg e_r):Hg\in H\backslash P, r\in\mathcal R\}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Every coefficient solution to (3.2) gives the word-bearing preimage

\[
 a=\sum_{Hg,r}\alpha_{Hg,r}N_Hg e_r.
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

By Lemma 2.1, the right side of (3.2) is exactly \(B(A^H)\).  Conditions
(3.1) say exactly that \(z\in B(A^H)\), and (3.3) is its expansion in the
displayed basis.  \(\square\)

This replaces \(|P||\mathcal R|\) raw orbit columns by
\(|P:H||\mathcal R|\) norm columns whenever the stabilizer is nontrivial.
It is an exact reduction, not a heuristic pruning.

## 4. When the stabilizer test is the full cyclic test

Let

\[
 K_z=\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(z)
\tag{4.1}
\]

as in v111.  Stabilizer relations always give the left ideal

\[
 L_H=\Lambda\langle h-1:h\in H\rangle\subseteq K_z.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The inclusion can be strict: distinct orbit vectors may satisfy linear
relations which do not come from equality of orbit points.  Therefore the
group stabilizer alone must not be substituted for the full annihilator.

### Proposition 4.1 (PERMUTATION-ORBIT CASE)

If

\[
 \boxed{K_z=L_H,}
\tag{4.3}
\]

equivalently if the cyclic module \(\Lambda z\) is the transitive
permutation module with no additional linear orbit relations, then the
stabilizer-norm test (3.2) is equivalent to the full v111 annihilator test.

#### Proof

For \(a\in A\), the condition \(L_Ha=0\) is equivalent to
\((h-1)a=0\) for every \(h\in H\), hence to \(a\in A^H\).  Under (4.3), this
is precisely \(K_za=0\).  Apply v111 Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 3.1 above.
\(\square\)

The equality (4.3) is itself a finite rank statement.  It must be checked by
comparing the kernel of the literal orbit matrix

\[
 \Lambda\longrightarrow Z,
 \qquad \lambda\longmapsto\lambda z,
\tag{4.4}
\]

with the row space generated by (4.2).  Comparing only the set-theoretic
orbit size is insufficient.

## 5. The fully invariant extreme

Suppose \(z\neq0\) and \(P\) fixes \(z\).  Then \(\Lambda z\) is the
one-dimensional trivial module and

\[
 K_z=I(P)=\ker(\varepsilon:\Lambda\to k).
\tag{5.1}
\]

There is only one left coset of \(P\), so Theorem 3.1 becomes:

### Corollary 5.1 (FULL-NORM TEST)

With

\[
 N_P=\sum_{g\in P}g,
\tag{5.2}
\]

the actual cyclic splitter exists if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 z\in\operatorname{span}_k
 \{B(N_Pe_r):r\in\mathcal R\}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus a full \(|P|\)-element orbit is compressed to one norm column per
registered normal generator.  This is the modular replacement for invalid
averaging: \(N_P\) is not divided by \(|P|\).

The distinction is load-bearing.  For nontrivial \(P\),

\[
 N_P^2=|P|N_P=0\quad\text{in }k[P],
\tag{5.4}
\]

so \(N_P\) is a socle operator, not a Reynolds idempotent.  A bare preimage
of an invariant \(z\) need not have any invariant replacement.  Failure of
(5.3) is exactly that three-primary failure.

## 6. Executable consequence for the R07 lane

stack receipt should, before constructing a huge unconstrained solve:

1. compute the literal orbit matrix of the stacked g760 defect modulo the

2. determine \(H=\operatorname{Stab}_P(z)\);

4. use the norm-column compression (3.2) for the stabilizer part; and
5. append any remaining equations from a basis of \(K_z/L_H\).

\[

\tag{6.1}
