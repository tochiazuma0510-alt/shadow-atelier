# R07 same-successor endpoint-repair torsor v195

Author: Sol / 2026-08-28

Status: paper theorem and the exact successor to v194 when the first
word-pair representative has a nonzero combined endpoint.  All finite
source-algebra representatives of the same first multiplier form one affine
torsor, and endpoint repair is one linear image-membership problem on its
direction ideal.  A complete marked presentation of the first successor

## 1. The fibre of a first-successor multiplier

Put \(k=\mathbf F_3\).  Let

\[
 \mathcal G\xrightarrow{\psi_1}\Delta_1
 \xrightarrow{\pi_{10}}\Delta_0
\tag{1.1}
\]

be the common word-bearing source and the correctly typed first diagonal
successor and roof.  Write

\[
 \Phi_1:k[\mathcal G]\longrightarrow k[\Delta_1],
 \qquad
 J_{\mathrm{src},1}=\ker\Phi_1.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Suppose v188/v191 have returned

\[
 \mu_1\in\ker(k[\Delta_1]\to k[\Delta_0])
\tag{1.3}
\]

and one finite-support word-pair lift

\[
 M_0\in k[\mathcal G],\qquad \Phi_1(M_0)=\mu_1.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (THE REPRESENTATIVE FIBRE IS AN AFFINE TORSOR)

The complete set of finite-support source-algebra representatives of
\(\mu_1\) is

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal L(\mu_1)=M_0+J_{\mathrm{src},1}.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Every element of this fibre maps to zero at the roof.

#### Proof

Two representatives have the same image under \(\Phi_1\) exactly when
their difference belongs to its kernel.  Conversely addition of a kernel
element preserves the image.  The roof map factors through \(\Delta_1\),
and (1.3) gives the last assertion. \(\square\)

Thus `same-first-shadow representative variation` is not an informal word
choice.  It means translation by the precisely named ideal
\(J_{\mathrm{src},1}\).

## 2. Finite pair and relator normal forms for the direction ideal

Let \(H_1=\ker\psi_1\).

### Lemma 2.1 (SUCCESSOR-FIBRE DIFFERENCE FORM)

An element \(N\in k[\mathcal G]\) belongs to
\(J_{\mathrm{src},1}\) if and only if it has a finite expression

\[
 \boxed{
 N=\sum_i a_i(U_i-V_i),\qquad
 \psi_1(U_i)=\psi_1(V_i).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Collect the finite support of \(N\) by fibres of \(\psi_1\).  Its image is
zero exactly when the coefficient sum in every fibre is zero.  Choosing one
supported base element in each fibre gives (2.1), exactly as in v191
Lemma 1.1.  The converse is immediate. \(\square\)

Assume now that literal words

\[
 S_1=\{s_1,\ldots,s_m\}\subseteq H_1
\tag{2.2}
\]

normally generate \(H_1\) in \(\mathcal G\).  This is the word-bearing
kernel condition supplied by a complete marked presentation of
\(\Delta_1\); a list of words which merely evaluates to one is not enough.

### Proposition 2.2 (FINITE NORMAL-RELATOR GENERATION)

Under (2.2),

\[
 \boxed{
 J_{\mathrm{src},1}
 =\sum_{j=1}^m k[\mathcal G](s_j-1)k[\mathcal G].}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently every direction has a finite expression

\[
 \boxed{
 N=\sum_{\ell}c_\ell A_\ell(s_{j_\ell}-1)B_\ell
   =\sum_\ell c_\ell
    (A_\ell s_{j_\ell}B_\ell-A_\ell B_\ell),}
\tag{2.4}
\]

after expanding the finite group-algebra coefficients into group elements.
Each displayed pair has the same value in \(\Delta_1\).

#### Proof

For any epimorphism of groups, the kernel of the induced group-algebra map
is the two-sided ideal generated by \(h-1\), \(h\in H_1\).  Indeed (2.1)
rewrites as

\[
 U-V=V(V^{-1}U-1),\qquad V^{-1}U\in H_1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The identities

\[
 ab-1=(a-1)+a(b-1),\qquad
 a^{-1}-1=-a^{-1}(a-1)
\tag{2.6}
\]

reduce products and inverses in \(H_1\) to its generators.  Conjugation
introduces left and right coefficients, so normal generation by the
\(s_j\) gives precisely the two-sided ideal in (2.3).  Expanding its finite
coefficients gives (2.4). \(\square\)

This is the compressed interface.  It uses a complete successor
presentation, not a Schreier list of every state of \(\Delta_1\).

## 3. The affine endpoint equation

Use the eleven-occurrence, three-combined-block construction of v194.  For
\(N\in k[\mathcal G]\), define the linear endpoint-change map

\[
 \mathcal E_d(N)=
 \bigl(
  D_{1,H1}(N\star d)_{H1},
  D_{1,H2}(N\star d)_{H2},
  D_{1,P}(N\star d)_P
 \bigr).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The star in (3.1) means that \(N\) is evaluated separately through all ten
typed acting coordinates, reinserted in the eleven literal positions, and
then combined in H1/H2/P printed order.  There is no assumption that one
element of one PB group algebra acts on an already combined row.

Let

\[
 \eta(M)=
 (\eta_{H1}(M),\eta_{H2}(M),\eta_P(M))
\tag{3.2}
\]

be v194's combined endpoint.

### Theorem 3.1 (SAME-SUCCESSOR ENDPOINT-REPAIR CRITERION)

For every \(N\in J_{\mathrm{src},1}\),

\[
 \boxed{\eta(M_0+N)=\eta(M_0)-\mathcal E_d(N).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence there is a finite-support representative of the same \(\mu_1\) which
passes the universal boundary gate if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 \eta(M_0)\in\mathcal E_d(J_{\mathrm{src},1}).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If \(N\) is a preimage in (3.4), then

\[
 M=M_0+N
\tag{3.5}
\]

maps to \(\mu_1\), has zero three-block endpoint, and therefore has finite
boundary chains \(q_{H1},q_{H2},q_P\) by v194 Theorem 3.2.

#### Proof

The occurrence substitutions, group-algebra action, signed prefix
transports, printed-order sums, and Fox endpoint maps are all \(k\)-linear.
Expanding the definition

\[
 \eta(M)=D_1e-D_1(M\star d)
\]

therefore gives (3.3).  Lemma 1.1 says that (3.5), with
\(N\in J_{\mathrm{src},1}\), ranges through every representative of the
same first-successor multiplier.  Thus endpoint zero is equivalent to
\(\mathcal E_d(N)=\eta(M_0)\), proving (3.4).  The last assertion is v194.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (FINITE-RELATOR ORBIT FORM OF THE REPAIR SPACE)

With the complete normal relators (2.2), the right side of (3.4) is the
finite linear span of terms of the form

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal E_d\bigl(A(s_j-1)B\bigr),
 \qquad A,B\in\mathcal G,\quad 1\le j\le m,}
\tag{3.6}
\]

where `finite linear span` means that each candidate uses finitely many
such terms; the orbit indexed by \(A,B\) need not itself be finite.

#### Proof

Apply the linear map \(\mathcal E_d\) to (2.3)--(2.4). \(\square\)

The qualifier in Corollary 3.2 prevents a false finite-state claim.  PB3
and PB4 are infinite, so a bounded enumeration of \(A,B\) is only a search
universe unless an additional closure theorem is proved.

## 4. Positive and negative meanings

A positive finite expression (2.4) with

\[
 \mathcal E_d(N)=\eta(M_0)
\tag{4.1}
\]

is an exact repair.  It gives the corrected word-pair polynomial by
collecting

\[
 M_0+\sum_\ell c_\ell
 (A_\ell s_{j_\ell}B_\ell-A_\ell B_\ell)
\tag{4.2}
\]

and then v194 extracts the boundary chains.  No new first-successor solve is
needed because every added pair is zero in \(k[\Delta_1]\).

A bounded search which finds no (4.1) is `UNKNOWN`.  A genuine negative
result requires a linear functional on the three PB group algebras which:

1. pairs nontrivially with \(\eta(M_0)\); and
2. annihilates the complete two-sided orbit (3.6), not a sampled word ball.

Such a negative result excludes finite-support universal representatives of
the named \(\mu_1\).  It does not exclude a different first-successor
multiplier, nor an intrinsically completed infinite-support multiplier in
the inverse limit.
